# OWOS Source-to-Curriculum Sparring Standard

## Purpose

This standard governs the movement from an external source to an OWOS course addition. The source may be an article, LinkedIn post, video, transcript, diagram, report, conversation, or framework. The source is evidence and inspiration. It is not a lesson.

The objective is to preserve what the source author contributed, find Hardeep Anand's original kernel through sparring, ground that kernel in a real utility decision, make it understandable to a non-technical learner, and design a complete learning experience before changing a canonical curriculum file.

## The operating principle

Every source-derived course idea passes through five states:

```text
SOURCE
  -> SPARRING
  -> KERNEL
  -> INTEGRATION PACKAGE
  -> APPROVED BLUEPRINT
  -> LESSON PRODUCTION
```

Skipping a state creates predictable failure. A source copied too quickly becomes derivative. A kernel without a method becomes a slogan. A method without a utility case becomes generic. A diagram without explanation becomes decoration. Lesson production without an approved blueprint creates pages that look finished but do not form a coherent course.

## State 1: Preserve the source

Preserve the complete source or the complete material supplied by Hardeep. Record the author, title, publication date, link, capture date, visibility, permission state, and any missing provenance. Do not invent missing metadata. Keep the source author's language distinguishable from Hardeep's language.

Create or update the source register and claims register. At this stage, a claim is a lead, not an approved fact. Current product, vendor, regulatory, standards, market, and technology claims require independent verification before they enter learner-facing instruction.

## State 2: Spar before drafting

Sparring is not a request for polished prose. It is the process for discovering what Hardeep sees that the source did not yet say.

During sparring, answer these questions in full:

1. What is the source author's central idea?
2. Which part is useful to Hardeep's body of work?
3. What changes when the idea enters a utility?
4. Which real asset, role, record, event, or decision makes the idea concrete?
5. What tension exists between departments, systems, or perspectives?
6. What does Hardeep add that is original?
7. What should a non-technical learner be able to explain afterward?
8. What should a practitioner be able to do afterward?
9. Where does the idea connect backward and forward in the existing course?
10. What claims require research, qualification, or permission?

Do not end sparring with a collection of attractive phrases. End it with one complete teaching proposition.

## State 3: Lock the kernel

The kernel is the smallest complete statement that carries the original teaching value. It contains a problem, a distinction, a method, and a consequence.

A useful kernel answers:

```text
The problem is...
People usually see it as...
The missing distinction is...
The method is...
The utility consequence is...
```

Hardeep must recognize the kernel as his contribution before the work advances. Attribution remains visible when the kernel was stimulated by another author.

## State 4: Build the integration package

The integration package is the review document prepared before the canonical curriculum blueprint changes. It must be detailed enough that another competent course builder can understand the intended lesson without relying on the chat transcript.

### Required contents

The package includes the following sections:

- source record and evidence boundary;
- source idea and attribution;
- Hardeep's original reframe;
- the complete kernel;
- the primary utility narrative;
- a non-technical entry example;
- a cross-sector transfer example;
- the named method, process, or framework;
- a full explanation of each method step;
- backward and forward course connections;
- learning objectives and role-specific meaning;
- misconceptions and category errors;
- visual and interaction architecture;
- assessment and feedback plan;
- professional work product;
- Knowledge Graph concepts and relationships;
- source, claim, permission, and research needs;
- approval decisions still required.

### Depth rule

Bullets may organize information, but they cannot carry the entire explanation. Every major idea requires connected prose that explains why it matters and how it works. A learner should not have to infer the relationship between two bullets.

Every technical term follows this order:

```text
familiar situation
  -> plain-language distinction
  -> utility example
  -> technical term
  -> learner action
```

### Method rule

A named method must be usable. For every step, explain:

- the question being answered;
- the action the learner takes;
- the people and records involved;
- the evidence created;
- the common failure;
- the review or approval owner;
- how the step connects to the next step.

### Record-and-reality rule

When official systems and lived experience describe the same operation, do not force them into one flattened statement. Preserve their type, source, author, time, confidence, and status.

The official record may be authoritative for one question while the field observation is more current for another. A closed work order and an operator's observation that the vibration continues can both be true. The lesson must teach the learner how to connect these statements, investigate the difference, and assign a responsible human to resolve it.

### Transfer rule

The utility remains the center. Use one ordinary example to open the door for a non-technical learner and one cross-sector example to prove transfer. Do not parade several shallow analogies.

The transfer example must preserve the structure of the method:

```text
official record
+ lived knowledge
+ shared identity
+ relationships
+ provenance
+ accountable decision
```

## Visual teaching contract

Every major concept with a visual shape must receive an explanatory visual selected through the Visual Arsenal. The package must state the visual's teaching job, natural shape, learner action, instructor introduction, reading order, learner conclusion, accessible description, mobile transformation, and reduced-motion equivalent.

Use an editorial illustration when a scene, asset, record conflict, or human handoff carries the idea. Use a network when relationships are the subject. Use a process stepper when the learner must see a method unfold. Use a comparison when two perspectives must remain distinct. Use a chart only when quantity or change is being taught.

Steersman-style playful diagrams are allowed and encouraged when they make the mechanism memorable. They still require technical accuracy, accessible meaning, restrained motion, and a stated teaching conclusion. Hand-drawn character does not excuse an unexplained picture.

## Course wiring contract

The package must identify one primary module home. It may identify secondary connections, but it must explain why each connection exists and whether the connection requires a cross-reference, an example, a revised objective, an assessment change, or a later production task.

Backward connections show what prior learning the idea depends on or repairs. Forward connections show what later learning the idea enables. Do not claim that an idea connects to half the course merely because the same words appear elsewhere.

## Approval sequence

1. Hardeep approves the kernel and integration direction.
2. The canonical curriculum blueprint is revised for review.
3. Hardeep approves the blueprint change.
4. The module design brief, course design matrix, work product, assessment, and recording script are revised.
5. Learner-facing Markdown and HTML are produced.
6. Evidence, practitioner, accessibility, browser, and course-quality checks run.
7. Hardeep approves release scope.

Approval at one stage does not silently approve the next.

## Definition of ready for blueprint revision

The integration package is ready only when the source is preserved, the original kernel is explicit, the utility case is credible, the non-technical entry works, the transfer example preserves the method, every method step is explained, course connections are justified, visuals have teaching jobs, assessment produces evidence, the professional work product is specified, and unresolved claims are visible.
