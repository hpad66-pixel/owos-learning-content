Topic sparring and intake

Determine whether the idea can become a useful course.

Codex workspace
Industry example“Preventing Lift Station Failures” begins as an idea about maintenance, telemetry, operator knowledge, and artificial intelligence claims. No module count exists yet.

You bring

  • Topic and point of view
  • Research and notes
  • Learners and examples

Codex produces

  • Intake brief
  • Research plan
  • Unanswered questions

Do not do yet

  • Choose module count
  • Open Author Studio
  • Generate lessons
Prompt 
I want to explore a possible OWOS course about [TOPIC].

My current point of view is [POINT OF VIEW].
The intended learners may be [LEARNERS].
The utility problems and examples are [EXAMPLES].
My research and notes are [MATERIAL].

Spar with me. Challenge the topic, separate fact from my position, identify missing questions, and help me decide whether this should become a course. Do not select a module count or generate lessons.
Exit gateHardeep agrees that the topic is worth formal research and formulation.
01

Research and evidence

Make the evidence, claims, limitations, and permissions visible.

Codex + research folders
Industry exampleCollect maintenance records, alarm histories, pump manuals, power-quality evidence, operator interviews, overflow procedures, and reliability references. An operator statement remains interpretation until its authority and use are reviewed.
Prompt 
Organize and evaluate all research for the proposed course. Preserve every original and checksum. Build the source register, claims register, evidence boundaries, research-coverage map, contradiction log, permissions record, and unresolved-question list. Use primary sources for load-bearing claims. Do not draft the curriculum until evidence coverage and limitations are visible.
Exit gateEvidence is adequate for formulation, or its limitations are explicitly accepted.
02

Course formulation

Define who graduates, what they can do, and what the course will not claim.

Codex workspace
Industry exampleThe graduate can frame one lift-station reliability problem, identify evidence, distinguish symptom from cause, choose controls, assign owners, and recognize when engineering or operational approval is required.
Prompt 
Formulate the course from the research. Define primary and secondary learners, prerequisites, course promise, graduation capability, measurable outcomes, common and practitioner depth, leader decisions, work-product progression, assessment strategy, modality, evidence boundaries, and relationships to other OWOS courses. Do not create modules until the graduation capability is useful, assessable, and supported.
ApprovalHardeep approves the Course Brief and graduation capability.
03

Syllabus and curriculum architecture

Determine first, second, third, and thereafter. Let learning determine length.

Codex workspace
Industry exampleOperational failure meaning may come first, identity and system boundaries second, failure modes and evidence third, followed by telemetry, maintenance decisions, authority, and a bounded reliability pilot. Research may produce another sequence.

Work backward

  • Final capability
  • Immediate prerequisite
  • Foundational prerequisite

Test the sequence

  • Missing prerequisites
  • Overloaded modules
  • Repeated learning jobs

Produce

  • Syllabus
  • Dependency map
  • Design matrix
Prompt 
Design the syllabus and curriculum from the approved graduation capability. Determine what must come first, second, third, and thereafter. Let learning jobs and prerequisites determine the module count. For every module define title, learner question, one learning job, prerequisite, outcome, misconception, transfer task, work product, and adjacency relationship. Reject arbitrary counts, overloaded lessons, and missing prerequisites.
ApprovalHardeep approves the syllabus, sequence, and resulting module count.
04

Course experience architecture

Give the course its own teaching and visual identity.

Codex workspace
Industry exampleThe lift-station course uses a reliability investigation fieldbook, degradation timelines, failure trees, evidence boards, and maintenance decision rooms. It does not borrow the semantic-graph visual grammar from another course.
Prompt 
Create the course experience architecture. Define teaching voice, utility world, recurring cases, visual language, signature mechanisms, interaction rhythm, assessment rhythm, artifact family, accessibility treatments, and prohibited motifs. Compare with existing OWOS courses and prevent common quality standards from creating a common appearance.
ApprovalHardeep accepts the Course Experience Brief and distinctiveness direction.
05

Lesson contracts

Lock the necessary job and boundaries of every approved module.

Structured source
Industry exampleA cavitation lesson contract teaches how to distinguish evidence of cavitation from other causes. It excludes pump redesign authority and does not duplicate the later maintenance-strategy lesson.
Prompt 
Create and validate one lesson contract for every approved module. Lock module identity, curriculum role, learner question, one learning job, prior knowledge, misconception, transfer task, depth, archetype, opening, mental model, signature mechanism, visual jobs, assessment jobs, work product, evidence references, boundary, prerequisites, and preparation relationships. Audit duplicates and weak transitions.
Exit gateEvery approved module has a validated contract.
06

Golden lesson

Prove the hardest capability once before bulk production.

Codex, then review
Industry exampleThe selected lesson may require a live failure-tree simulation, an evidence diagnosis, a maintenance decision, and a professional Reliability Evidence Card. It proves capability, not a reusable page layout.
Prompt 
Select the golden lesson that best tests the course's hardest teaching, visual, interaction, assessment, and transfer requirements. Build its design brief, Visual Arsenal selection, storyboard, structured source, real assets, simulation, assessment, work product, FAQ, evidence boundary, compiled preview, browser evidence, and scored QA report. Present it for approval as a capability benchmark, not a page template.
ApprovalHardeep approves the golden lesson's capability and quality level.

Structured module production

Codex production

Phase 13 prompt

Complete activation prompt 
Activate the complete OWOS Course Production System for the topic and research developed in this conversation.

Treat this as a new governed course unless the repository already contains an approved course folder. Do not assume a module count, syllabus, curriculum sequence, delivery format, visual style, or page structure.

Begin by preserving this conversation and all supplied research. Determine the current phase from repository evidence.

Execute:
1. topic sparring and intake;
2. research and evidence organization;
3. course formulation;
4. syllabus and curriculum architecture;
5. course experience architecture;
6. lesson contracts;
7. golden lesson selection, production, testing, scoring, and approval;
8. structured module production in prerequisite order;
9. Author Studio working-candidate preparation;
10. Phase 13 deterministic compilation, rendered QA, per-graphic responsive QA, and evidence-backed scoring;
11. Phase 14 owner review in Desktop, iPad, iPhone, 200 percent, and 400 percent views;
12. bounded owner-finding remediation followed by affected Phase 13 and Phase 14 reruns;
13. immutable-checksum human review for practice, novice comprehension, factual accuracy, security, accessibility, physical devices, credential boundaries, and course coherence;
14. course assembly and course-specific landing-page reconciliation;
15. Course Quality-Control Dossier;
16. final Git publication, platform intake, production deployment, custom-domain verification, and live browser QA only after all required approvals name the same checksum.

For the curriculum, determine what the learner must understand first, second, third, and thereafter. Let learning jobs and prerequisites determine the module count.

Give every module its own contract, design brief, storyboard, narrative structure, opening, visual reasoning, signature mechanism, simulation strategy, assessment rhythm, professional work product, FAQ, and evidence boundary.

Apply one common quality standard without producing one common appearance. Prevent repeated openings, visual shapes, interaction pairs, quiz sequences, work-product formats, FAQs, and factory prose.

Use structured source. Treat HTML as compiled delivery output. Use Author Studio after curriculum architecture and structured module candidates exist.

Require complete conversational teaching that works without video. Define terms, spell out acronyms, explain every visual and interaction, use concrete utility examples, and preserve source, authority, time, uncertainty, and permission boundaries.

Count only real visual assets and governed executable components. Render and measure every graphic. Text clipping, overlap, escape from boxes, unreadable compression, insufficient padding, broken connectors, contrast failures, and desktop, tablet, phone, zoom, or reduced-motion failures are release blockers.

Select interactions and assessments from the cognitive job. Require immediate feedback, retry, distributed checks, an applied demonstration, and a professional work product.

Complete an evidence-backed Module Quality-Control Report for every module and a Course Quality-Control Dossier for the course. A score cannot override blocked factual, practitioner, accessibility, technical, security, credential, or release gates.

Do not report final publication until owner approval and every required human review name the exact candidate checksum, the source commit is imported, platform tests pass, production deployment completes, custom-domain files match, and live desktop, iPad, iPhone, 200 percent, and 400 percent tests pass.

Before each major phase, report completed work, available evidence, unresolved decisions, required approval, and next-phase output.

Do not ask me to operate internal scripts. Preserve all substantive direction and evidence in the repository.
