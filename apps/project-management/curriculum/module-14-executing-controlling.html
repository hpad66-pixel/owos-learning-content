Chapter 14 · Executing and controlling

Running the work, and keeping it on track

Planning is done; now the job actually happens. This chapter is about the doing: the process groups that organize the whole life of a project, the weekly rhythm that keeps execution honest, and the disciplined way you handle the changes that will absolutely come.

The job in motion

The Millpond Road water main replacement, under way. Same job. Crews are working, money is flowing, and requests to change things are landing on your desk. This chapter is how you keep it all moving to plan and control the changes without losing the baseline.

What you will be able to do

  • Run a weekly rhythm that keeps execution and control tied together

1 / 3

The five process groups

Every project, of any size, runs through five process groups. The trap is thinking they are sequential phases. They overlap: you plan while you execute, and Monitoring and Controlling runs the entire time, wrapping around everything else. Click through them.

2 / 3

The operating rhythm

Execution and control are not separate activities you do at different times; they are one weekly loop. A steady operating rhythm is what keeps a project honest between the big milestones. The method below is the loop that runs every week on a well-managed job.

3 / 3

Integrated change control

Changes are not the enemy; uncontrolled changes are. Integrated change control is the disciplined way to handle a request: nothing gets built, dropped, or repriced until you assess its full impact and someone with authority decides. Walk the flow.

The reason you assess all the baselines is the ripple you saw back in the governance chapter: a change to one thing moves the others. Feel it again, in the moment a change request lands.

Leader's call

Run a rhythm and control your changes. A steady weekly loop keeps small problems small, and a real change-control process keeps the baseline meaning something. The projects that lose control rarely lose it all at once; they lose it one unlogged change and one skipped status at a time.

What to carry out of this chapter

  • 1.
    The process groups overlap. Initiating, planning, executing, monitoring-and-controlling, and closing are not a straight line, and control runs the whole time.
  • 2.
    Run a weekly rhythm. Plan, do, check against the plan, adjust. Small corrections weekly beat big rescues later.
  • 3.
    Control every change. Log it, assess the full impact, decide with the right authority, and update all baselines together.

The leader's bottom line

Execution is not a burst of effort; it is a rhythm and a discipline. Keep the weekly loop honest and put every change through the process, especially the small ones, and the baseline you fought to build in the earlier chapters will still mean something at the end.
