# Course Experience Brief: Utility Project Delivery

Status: current strength preserved, retrofit controls added

## Course identity

| Decision | Course-specific answer |
| --- | --- |
| Central argument | Utility projects succeed when decisions, scope, schedule, money, risk, contracts, people, safety, and operating outcomes stay connected. |
| Learner transformation | Move from knowing project-management terms to making defensible delivery decisions under utility constraints. |
| Conversational teaching voice | An experienced utility program director reviewing real work with a project manager, superintendent, engineer, operator, finance lead, and executive sponsor. |
| Utility world | Lift stations, pipelines, treatment assets, capital programs, permits, contractors, rate impacts, commissioning, claims, field changes, and operating handover. |
| Visual language | Field notebook, construction plan set, schedule wall, commercial review desk, risk room, and executive control board. |
| Signature learning mechanisms | Scope boundary, delivery-method decision, critical-path engine, schedule compression, estimate range, earned-value forecast, claim anatomy, quality-cost tradeoff, Monte Carlo range, stakeholder strategy, negotiation zone, control-room status, commissioning gate, lifecycle value, portfolio balance, and capstone decision gates. |
| Professional artifact family | A progressively assembled utility project-delivery kit. |
| Prohibited motifs | Replacing existing simulations with generic cards, forcing graph visuals into schedule or cost lessons, copying the data-governance evidence-desk layout, and flattening the capstone into a quiz page. |

## Written-first teaching contract

- Preserve the course's practical, worked-example strength.
- Expand any section that currently assumes narration or prior project-management knowledge.
- Explain calculations line by line before asking learners to operate them.
- Keep contract, safety, regulatory, and professional boundaries explicit.
- Use graphics from the natural project artifact: plan, schedule, curve, map, register, markup, or dashboard.

## Retrofit priorities

1. Preserve the current 21-lesson variety and Cypress Creek capstone.
2. Add a formal course-level experience manifest and distinctiveness gate.
3. Strengthen source, competency, completion, and final-assessment evidence.
4. Add read-without-video novice reviews to calculation-heavy and contract-heavy lessons.
5. Do not visually redesign this course to resemble Meaning Before Models or Data Before AI.
