Chapter 10 · Quality and risk

Quality: building it right, not gold-plating it

Quality is not making the fanciest thing. It is delivering exactly what the requirements called for, provably. This chapter covers how you build quality in (assurance), how you check it (control), where to spend to get it, and what to do when something does not conform.

Assurance, control, and the plan

Two words that sound alike and mean different things. Quality Assurance (QA) is about the process: the procedures, training, and approved materials that prevent defects. Quality Control (QC) is about the product: the inspections and tests that catch defects. Assurance prevents, control detects. Sort a few.

QC runs on an Inspection and Test Plan (ITP), which lists every check on the job. Two kinds of checkpoint matter most. A hold point stops the work until an inspector signs off. A witness point invites the inspector but lets work continue. Walk the Millpond ITP.

How much should you spend on all this checking? There is a real answer, and it is not "as much as possible." The cost of quality is prevention and appraisal spend plus the cost of the failures that slip through. Spend too little and failures are expensive; spend too much and you over-inspect. There is a sweet spot. Find it.

When something does not meet spec, it gets logged as a Non-Conformance Report (NCR). The NCR is not a punishment, it is a controlled way to decide what to do (rework it, repair it, use it as-is with engineering approval, or reject it) and to prove you handled it. A job with zero NCRs is usually a job that is not inspecting hard enough.

Leader's call

Put your quality money at the front, in prevention and hold points, not at the end in rework. A joint inspected before backfill costs minutes; the same joint found after a road is repaved costs a project. And do not read zero NCRs as good news, read it as nobody looking.

What to carry out of this chapter

  • 1.
    Quality is conformance, not gold-plating. Deliver exactly what the requirements asked for, and prove it.
  • 2.
    Assurance prevents, control detects. Build quality in with process; catch what slips through with inspection and test.
  • 3.
    Put hold points where defects get buried. The ITP checkpoint before backfill is worth a hundred after handover.
  • 4.
    There is a sweet spot. Spend enough on prevention to keep failures low, and treat every NCR as information.

The leader's bottom line

Quality is cheapest when it is prevented and most expensive when it is buried. Spend at the front, inspect at the points of no return, and log every non-conformance honestly. That is the whole discipline, and it pays for itself many times over.
