How you deliver a project

Two questions decide how a project runs. First, how much do you plan up front versus figure out as you go? Second, who designs it and who builds it, and how do you contract that? Get these right and everything downstream is easier.

Section 1 of 2

Plan it all up front, or figure it out as you go?

Every project sits somewhere between two styles. A predictive approach means you plan the whole thing in detail first, then build it in order. It works when you have done the work before and know what to expect, like replacing a standard water main. An agile approach means you do a small piece, learn from it, adjust, and repeat. It works when there is a lot you cannot know yet, like a new smart-meter rollout where you are still learning what the software and the customers will do.

Most real utility work is a hybrid: run the construction predictively, because pipe in the ground is well understood, but run the new technology or the public-outreach parts in short cycles, because those you have to learn as you go.

The simple rule: the more you already know about the work, the more you can plan up front. The more is unknown, the more you should work in small steps so a surprise costs you a week, not the whole budget.

Ask how much of the scope is genuinely new. If it is a repeat of past jobs, plan it fully and control to the plan. If a big chunk is unproven, carve that chunk out and run it in short cycles with checkpoints, while the rest stays predictive.

Forcing an agile, learn-as-you-go effort into a fixed lump-sum plan is how technology projects blow up. Forcing a well-understood pipe job into endless "iterations" just wastes time. Match the style to the certainty, and say which parts are which when you fund it.

The two styles even look different. Predictive is a straight line through phases. Agile is a loop you go around again and again.

Section 2 of 2

Who designs it, who builds it: delivery methods

Once you know the style, you decide how to contract the work. Here are the five you will meet most in water, in plain terms.

Design-Bid-Build (DBB): you finish the design, put it out to bid, and the low bidder builds it. Traditional. You keep full control of the design, and the low bid keeps the price honest. The downside is that the designer and the builder are not on the same team, so when the field does not match the drawings, you get change orders.

Design-Build (DB): one company both designs and builds. It is faster and there is a single point of responsibility. The trade-off is you give up some control over the design details.

Construction Manager at Risk (CMAR): you bring the builder in early, during design, to advise on cost and buildability, then they commit to a guaranteed maximum price. You get teamwork and price certainty while keeping your own designer.

Progressive Design-Build (Progressive DB): design-build, but you pick the team on qualifications first, then develop the design and the price together, step by step. Good when the scope is still fuzzy.

Public-Private Partnership (P3): a private partner finances, builds, and often operates the asset for years, and you pay over time. You use it for very large projects when you do not have the capital up front.

Choosing well

How to pick a delivery methodMethod
1Name what matters most on this job: lowest price, speed, price certainty, design control, or no capital up front.
2Check how well you know the scope. Well understood points to DBB. Still fuzzy points to Progressive DB.
3Check your own capacity. No capital points to P3. Want a partner during design points to CMAR.
4Confirm your state and rules allow it. Not every method is permitted for every public agency; check first.

What to remember

Predictive means plan it all up front. Agile means small steps, learn, repeat. Most real work is a hybrid.

The more you already know, the more you can plan; the more is unknown, the smaller your steps should be.

Delivery methods trade off control, speed, and price certainty. DBB gives control and low bids; DB gives speed and one point of responsibility; CMAR and Progressive DB give teamwork and price certainty; P3 covers a huge job when you lack the capital.

Pick the method by what the project cares about most, how well you know the scope, and what your rules allow.
