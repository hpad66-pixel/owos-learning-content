# Module Design Brief: Module 19 Program and Portfolio Management

## Identity

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |

| Working title | Program and portfolio management |
| Learner roles | Capital planner, program manager, finance lead, resource owner, executive, board |
| Competencies | Separate project, program, and portfolio work; score transparently; test capacity; recommend sequence |

## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Consequential situation | More worthwhile projects exist than the utility can fund or deliver. |
| Initial decision | What to fund, sequence, defer, reshape, or stop. |
| Professional consequence | An unaffordable or undeliverable plan hides delay, staff overload, and unrealized benefits. |
| Post-lesson capability | Connect objectives, obligations, criteria, dependencies, funding, capacity, and review triggers. |
| Work product | Portfolio recommendation. |
| Completion evidence | Opening, portfolio gate, recommendation, final check. |

- Approximate teaching words: More than 2,700.
- Worked example: Pressure-zone program and annual capital budget.
- Misconception: Highest score always wins, or leveling creates capacity.
- Boundary: Instructional scores do not represent a live plan.
- Debriefs: Present.
- Without media: Complete teaching, recommendation, FAQ, and boundary.

## Learner FAQ plan

| Question | Answer | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project, program, portfolio? | Deliverable, shared benefit, and organizational choice. | Method guidance |
| Highest score first? | Not automatically; obligations and constraints matter. | Policy governs |
| Leveling adds staff? | No, it moves work. | Capacity evidence governs |
| Why publish weights? | They reveal the policy choice before scores. | Governance governs |
| Stop versus defer? | Stop when case fails; defer when case remains but timing does not. | Authority governs |
