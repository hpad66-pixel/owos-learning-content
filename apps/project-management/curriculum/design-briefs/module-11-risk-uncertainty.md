# Module Design Brief: Module 11 Risk and Uncertainty

## Identity

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Course and module ID | `owos-master-project-delivery-001` / `pm-11-risk-uncertainty` |
| Working title | Risk: managing what might happen |
| Learner roles | Utility project manager, risk owner, estimator, scheduler, sponsor, operator |
| Competencies | Separate risks from issues, rank exposure, calculate Expected Monetary Value, select a response, document residual risk |
| Controlled sources | Project Management Institute standards; approved project risk plan; current estimate, schedule, contract, and field evidence |
| Evidence boundary | Millpond is instructional. Actual exposure, authority, reserve, and response decisions depend on the current controlled project record. |

## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What consequential situation opens the lesson? | Millpond has uncertain utility conflicts, long-lead equipment, weather, and cost exposure before work proceeds. |
| What must the learner decide before teaching begins? | Which uncertainties require action, transfer, reserve, monitoring, or acceptance. |
| What professional consequence makes this matter? | Unmanaged exposure can become delay, cost growth, unsafe work, dispute, or service consequence. |
| What should the learner be able to do afterward? | Connect an uncertain event to evidence, exposure, authority, response, trigger, and residual risk. |
| What usable work product will the learner create? | Risk decision record. |
| What evidence is required for completion? | Opening situation, risk-response gate, completed risk record, and final record check. |

## Written-first review

- Approximate conversational teaching words: More than 2,000.
- Worked utility example and marker: Millpond utility-conflict Expected Monetary Value calculation.
- Misconception addressed: A risk is the same as an issue, or transfer removes the event.
- Boundary or non-example: The instructional dollar example is not a forecast for a live project.
- Component debriefs: Visible after the matrix and in the response-record teaching.
- What remains if every video and animation is removed: Complete definitions, calculation, comparison, work product, feedback, FAQ, and evidence boundary.

## Visual pacing review

- Longest run of consecutive full prose blocks: Two.
- Visual, interaction, worked example, or callout used to break each dense section: Matrix, Expected Monetary Value estimate, response comparison, role callout, and record builder.
- Any uninterrupted prose exception and reason: None.
- Editorial illustration reading guide and learner conclusion, when used: Not applicable.
- Dark-surface contrast plan: Shared retrofit drawer header and existing leader callouts use explicit light text.

## Learner FAQ plan

| Likely learner question | Why it may remain unclear | Direct plain-English answer | Utility example | Diagram, comparison, or worked sequence | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When is a risk an issue? | Teams mix future and current conditions | A risk is uncertain; an issue has occurred | Missed valve ship date | Risk-to-issue sequence | Project process governs |
| Does high score mean stop? | Priority is confused with decision | It signals attention, not an automatic stop | Trench conflict can be mitigated | Matrix | Authority governs |
| Why use Expected Monetary Value? | The average is not a literal outcome | It supports comparison across exposure | $60,000 risk-weighted conflict | Worked calculation | Not a forecast |
| Which reserve covers surprise? | Reserve categories overlap in speech | Contingency covers identified uncertainty; management reserve addresses true surprise | Unknown field condition | Comparison | Utility controls govern |
| Who owns the risk? | Ownership is confused with spending authority | The owner watches and responds; authority may sit elsewhere | Project manager versus sponsor | Record sequence | Delegation governs |
