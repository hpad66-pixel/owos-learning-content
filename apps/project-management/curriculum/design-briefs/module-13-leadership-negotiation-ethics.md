| Field | Decision |

## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What consequential situation opens the lesson? | Millpond requires crew leadership, a change-order negotiation, conflict handling, and an ethical decision. |
| What must the learner decide before teaching begins? | How to enter the conversation without exceeding authority or trading away trust. |
| What professional consequence makes this matter? | A poor conversation can damage safety, cost, schedule, relationship, and public trust. |
| What should the learner be able to do afterward? | Prepare facts, interests, alternatives, authority, boundaries, and a recorded next action. |
| What usable work product will the learner create? | Difficult-conversation brief. |
| What evidence is required for completion? | Opening situation, conversation gate, brief, and final applied check. |

## Written-first review

- Approximate conversational teaching words: More than 1,900.
- Worked utility example and marker: Millpond change-order price and public-project ethics.
- Misconception addressed: Every conflict should be collaborative, or negotiation success excuses an ethical breach.
- Boundary or non-example: The course does not interpret a live contract or personnel matter.
- Component debriefs: Present after simulations and in the brief review.
- What remains if every video and animation is removed: Complete teaching, examples, comparison, artifact, FAQ, and evidence boundary.

## Visual pacing review

- Longest run of consecutive full prose blocks: Two.
- Visual, interaction, worked example, or callout used to break each dense section: Leadership slider, negotiation simulation, conflict map, ethics comparison, gate, and brief.
- Any uninterrupted prose exception and reason: None.
- Editorial illustration reading guide and learner conclusion, when used: Not applicable.
- Dark-surface contrast plan: Existing callouts and shared drawer header use explicit light text.
