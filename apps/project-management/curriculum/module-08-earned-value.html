Earned value: are you really on track?

You have spent half the budget. Are you halfway done, or in trouble? Percent spent cannot tell you. Earned Value Management can, by comparing three numbers, and it is far easier to see than to read about. So we will build the picture and let you drag it.

The job we are tracking

The Millpond Road water main replacement, mid-construction. A $1.3M job. We are partway through, some money is spent, some pipe is in the ground. This chapter works out whether we are actually on track, and where we will end up.

What you will be able to do

  • Read the CPI and SPI indices and say instantly whether a project is healthy
  • Forecast the final cost (EAC) from today's performance

Three numbers, not one

Earned Value Management (EVM) works because it stops looking at money alone and compares three things. Planned Value (PV) is what you planned to have accomplished by now. Earned Value (EV) is the budgeted worth of the work actually done. Actual Cost (AC) is the cash out the door. Sort a few.

The magic is in comparing them. Put all three on one chart, mark where the project is today, and the whole health of the job jumps out. Drag the sliders and watch it.

Two indices turn that picture into a scorecard. The Cost Performance Index (CPI) is EV divided by AC: above 1 is under budget, below 1 is over. The Schedule Performance Index (SPI) is EV divided by PV: above 1 is ahead, below 1 is behind. One glance at the two numbers tells you where a project stands.

2 / 2

Forecasting the finish

The best part of earned value is that it predicts. If a job is running at a CPI of 0.87 now, it will very likely keep running that way, so the final cost will overshoot the budget. That forecast is the Estimate at Completion (EAC), and the simplest, most-used formula is Budget at Completion (BAC) divided by CPI.

A few cousins finish the toolkit. The Estimate to Complete (ETC) is what is left to spend from here. The Variance at Completion (VAC) is how far off budget you will land. And the To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI) tells you the efficiency you would need on the rest of the work to still land on budget, which is your reality check.

Leader's call

Ban "percent of budget spent" from your status reports. It hides trouble. Report EV, CPI, SPI, and the EAC forecast. Those four numbers tell a board exactly where the project stands and where it will land, early enough to actually do something about it.

What to carry out of this chapter

  • 1.
    Compare three numbers, not one. Planned Value (where you should be), Earned Value (what is done), Actual Cost (what you spent).
  • 2.
    Two indices diagnose the job. CPI = EV / AC for cost, SPI = EV / PV for schedule. Above 1 good, below 1 trouble.
  • 3.
    Earned value forecasts. EAC = BAC / CPI tells you where the job will finish, long before it does.
  • 4.
    Report earned value, not spending. Percent spent hides the truth; EV, CPI, SPI, and EAC reveal it early.

The leader's bottom line

Earned value is an early-warning system. A CPI drifting below 1 in month two tells you the budget is in trouble while you can still fix it. Watch CPI and SPI every reporting period, and act the moment they slip, not the month the money runs out.
