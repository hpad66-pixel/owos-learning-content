# One Water AI Executive Fellowship

## Master syllabus

**Program length:** 24 weeks

**Learning commitment:** 5 to 6 hours per week

**Total guided effort:** approximately 132 hours

**Curriculum:** 8 courses, 64 modules, 8 live executive forums, 16 applied studios, and one capstone defense

**Cohort size:** 25 participants

**Program tuition target:** $10,000 per participant
**Status:** master curriculum candidate for Hardeep Anand review

## The value proposition

The water sector does not need another general course that explains what artificial intelligence is.
It needs leaders who can tell the difference between a demonstration and a defensible utility
application. It needs operators, engineers, managers, data leaders, procurement teams, consultants,
and executives who can work from the same language, test the same evidence, and identify the person
who remains accountable.

The One Water AI Executive Fellowship is a six-month applied program for professionals responsible
for water, wastewater, and stormwater decisions. Participants learn the technology in plain English,
apply it to utility work, build with current tools, test failure and governance controls, and leave
with an implementation portfolio for one real organizational opportunity.

This is not a collection of videos. It is a working program. Every course produces part of the
participant's One Water AI Fieldbook. Every studio converts an idea into a decision, diagram,
control, or work product. Every claim must carry its source and limitation. Every proposed use case
must name its data, knowledge, risk, owner, measurement, stop condition, and next action.

## Why this program earns a premium position

Participants do not leave with awareness alone. They leave able to:

1. explain machine learning, generative AI, retrieval, knowledge graphs, skills, tools, agents, and
   agentic systems without hiding behind jargon;
2. distinguish chat, retrieval, deterministic workflow, single-agent, and multi-agent approaches;
3. diagnose whether a utility problem is ready for AI or still requires data, process, knowledge,
   governance, or organizational repair;
4. build and test prompts, reusable Claude Skills, source-grounded retrieval patterns, and bounded
   agent workflows;
5. evaluate output quality, provenance, permissions, failure modes, cost, and human-review effort;
6. identify valuable applications across operations, maintenance, water quality, customer service,
   capital delivery, regulation, finance, and administration;
7. create an AI governance and procurement record suitable for organizational review;
8. compare value, feasibility, consequence, adoption cost, and implementation risk;
9. lead cross-functional discussion between utility practice, data, technology, legal, security,
   procurement, and executive leadership; and
10. defend a governed 90-day pilot and a one-year organizational roadmap.

## Intended participants

The fellowship is designed for professionals who influence consequential decisions:

- general managers, executive directors, and deputy directors;
- operations, maintenance, engineering, water-quality, stormwater, and wastewater leaders;
- capital-program, asset-management, customer-service, finance, and communications leaders;
- chief information, technology, data, cybersecurity, innovation, and transformation officers;
- legal, privacy, procurement, risk, audit, and governance professionals;
- senior consultants, solution providers, researchers, regulators, association leaders, and
  nonprofit executives serving the water sector; and
- selected emerging leaders with the authority and organizational support to complete an applied
  capstone.

Participants do not need to program. They do need a real work context, professional judgment, and
the willingness to test an idea against evidence.

## Program architecture

Each course runs for three weeks. Most weeks contain two or three concise modules, one applied studio,
and one fieldwork assignment. Participants use Articulate learning experiences for guided study,
the OWOS knowledge graph for source and relationship exploration, and live cohort sessions for
discussion, critique, and application.

| Course | Weeks | Central question | Portfolio outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. AI Fluency for One Water | 1 to 3 | What is this technology, and what changes in utility work? | AI terms and decision field card |
| 2. Data, Knowledge, and Trusted Context | 4 to 6 | What must be connected and governed before an answer is trusted? | Data and knowledge readiness map |
| 3. Agents, Skills, and Orchestration | 7 to 9 | How does AI move from answering to doing bounded work? | Utility Agent Canvas and Claude Skill |
| 4. Utility Applications and Opportunity Design | 10 to 12 | Where can AI create real value across One Water? | Prioritized utility opportunity portfolio |
| 5. Governance, Security, and Human Authority | 13 to 15 | What controls make an application defensible? | AI governance and control record |
| 6. Strategy, Economics, and Organizational Change | 16 to 18 | How does a utility fund, organize, adopt, and scale useful AI? | Investment case and operating model |
| 7. Applied AI Studios | 19 to 21 | Can the participant build, test, break, and improve a bounded system? | Tested prototype evidence package |
| 8. Capstone: From Opportunity to Governed Pilot | 22 to 24 | Can the participant defend a useful, bounded, measurable pilot? | 90-day pilot brief and one-year roadmap |

## Capstone completion standard

The capstone must identify:

1. the utility problem, current process, and baseline;
2. intended outcome, affected people, and measurable value;
3. accountable sponsor, owner, reviewers, users, and decision rights;
4. approved sources, data limitations, definitions, and provenance;
5. selected architecture and reason simpler alternatives were rejected;
6. tools, connections, identity, permissions, and records;
7. human checkpoints, prohibited actions, stop conditions, and escalation;
8. test cases, evaluation thresholds, incident response, and continuing assurance;
9. model, infrastructure, integration, review, workforce, and change costs;
10. the 90-day pilot sequence and one-year organizational roadmap; and
11. a candid statement of what remains unknown.

## Learning rhythm

Each module must:

1. begin with a consequential utility situation;
2. ask the learner to decide before the explanation;
3. teach the idea in conversational, plain English;
4. use explanatory graphics and interaction to reveal the mechanism;
5. include source-aware examples, failure, and correction;
6. connect the idea to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader perspectives when roles differ;
7. add one useful record to the One Water AI Fieldbook;
8. include distributed knowledge checks with explanatory feedback;
9. end with a module-specific FAQ, evidence boundary, and next action; and
10. preserve the current module's graph, sources, community, and instructor guidance.

## Completion evidence

Program completion requires:

- completion of required Articulate modules and assessments;
- participation in at least six of eight live executive forums;
- completion of required studios and fieldwork;
- a completed One Water AI Fieldbook;
- a source-backed capstone portfolio;
- a capstone defense and revision; and
- an explicit statement of professional responsibility and next action.

Completion does not authorize engineering, operational, regulatory, financial, procurement, or
security decisions. Any credential remains disabled until its independent evidence, assessment,
identity, and issuance gates are approved.
