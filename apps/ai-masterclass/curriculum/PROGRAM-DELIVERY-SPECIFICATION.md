# One Water AI program delivery specification

## Purpose

This specification turns the curriculum blueprint into a teachable, reviewable, and measurable
program. It governs the learner route, instructional modes, workload, evidence, review, and release
boundaries. It does not authorize public release, a credential, or operational use.

## Program model

The program has one universal core and two optional technical lines:

| Line | Modules | Guided hours | Required for every participant | Entry decision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Executive Fellowship core | 64 | 132 | yes | accepted participant |
| Builder Bridge | 8 | 48 | no | diagnostic, evidence, or instructor placement |
| Advanced Agent Systems | 8 | 48 | no | core agent foundation plus diagnostic, evidence, or instructor placement |

The core builds shared judgment across executives, operators, engineers, finance, administration,
data, governance, consulting, and public leadership. Builder Bridge develops practical construction
and release foundations. Advanced Agent Systems develops deeper technical design and evaluation.

## Learner route

1. **Readiness and role diagnostic:** establish the learner's work context, prior evidence,
   technical comfort, decision authority, accessibility needs, and target work product.
2. **Core learning contract:** name the organizational opportunity, sponsor, evidence boundary,
   protected information, and time commitment.
3. **Eight-course core:** complete guided study, studios, forums, fieldwork, Fieldbook records, and
   four milestone reviews.
4. **Extension decision:** assign Builder Bridge, Advanced Agent Systems, both in sequence, or
   neither. Record the reason and evidence.
5. **Capstone defense:** defend the problem, baseline, sources, architecture, controls, economics,
   adoption plan, evaluation, stop conditions, and remaining unknowns.
6. **Post-program review:** verify the evidence record and make a separate credential decision only
   after identity, assessment, and issuance gates are approved.

## Readiness diagnostic

The diagnostic is not a high-stakes exam. It is an instructional placement instrument. It must
sample the learner's ability to:

- interpret a utility process, record, role, and consequence;
- distinguish a claim from its source and locator;
- read a simple data table and identify missing or conflicting values;
- explain a fixed workflow, retrieval system, and agent in plain English;
- read a short code or request trace when a technical route is being considered;
- identify who may approve, act, stop, correct, and review; and
- state where assistance, accessibility support, or prerequisite study is needed.

The recorded result is one of: core only, core plus Builder Bridge, core plus Advanced Agent
Systems, both extensions in sequence, or instructor review required.

## Constructive alignment rule

Every module is built in this order:

1. define an observable learner decision or performance;
2. name the work product that proves it;
3. define ordinary, boundary, failure, and correction evidence;
4. design the learning experience and feedback needed to produce that evidence;
5. choose the media, interaction, and tool only after the evidence is clear; and
6. verify sources, accessibility, authority, and release status before publishing.

Every objective uses an observable verb. Every assessment maps to at least one objective. Every
major claim maps to an authority and locator. Every consequential action maps to a named person.

## Instructional modes

| Mode | Job | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guided digital lesson | establish language, mechanism, distinctions, and limits | distributed checks and module work product |
| Applied studio | make, test, break, inspect, and correct | trace, artifact, test result, and correction record |
| Executive forum | explain, challenge, compare, and decide across roles | decision note and peer or instructor feedback |
| Fieldwork | apply the method to the participant's organization | source-backed Fieldbook record |
| Review clinic | improve a weak work product against explicit criteria | before-and-after record and reviewer disposition |
| Capstone defense | defend a bounded pilot under challenge | portfolio, oral defense, revision, and final decision |

## Workload model

The 132 guided core hours assume 5 to 6 hours per week for 24 weeks. A typical three-week course
contains:

- 6 to 8 hours of guided digital study;
- 3 to 4 hours of applied studio work;
- 2 to 3 hours of fieldwork and Fieldbook development;
- 1.5 to 2 hours of live forum and review; and
- 1 to 2 hours of revision, assessment, and reflection.

An optional extension contains eight six-hour modules. Each extension module includes guided study,
a technical or analytical exercise, a failure or correction case, and an assessed work product.

## Milestone evidence

| Milestone | Required artifacts | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fluency and evidence | AI decision field card, data and knowledge readiness map, sources and limitations | Does the participant understand the problem and evidence before choosing technology? |
| Agent and opportunity | architecture comparison, agent canvas, role and handoff design, opportunity portfolio | Is the proposed system the smallest useful and governable option? |
| Governance and investment | control record, evaluation plan, lifecycle plan, total-cost model, adoption plan | Can the organization own, test, fund, monitor, correct, and stop it? |
| Prototype and capstone | working trace, failure record, release rehearsal, pilot brief, one-year roadmap | Is there enough evidence to authorize a bounded pilot, revise it, or stop? |

## Assessment system

Assessment uses five levels of evidence:

1. **Recognition:** identify a term, component, role, or failure.
2. **Explanation:** explain the mechanism and limitation in plain English.
3. **Application:** complete a bounded utility work product.
4. **Diagnosis and correction:** identify a weak result, explain why it fails, and improve it.
5. **Defense:** justify a decision under cross-functional challenge and revise the record.

Scores alone are insufficient for milestone passage. The record must preserve the artifact,
criteria, reviewer, feedback, revision, disposition, and date. A weak area is revised or narrowed. It
is not hidden by averaging unrelated scores.

## Tool policy

Tool categories may include a learning platform, authoring system, approved language model,
retrieval system, knowledge graph, repository, diagramming tool, data workspace, test environment,
and deployment sandbox. The curriculum teaches transferable categories and records the tool used,
version or date, source boundary, permission, cost, and observed limitation.

No vendor name substitutes for a learning objective. No demonstration substitutes for a test. No
connection to a system grants permission to act in that system.

## Instructor and reviewer roles

| Role | Accountable work |
| --- | --- |
| Program director | scope, faculty alignment, learner route, exceptions, and release recommendation |
| Domain instructor | One Water accuracy, operational context, examples, and authority boundaries |
| Technical instructor | architecture, code or trace accuracy, testing, security, and production controls |
| Studio coach | learner work products, failure practice, feedback, and revision |
| Evidence reviewer | claims, sources, locators, limitations, and contradiction handling |
| Accessibility reviewer | structure, interaction, keyboard use, alternatives, captions, contrast, and comprehension |
| Capstone panel | cross-functional challenge, disposition, and final evidence record |

One person may hold more than one role, but each review action must identify the role being exercised.

## Portfolio architecture

The One Water AI Fieldbook is the learner's accumulating evidence system. It contains the module
work products, four milestone packages, reviewer feedback, revisions, source register, decision
records, control records, failure evidence, and capstone. The learner may reuse a work product in
their organization only after local authority and protected-information rules are satisfied.

## Accessibility and inclusion standard

Every experience must support keyboard operation, readable structure, sufficient contrast,
captions or transcripts, text alternatives for meaningful visuals, non-color status cues, plain
language definitions, flexible pacing, and a route to request accommodation. Timed work, drag-only
interaction, inaccessible diagrams, and unexplained abbreviations require an equivalent route.

## Production and release gates

A curriculum blueprint is not a released course. Each module must pass:

1. curriculum and constructive-alignment review;
2. source, claim, and locator verification;
3. domain and technical accuracy review;
4. assessment and feedback review;
5. accessibility and visual review;
6. authoring-system build and clean-room test;
7. learning-platform packaging and tracking verification;
8. instructor and support readiness;
9. pilot learner observation and issue closure; and
10. explicit release approval recorded in the repository.

Credential language remains disabled until identity, assessment, evidence, issuance, revocation, and
appeal controls are separately approved.

## Market statement boundary

The reviewed market evidence supports a statement that One Water AI combines elements that are
usually taught separately: One Water practice, data and knowledge governance, applied AI, formal
agent systems, human authority, production controls, organizational adoption, and a governed pilot
portfolio. Marketing must not convert that observation into an unqualified claim that no comparable
program exists anywhere. The comparison set and review date must remain visible.
