# Staff Direction for M10: The Autonomy Slider: Augmentation vs. Automation

## Your assignment

Give learners a practical autonomy and interaction model for deciding how much an artificial intelligence system may suggest, prepare, act, retry, explain, pause, recover, or escalate while a person retains usable control.

Open with this situation: A pilot that began by drafting work-order notes is quietly expanded to create and close work orders without a new authority review.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Set the narrowest useful automation level and specify visible status, evidence, preview, confirmation, interruption, correction, recovery, prohibited action, and human escalation.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: Cruise control can hold speed, but the driver still owns the road, weather, route, and decision to stop. Utility automation needs an equally clear boundary.

The learner must leave with a saved **agent interaction, autonomy, and recovery specification**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain The Autonomy Slider: Augmentation vs. Automation in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish assistance, augmentation, workflow automation, bounded agency, autonomous action, transparency, confirmation, interruption, correction, recovery, escalation, and operating authority without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Never imply that a friendly interface, explanation, or artificial intelligence system may operate treatment, distribution, collection, flood-control, or safety systems without approved deterministic controls and qualified human authority.
6. Create a usable agent interaction, autonomy, and recovery specification with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

### `M10.CE01` Human-agent interaction and agentic user experience

Design the interaction that lets a person understand what the system is doing, inspect its evidence and limits, control consequential actions, interrupt work, correct mistakes, recover, and escalate.

- Required work product: **agent interaction, authority, and recovery specification**
- Secondary module connections: M24, M31, M41, M45, M46, M47, M48, M49, M50, M51, M52
- Learning objectives:
  - Explain calibrated trust and common ground in plain English.
  - Distinguish transparency, explanation, source evidence, system status, confirmation, and human authority.
  - Design progress, preview, confirmation, cancel, interrupt, correction, recovery, and escalation behavior for a utility task.
  - Evaluate an interface for automation bias, false confidence, inaccessible interaction, hidden state, and weak failure recovery.
  - Create an agent interaction, authority, and recovery specification.
- Assessment evidence:
  - interface risk critique
  - interaction-state ordering
  - failure recovery simulation
  - completed interaction specification
- Evidence boundary: A friendly or transparent interface does not make an answer accurate or an action authorized. Interface design must expose, not hide, the remaining evidence and authority limits.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among assistance, augmentation, workflow automation, bounded agency, autonomous action, transparency, confirmation, interruption, correction, recovery, escalation, and operating authority;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.
