# Staff Direction for M57: Demand Forecasting and Hydraulic/Network Modeling

## Your assignment

Teach demand forecasting and hydraulic or network modeling by keeping observed data, model assumptions, calibration, scenarios, uncertainty, physical limits, and decision use visible.

Open with this situation: A demand forecast looks precise but depends on weather, growth, customer behavior, meter coverage, and a calibration period that may not represent the decision horizon.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Decide whether a forecast or simulation is fit for the planning or operating question and what sensitivity and validation are required.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A map can guide a trip only when the scale, date, and road assumptions fit the route. Models also depend on fit to purpose.

The learner must leave with a saved **forecast and hydraulic-model decision sheet**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain Demand Forecasting and Hydraulic/Network Modeling in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish forecast, simulation, model, calibration, validation, scenario, sensitivity, uncertainty, and decision horizon without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Do not treat model output as observed fact or hide uncertainty behind a single line. Preserve engineering ownership and approved model assumptions.
6. Create a usable forecast and hydraulic-model decision sheet with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design from the **forecast and hydraulic-model decision sheet** and the completion evidence. Align each
outcome to teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and a saved record. Keep Foundation,
Practitioner, and Leader views connected to the same completion standard. The module belongs in
**AI Across the Water Lifecycle** and must not absorb the learning job of an adjacent module.

Reject vague outcomes such as know, learn, appreciate, or become familiar with. Require the learner
to explain, distinguish, analyze, evaluate, create, or defend something visible.

## Writing team

Write to one intelligent utility professional who is new to the topic. Define every term before
using it. Explain the major ideas already present in the source sequence:

- `Learning objectives`
- `Demand forecasting`
- `Hydraulic modeling + AI`
- `Applications`
- `Knowledge check`
- `Role takeaways`
- `Glossary`
- `Next`
- `Sources`

These headings are source inventory, not permission to keep weak prose. Every section must serve an
outcome, the opening decision, the work product, or the evidence boundary. Use the module situation
and one strong analogy. Do not invent a utility, incident, quotation, statistic, law, standard, or
source.

## Assessment team

Use this varied sequence as the starting design: estimate and explain, matching, decision multiple choice, final applied check. Place each check beside
the idea it evaluates. Give immediate explanatory feedback and retry. The final applied check must
inspect required fields in the **forecast and hydraulic-model decision sheet**. Scrolling, time spent, confidence, and an
unreviewed reflection do not prove completion.

## Articulate and production team

## Marketing and enrollment team

You may say that the module helps the learner **decide whether a forecast or simulation is fit for the planning or operating question and what sensitivity and validation are required** and produce a
**forecast and hydraulic-model decision sheet**. You may not promise savings, compliance, certification, safety,
implementation success, career advancement, or professional mastery. All public claims require a
separate source and approval record.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among forecast, simulation, model, calibration, validation, scenario, sensitivity, uncertainty, and decision horizon;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.

## Evidence and scope boundary

Do not treat model output as observed fact or hide uncertainty behind a single line. Preserve engineering ownership and approved model assumptions.
