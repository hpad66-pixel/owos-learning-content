# Staff Direction for M25: Cybersecurity for Water: CISA, NIST CSF 2.0, AWIA, WaterISAC, ISO 27001

## Your assignment

Connect water-sector cybersecurity, enterprise risk, facility protection, identity, least privilege, incident response, and AI use without exposing operational details.

Open with this situation: A vendor assistant needs documents and work-order data, but the proposed connection reaches farther into utility systems than the task requires.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Set the identity, access, network, data, monitoring, and response controls for a bounded utility use case.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A utility does not issue one master key because a contractor needs one room. Digital access should follow the same rule.

The learner must leave with a saved **water AI cybersecurity threat and control map**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain Cybersecurity for Water: CISA, NIST CSF 2.0, AWIA, WaterISAC, ISO 27001 in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish asset, threat, vulnerability, consequence, identity, privilege, segmentation, monitoring, and response without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Use current United States primary authority and official guidance. Do not publish facility-sensitive architecture, credentials, vulnerabilities, or exploitable operating detail.
6. Create a usable water AI cybersecurity threat and control map with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design from the **water AI cybersecurity threat and control map** and the completion evidence. Align each
outcome to teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and a saved record. Keep Foundation,
Practitioner, and Leader views connected to the same completion standard. The module belongs in
**Governance, Security and Provenance** and must not absorb the learning job of an adjacent module.

Reject vague outcomes such as know, learn, appreciate, or become familiar with. Require the learner
to explain, distinguish, analyze, evaluate, create, or defend something visible.

## Writing team

Write to one intelligent utility professional who is new to the topic. Define every term before
using it. Explain the major ideas already present in the source sequence:

- `Learning objectives`
- `The threat landscape`
- `NIST CSF 2.0`
- `Water-specific rules`
- `Securing AI systems`
- `Knowledge check`
- `Role takeaways`
- `Glossary`
- `Next`
- `Sources`

These headings are source inventory, not permission to keep weak prose. Every section must serve an
outcome, the opening decision, the work product, or the evidence boundary. Use the module situation
and one strong analogy. Do not invent a utility, incident, quotation, statistic, law, standard, or
source.

## Assessment team

Use this varied sequence as the starting design: estimate and explain, matching, decision multiple choice, final applied check. Place each check beside
the idea it evaluates. Give immediate explanatory feedback and retry. The final applied check must
inspect required fields in the **water AI cybersecurity threat and control map**. Scrolling, time spent, confidence, and an
unreviewed reflection do not prove completion.

## Marketing and enrollment team

You may say that the module helps the learner **set the identity, access, network, data, monitoring, and response controls for a bounded utility use case** and produce a
**water AI cybersecurity threat and control map**. You may not promise savings, compliance, certification, safety,
implementation success, career advancement, or professional mastery. All public claims require a
separate source and approval record.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among asset, threat, vulnerability, consequence, identity, privilege, segmentation, monitoring, and response;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.
