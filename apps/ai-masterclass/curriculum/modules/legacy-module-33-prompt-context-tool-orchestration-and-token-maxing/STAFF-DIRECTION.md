# Staff Direction for M33: Prompt, Context, Tool Orchestration, and Token Maxing

## Your assignment

Teach prompt, context, and tool orchestration as three connected layers that define the task, supply trusted material, constrain behavior, use tools, and evaluate results.

Open with this situation: A team keeps rewriting one prompt even though the real problem is missing source material, weak permissions, no output contract, and no evaluation step.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Decide which failure belongs to the prompt, the supplied context, the surrounding workflow, the source, or the human review design.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A good work order needs a clear task, the right records, approved tools, safety limits, and a closeout check. A prompt alone is only one part.

The learner must leave with a saved **governed prompt and context specification**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain Prompt, Context, Tool Orchestration, and Token Maxing in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish prompt, system instruction, context, source, tool, application control layer, output contract, evaluation, and retry without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Do not promise a universal prompt formula or expose private hidden reasoning. Treat model-specific behavior and context limits as dated test results.
6. Create a usable governed prompt and context specification with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

or legal claims. Give exact page, section, table, figure, or paragraph locators.

- Which sources can support the distinctions among prompt, system instruction, context, source, tool, application control layer, output contract, evaluation, and retry?
- What evidence would allow a practitioner to make this decision: Decide which failure belongs to the prompt, the supplied context, the surrounding workflow, the source, or the human review design.
- Which failure modes, counterexamples, and edge conditions could make the governed prompt and context specification misleading?
- How does the question change across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, finance, engineering, administration, technology, and public accountability?
- Which actions require qualified operator, engineer, water-quality, cybersecurity, privacy, legal, procurement, records, finance, accessibility, or executive review?
- Which claims remain outside the module boundary: Do not promise a universal prompt formula or expose private hidden reasoning. Treat model-specific behavior and context limits as dated test results.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design from the **governed prompt and context specification** and the completion evidence. Align each
outcome to teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and a saved record. Keep Foundation,
Practitioner, and Leader views connected to the same completion standard. The module belongs in
**Using AI in Professional Work** and must not absorb the learning job of an adjacent module.

Reject vague outcomes such as know, learn, appreciate, or become familiar with. Require the learner
to explain, distinguish, analyze, evaluate, create, or defend something visible.

## Writing team

Write to one intelligent utility professional who is new to the topic. Define every term before
using it. Explain the major ideas already present in the source sequence:

- `Context engineering`

- `Token maxing`

These headings are source inventory, not permission to keep weak prose. Every section must serve an
outcome, the opening decision, the work product, or the evidence boundary. Use the module situation
and one strong analogy. Do not invent a utility, incident, quotation, statistic, law, standard, or
source.

## Assessment team

Use this varied sequence as the starting design: estimate and explain, matching, decision multiple choice, final applied check. Place each check beside
the idea it evaluates. Give immediate explanatory feedback and retry. The final applied check must
inspect required fields in the **governed prompt and context specification**. Scrolling, time spent, confidence, and an
unreviewed reflection do not prove completion.

## Marketing and enrollment team

You may say that the module helps the learner **decide which failure belongs to the prompt, the supplied context, the surrounding workflow, the source, or the human review design** and produce a
**governed prompt and context specification**. You may not promise savings, compliance, certification, safety,
implementation success, career advancement, or professional mastery. All public claims require a
separate source and approval record.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among prompt, system instruction, context, source, tool, application control layer, output contract, evaluation, and retry;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.

## Evidence and scope boundary

Do not promise a universal prompt formula or expose private hidden reasoning. Treat model-specific behavior and context limits as dated test results.

A staff member can explain why the module exists, what the learner decides, what must be researched,
what the learner produces, what each graphic teaches, how learning is assessed, what content may
move, what claims are prohibited, and which human reviews remain before production.
