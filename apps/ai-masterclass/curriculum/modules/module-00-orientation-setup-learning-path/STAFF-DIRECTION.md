# Staff Direction for Module M00

## Your assignment

Build the front door to One Water AI. A learner should finish M00 knowing why the program matters to
their work, where they fit, how they will participate safely, what they want to produce, and what
they will do first. Do not turn this module into a software-installation class. Do not assume the
learner is a programmer. Do not ask the learner to absorb the entire curriculum before they can begin.

The module must work for an operator, clerk, engineer, planner, finance professional, executive,
elected official, consultant, vendor, technologist, researcher, and emerging professional. Examples
may change by role. The completion standard does not.

## What the learner must leave with

The learner must save a One Water AI Learning Charter containing:

1. their role and the decisions they influence;
2. one real water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, administrative, financial, technical, or
   public-service problem that will anchor their learning;
3. their selected role lens and reason for choosing it;
4. a readiness baseline and any support they need;
5. the source, privacy, evidence, and human-authority rules they agree to follow;
6. the professional work product they intend to create or improve;
7. the evidence that would show useful progress; and
8. the first accountable action, owner, and date.

If the module does not produce this record, the module is not complete.

## Research team

Research the questions below. Use the governed curriculum first. Register every external source and
use current primary authority for any load-bearing claim.

- What must every learner understand before entering the shared curriculum?
- Which setup requirements are truly universal, and which belong in optional technical preparation?
- What information may a learner safely use in course tools, and which information requires review?
- What readiness dimensions help route support without becoming an employment or performance test?
- What questions reliably connect a learner's role to a One Water problem and useful work product?
- What accommodations and alternatives allow the same outcome to be demonstrated in different ways?
- What language explains the pathway system without implying separate or lesser curricula?

Deliver a source package, claim register, glossary candidate list, readiness-diagnostic rationale,
and a recommendation for every current and proposed M00 content block. Mark fact, internal program
decision, Hardeep position, instructional scenario, and unresolved question separately.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design. Start with the Learning Charter and completion evidence. Align every teaching
section, activity, and check to one or more non-negotiable outcomes. Use observable verbs. A heading
such as `Getting set up` is not an outcome. State what the learner will identify, explain, classify,
choose, create, or revise.

Apply these rules:

- Every required activity must produce visible evidence.
- The readiness diagnostic routes support. It does not rank the learner's worth or job fitness.
- The pathway choice changes emphasis and examples. It does not split the curriculum authority.
- Technical preparation must be optional unless the next required learning experience truly depends
  on it.
- Every role example must connect to a real decision, record, asset, service, or public consequence.
- Every major component receives a plain-English instructor explanation before the learner uses it.
- The module ends with a specific first action and a transition into M01.

## Writing team

Write to one intelligent professional who is new to the program. Explain every new term immediately.
Use full sentences. Connect every abstraction to a utility situation. Keep the learner calm and
capable. Do not use vendor hype, artificial-intelligence clichés, unsupported claims, or technical
posturing.

Required teaching sections:

1. what One Water AI is and what it does not authorize;
2. how one shared curriculum serves different professional roles;
3. how to choose an anchor problem;
4. how to complete and interpret the readiness baseline;
5. how pathway emphasis works;
6. how to protect sources, privacy, and human authority during learning;
7. how to complete the Learning Charter;
8. where to find help and optional technical preparation;
9. what completion requires and what happens next; and
10. a module-specific FAQ and evidence boundary.

## Graphics and interaction team

Design graphics to explain relationships and choices. Do not add decorative stock images.

- Show one shared curriculum connecting multiple roles and decisions.
- Show readiness as a support profile, not a grade.
- Show pathways branching for relevance and reconnecting to the shared core.
- Let the learner classify information as approved, restricted, uncertain, or prohibited.
- Show the Learning Charter as a usable professional record with fields that connect forward into
  later modules.

Every graphic needs a teaching job, reading guide, learner conclusion, alternative text, phone
behavior, and reduced-motion equivalent. The module must use the governed component library or add a
tested shared component when the required mechanism does not exist.

## Assessment team

Assess only the non-negotiable outcomes. Include immediate explanatory feedback and retry.

- Use question flip cards to address entry misconceptions.
- Use classification to test information boundaries.
- Use a scenario decision to test pathway reasoning.
- Use the saved Learning Charter as the final applied check.
- Do not treat scrolling, time spent, confidence, or diagnostic score as completion.

The learner passes when required teaching is viewed, required interactions are completed, the
boundary check is passed, and the Learning Charter is saved with every required field.

## Articulate and production team

Build only from the approved manuscript and import package. Preserve stable block identifiers so
comments, assessments, graphics, and content-placement records remain traceable. The terminal,
application programming interface key, secret-file, and code-editor proposals must not appear as
universal required setup unless the blueprint is later changed through review.

Test keyboard, touch, phone, reduced motion, contrast, focus order, feedback, retry, save, return,
and completion behavior. Record the exact source revision used for the Articulate build.

## Marketing and enrollment team

Use the following value statement as the working internal direction:

> M00 gives every learner a clear starting point. They connect the curriculum to one real problem,
> choose a relevant emphasis, understand the evidence and participation rules, and leave with a
> personal Learning Charter that guides the rest of the program.

You may describe clarity, orientation, pathway selection, a readiness baseline, and the Learning
Charter. Do not promise promotion, salary change, cost savings, compliance, certification,
implementation success, or professional mastery.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when any of these conditions is true:

- required orientation depends on terminal or code-editor use;
- the learner cannot explain the program boundary in plain English;
- outcomes use vague verbs such as know, learn, or understand without visible evidence;
- a required activity has no feedback or completion evidence;
- role tracks appear to be separate curricula;
- the readiness diagnostic looks like a performance rating;
- the Learning Charter is missing a source boundary, support need, success evidence, or next action;
- a content block moved without preserving its original source and placement history;
- marketing copy makes an unsupported result or credential claim;
- accessibility or phone use is treated as a later repair; or
- any evidence, practitioner, learning-design, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.

## Definition of a successful prototype

The prototype is successful when a staff member can open M00 and answer, without guessing:

- what this module is for;
- what the learner must be able to do;
- what must be researched and sourced;
- what must be written and designed;
- what the learner must produce;
- what does not belong in the module;
- what can be moved, copied, cross-referenced, or made optional;
- how quality will be judged; and
- what remains pending before learner-facing production.
