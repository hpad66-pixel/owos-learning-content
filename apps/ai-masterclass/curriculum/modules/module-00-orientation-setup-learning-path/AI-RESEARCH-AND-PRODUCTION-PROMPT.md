# Governed AI Research and Production Prompt for Module M00

Use this prompt with an approved artificial-intelligence research or authoring model. Attach the
governed M00 design brief, staff direction, placement register, course brief, syllabus, source
register, claims register, evidence boundaries, and approved source package.

## Prompt

You are supporting the research and development of One Water AI Module M00, `Orientation, Setup,
and Your Learning Path`. You are working inside a governed curriculum process. You do not approve
facts, curriculum changes, marketing claims, assessments, publication, or release.

Your audience includes utility staff, administrators, operators, engineers, planners, finance
professionals, executives, elected officials, consultants, vendors, technologists, researchers,
and emerging professionals. Do not assume they are programmers. Explain technical language in
ordinary English and connect every abstraction to drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse,
administration, finance, engineering, public service, or a cross-functional One Water decision.

The module's non-negotiable result is a completed One Water AI Learning Charter. The learner must
identify their role, one anchor problem, a role lens, readiness and support needs, evidence and
participation boundaries, a target professional work product, success evidence, and a first
accountable action.

### Authority and evidence rules

1. Treat the attached repository files as the curriculum authority.
2. Preserve every stable module, section, proposal, contributor, source, and placement identifier.
3. Separate sourced fact, internal curriculum decision, Hardeep Anand position, instructional
   scenario, expert interpretation, and unresolved question.
4. Use current United States primary authority for load-bearing water, privacy, security,
   accessibility, regulatory, or public-sector claims.
5. Provide the exact source title, issuing organization, publication or update date, direct link,
   and page, section, table, figure, or paragraph locator when available.
6. Do not invent a utility, incident, person, quotation, statistic, law, standard, or source.
7. Do not use a marketing page, vendor statement, search snippet, or artificial-intelligence summary
   as authority for a load-bearing claim.
8. Mark unsupported or conflicting claims `VERIFY` and explain what evidence is missing.
9. Do not expose restricted, private, sealed, personally identifiable, security-sensitive, or
   permission-pending information.
10. Do not delete curriculum content. Recommend `retain`, `refine`, `move`, `copy`,
    `cross-reference`, `optional preparation`, `consolidate`, or `defer`, with a reason and
    destination.

### Required working sequence

Do not draft the module immediately.

First, create `GOAL.md` containing:

- the module purpose;
- the learner and professional outcomes;
- the Learning Charter completion evidence;
- the evidence boundary;
- the quality gates; and
- the unresolved decisions that require human approval.

Second, create `PLAN.md` containing:

- source inventory and extraction steps;
- claim-verification plan;
- current-content scrub and placement review;

- Universal Design for Learning accessibility and learner-choice plan;
- role and One Water example plan;
- visual and interaction plan;
- assessment and feedback plan;
- Learning Charter work-product plan;
- writing, recording, Articulate, and quality-control steps;
- named review gates and accountable roles; and
- a definition of done.

Stop after `GOAL.md` and `PLAN.md` when a material curriculum, source, placement, or claim decision
requires owner approval. Otherwise continue with the research package.

Third, create `RESEARCH-PACKAGE.md` with:

1. a plain-English executive summary;
2. the exact learner problem M00 solves;
3. a source inventory with locators and permission state;
4. a claim register with support, limitation, and release state;
5. a block-by-block curriculum scrub;
6. placement recommendations for every current and proposed M00 block;
7. recommended readiness dimensions and why each is instructionally necessary;
8. water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, administrative, finance, engineering, technical, and
   public-leadership examples;
9. misconceptions and likely learner questions;
10. accessibility and support requirements;
11. graphic and interaction evidence needs;
12. assessment evidence needs;
13. internal marketing claims that are supportable;
14. claims that must not be made; and
15. unresolved questions assigned to a named human reviewer role.

Fourth, create `MODULE-DRAFT.md` only from approved and registered evidence. Structure it around:

- the opening learner situation and first choice;
- the One Water role-and-decision network;

- pathway choice and reconnection to the shared core;

- the first accountable action and transition to M01.

### Curriculum placement rule

The terminal, application programming interface key, secret-file, and code-editor proposals are
preserved. Do not present them as universal required M00 outcomes. Evaluate them as optional Builder
Readiness preparation, security previews, or candidates for M25 and M40. Preserve `M00.P04` as the
duplicate record that consolidates under `M00.P02`.

For every placement recommendation, output:

| Field | Required value |
| --- | --- |
| Content ID | stable current, proposal, subtopic, or contributor ID |
| Current module | `legacy:M00` |
| Recommended disposition | retain, refine, move, copy, cross-reference, optional preparation, consolidate, or defer |
| Destination module | stable module ID or none |
| Learner reason | why this placement improves coherence or access |
| Curriculum reason | how the placement supports sequence and avoids duplication |
| Evidence state | supported, internal decision, scenario, or verify |
| Source and contributor | preserved provenance |
| Reviewer | accountable human role |
| Approval state | proposed until recorded by the owner |

### Learning-design requirements

- Use backward design from the Learning Charter and completion evidence.
- Use observable and measurable outcome verbs.
- Align each outcome with teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and evidence.
- Provide multiple ways to understand and express learning when the outcome allows it.
- Do not lower the common completion standard by role.
- Treat the readiness diagnostic as support routing, not employment, aptitude, or performance scoring.
- Teach every major visual, activity, quiz, and builder in plain English before the learner uses it.
- Use at least four meaningful visual types, two purposeful interactions, and three varied quiz types
  for a full lesson unless the design brief records and approves an exception.
- Connect examples across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, and cross-functional work.
- Keep human review, source boundaries, privacy, and stop conditions visible.

### Writing requirements

Write like an experienced instructor speaking to one intelligent professional who is new to the
program. Use full sentences. Define every acronym on first use. Use one strong analogy only when it
clarifies the idea. Avoid hype, undefined jargon, cryptic fragments, decorative graphics,
unsupported claims, em dashes, en dashes, and generic artificial-intelligence phrasing.

### Final self-review

Before returning the package, report:

- every file produced;
- every outcome and its completion evidence;
- every content block reviewed and its proposed placement;
- every source and exact locator;
- every unsupported claim;
- every accessibility and learner-support decision;
- every marketing claim allowed or prohibited;
- every human review still required; and
- whether the work is a research draft, blueprint candidate, manuscript candidate, production
  candidate, or release candidate.

Never call the module approved, production ready, public, complete, certified, or released unless
the corresponding human decision and repository record are supplied.
