# Staff Direction for M17: Life Is a Graph: Taxonomy, Ontology, Knowledge Graphs

## Your assignment

Teach taxonomy, ontology, and knowledge graphs in plain language so learners can move from lists and hierarchies to governed relationships among assets, people, records, events, and decisions.

Open with this situation: Operations, engineering, finance, and customer service use different names for the same asset and cannot trace which records describe it.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Choose the structure needed to organize terms, define meaning, or connect relationships for a One Water question.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A tree gives each thing an address. A web shows how things affect one another. Utilities need both the address and the relationships.

The learner must leave with a saved **One Water concept and relationship map**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain Life Is a Graph: Taxonomy, Ontology, Knowledge Graphs in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish list, taxonomy, ontology, entity, relationship, property, rule, and knowledge graph without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Do not present a graph as automatically correct or complete. Meaning, identity, provenance, validation, and stewardship remain governed work.
6. Create a usable One Water concept and relationship map with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design from the **One Water concept and relationship map** and the completion evidence. Align each
outcome to teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and a saved record. Keep Foundation,
Practitioner, and Leader views connected to the same completion standard. The module belongs in
**Retrieval, Generation and the Data Foundation** and must not absorb the learning job of an adjacent module.

Reject vague outcomes such as know, learn, appreciate, or become familiar with. Require the learner
to explain, distinguish, analyze, evaluate, create, or defend something visible.

## Assessment team

Use this varied sequence as the starting design: estimate and explain, matching, decision multiple choice, final applied check. Place each check beside
the idea it evaluates. Give immediate explanatory feedback and retry. The final applied check must
inspect required fields in the **One Water concept and relationship map**. Scrolling, time spent, confidence, and an
unreviewed reflection do not prove completion.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among list, taxonomy, ontology, entity, relationship, property, rule, and knowledge graph;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.

## Evidence and scope boundary

Do not present a graph as automatically correct or complete. Meaning, identity, provenance, validation, and stewardship remain governed work.
