# Module 01 FAQ

## Is this saying that AI should never be used in utility work?

No. The lesson is about using AI where it helps while keeping the accountable person and the source
boundary clear. A bounded, reviewed use case is often a better place to begin than an undefined
promise of automation.

## If an AI answer is accurate, why does a person still need to review it?

Accuracy in one moment does not transfer responsibility. The reviewer knows the actual situation,
the current records, the applicable procedure, and the consequence of acting. The person is not
there merely to approve a screen. They are there to exercise accountable judgment.

## Does this mean the reviewer has to redo all of the work?

Not necessarily. Review should match the risk and the task. The boundary card helps the team decide
what evidence, checks, and approvals are proportionate before the tool is used.

## Can an AI tool make an operating decision by itself?

This module does not authorize any operating use. Whether any system may take an action depends on
the task, controls, policy, safety requirements, validation, and approved authority. Start by
making the handoffs visible.

## What is an approved source boundary?

It is a clear statement of what information the tool may use for a task and what it may not use.
For example, a team may limit a retrieval task to an approved current document set rather than the
open internet or a collection of unknown files.

## What should I do if the AI response is unclear or outside the task?

Do not stretch the tool beyond its boundary. Stop, escalate, or route the question to the
appropriate person. A good boundary card names that stop condition in advance.
