# Module 01: AI and Utility Work

## Manuscript status

Structured authoring draft. It defines the instructional experience but deliberately does not make
unverified technology, legal, operational, or performance claims. Replace bracketed source prompts
with approved, registered evidence during research intake.

## Learner promise

By the end of this module, the learner can explain the difference between using AI to support a
utility task and allowing a system to make an accountable utility decision. The learner can also
name the person who must remain responsible.

## Opening situation

You are part of a utility team. A new AI tool summarizes a set of customer messages, maintenance
records, and recent work orders. It recommends a next step. The recommendation may be useful, but
it is not the same thing as an authorized decision.

Before you learn the technical language, make one choice: what would you need to know before anyone
acts on that recommendation?

## Instructor explanation: the central distinction

AI can help people notice patterns, organize information, draft language, compare options, and make
work easier to review. That is assistance. The accountable person still decides whether the output
is appropriate for the situation, checks the evidence, and records the decision when the work calls
for it.

In utility work, that distinction matters because the result may affect an asset, a customer, an
operating condition, a budget, a public communication, or a regulatory record. A helpful answer is
not automatically a safe instruction. The point of this lesson is not to make you afraid of AI. It
is to help you use it with enough clarity that its value does not come at the cost of accountability.

## Explanatory graphic: the accountability decision map

Show a five-step map:

1. A person states a bounded task.
2. The system receives approved context and produces a draft, pattern, or recommendation.
3. A designated reviewer checks the output against the record and the actual situation.
4. An authorized person accepts, changes, escalates, or rejects the recommendation.
5. The decision and its reasoning are recorded where required.

### How the instructor explains the graphic

Read the map from left to right. The system can contribute at step two. It does not silently absorb
steps three through five. Ask the learner to identify the accountable reviewer and the authorized
decision owner for their own work context.

## Interaction: sort the responsibility

Give learners example actions to place into one of three columns:

- suitable for AI assistance;
- requires human review;
- requires authorized human decision.

Each choice receives an explanation connected to the utility consequence. The final feedback must
say why a task belongs in that column, not simply whether it is correct.

## Instructor explanation: why context changes the answer

An AI system does not understand a utility in the same way an experienced operator, engineer, or
manager understands it. It works from the information, instructions, and tools available to it. If
the context is incomplete, outdated, poorly governed, or outside the question, the result can sound
confident while missing the point.

The learner now compares two hypothetical responses to the same question. One uses a small,
approved, current source set. The other has no stated source boundary. The goal is not to judge a
brand. The goal is to see why source, scope, and review change the trustworthiness of an answer.

## Worked sequence: handoff, not magic

Show a four-part handoff:

```text
Question → approved context → AI-supported draft → accountable review and recorded decision
```

Explain that a useful workflow makes the handoff visible. If nobody can say who reviewed the output
or what sources were used, the work is harder to defend and harder to improve.

## Learner work product: AI use-case boundary card

The learner completes a concise card:

| Field | Learner entry |
| --- | --- |
| Utility task | What specific task should AI help with? |
| Intended support | Summarize, classify, draft, retrieve, compare, or another bounded support function |
| Source boundary | Which sources may be used? |
| Accountable reviewer | Who must review the output? |
| Decision owner | Who may authorize action? |
| Stop condition | When must the system defer, escalate, or stop? |
| Record | What must be saved or documented? |

## Final applied check

Present a short utility situation using only approved scenario language. Ask the learner to choose
the missing safeguard and explain their choice. The feedback should connect the answer to the
learner's boundary card.
