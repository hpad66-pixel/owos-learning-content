# Staff Direction for M05: How a Model Is Actually Made

## Your assignment

Explain how models and language systems move from data and design through training, evaluation, release, monitoring, incident response, rollback, correction, and retirement so learners can ask where behavior and limitations came from.

Open with this situation: A supplier reports a strong benchmark score, but the utility cannot see the training boundary, evaluation population, adaptation method, or operating conditions.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Identify the complete lifecycle, provenance, evaluation, operating, and recovery evidence a utility needs before accepting a model or language-system suitability claim.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: Hiring someone, training them, testing them, assigning a job, and supervising their work are different steps. Model development also needs those steps kept separate.

The learner must leave with a saved **production AI lifecycle and provenance card**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain How a Model Is Actually Made in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish data preparation, experiment, training, adaptation, evaluation, artifact, release, deployment, monitoring, incident, rollback, correction, and retirement without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Do not infer training data, architecture, labor, performance, monitoring quality, or production fitness from marketing or one demonstration. Keep proprietary, unknown, untested, and unapproved details visible.
6. Create a usable production AI lifecycle and provenance card with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

### `M05.CE01` The production AI lifecycle, from evidence to operation

Connect data, model or language-system design, evaluation, release, monitoring, incident response, rollback, and improvement in one traceable lifecycle. The learner should see that a working demonstration is only one controlled stage.

- Required work product: **production AI lifecycle and provenance card**
- Secondary module connections: M16, M24, M40, M43, M53
- Learning objectives:
  - Explain the difference among experiment, demonstration, pilot, production service, and governed operating capability.
  - Trace one utility application through data, model, prompt, context, tool, evaluation, release, monitoring, incident, rollback, and correction records.
  - Identify the owner, evidence, entry condition, exit condition, and stop rule at every lifecycle gate.
  - Distinguish a model change from a data, retrieval, prompt, tool, permission, interface, or workflow change.
  - Create and defend a production AI lifecycle and provenance card for a bounded utility use case.
- Assessment evidence:
  - lifecycle ordering check
  - change-type classification
  - utility release-gate decision
  - completed lifecycle card
- Evidence boundary: Do not treat a stage name, vendor platform, deployment, or passing test as proof that a utility application is safe, useful, supported, or approved.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among data preparation, experiment, training, adaptation, evaluation, artifact, release, deployment, monitoring, incident, rollback, correction, and retirement;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.
