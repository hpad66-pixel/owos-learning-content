# Staff Direction for M50: The Vendor: AI-Driven Sales, Marketing, Customer Service, Lead Management

## Your assignment

Teach vendors how AI may support research, marketing, sales, lead management, proposals, service, and customer success without inventing claims or abusing utility trust.

Open with this situation: A vendor automates outreach with generic claims about utility savings and sends messages that show no understanding of procurement, operations, or public accountability.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Choose an AI-assisted commercial workflow and define the evidence, consent, disclosure, approval, records, and handoff it requires.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A good proposal is like a treatment submittal. It must show what is offered, what evidence supports it, what limits apply, and who stands behind it.

The learner must leave with a saved **utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain The Vendor: AI-Driven Sales, Marketing, Customer Service, Lead Management in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish lead, prospect, customer, claim, evidence, consent, personalization, proposal, service record, and human handoff without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: Do not fabricate testimonials, savings, clients, certifications, regulatory claims, or product performance. Protect contact data and honor procurement boundaries.
6. Create a usable utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

or legal claims. Give exact page, section, table, figure, or paragraph locators.

- Which sources can support the distinctions among lead, prospect, customer, claim, evidence, consent, personalization, proposal, service record, and human handoff?
- What evidence would allow a practitioner to make this decision: Choose an AI-assisted commercial workflow and define the evidence, consent, disclosure, approval, records, and handoff it requires.
- Which failure modes, counterexamples, and edge conditions could make the utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card misleading?
- How does the question change across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, finance, engineering, administration, technology, and public accountability?
- Which actions require qualified operator, engineer, water-quality, cybersecurity, privacy, legal, procurement, records, finance, accessibility, or executive review?
- Which claims remain outside the module boundary: Do not fabricate testimonials, savings, clients, certifications, regulatory claims, or product performance. Protect contact data and honor procurement boundaries.

## Curriculum and learning-design team

Use backward design from the **utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card** and the completion evidence. Align each
outcome to teaching, practice, feedback, assessment, and a saved record. Keep Foundation,
Practitioner, and Leader views connected to the same completion standard. The module belongs in
**Role-Based Practice** and must not absorb the learning job of an adjacent module.

Reject vague outcomes such as know, learn, appreciate, or become familiar with. Require the learner
to explain, distinguish, analyze, evaluate, create, or defend something visible.

## Writing team

Write to one intelligent utility professional who is new to the topic. Define every term before
using it. Explain the major ideas already present in the source sequence:

- `Learning objectives`
- `Know the buyer`
- `AI go-to-market`
- `Customer service, honestly`
- `Knowledge check`
- `Role takeaways`
- `Glossary`
- `Next`
- `Sources`

These headings are source inventory, not permission to keep weak prose. Every section must serve an
outcome, the opening decision, the work product, or the evidence boundary. Use the module situation
and one strong analogy. Do not invent a utility, incident, quotation, statistic, law, standard, or
source.

## Assessment team

Use this varied sequence as the starting design: true or false with correction, ordering, evidence classification, final applied check. Place each check beside
the idea it evaluates. Give immediate explanatory feedback and retry. The final applied check must
inspect required fields in the **utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card**. Scrolling, time spent, confidence, and an
unreviewed reflection do not prove completion.

## Articulate and production team

Build only from the approved manuscript and import package. Preserve module, section, proposal,
subtopic, enhancement, contributor, source, assessment, visual, and work-product identifiers. Test
keyboard, touch, phone, zoom, contrast, reduced motion, focus order, feedback, retry, save, return,
and deterministic completion. Record the exact curriculum revision used.

## Marketing and enrollment team

You may say that the module helps the learner **choose an ai-assisted commercial workflow and define the evidence, consent, disclosure, approval, records, and handoff it requires** and produce a
**utility-market AI commercial workflow and proof card**. You may not promise savings, compliance, certification, safety,
implementation success, career advancement, or professional mastery. All public claims require a
separate source and approval record.

## Quality-control team

Reject the module when:

- the learner cannot explain the core distinctions among lead, prospect, customer, claim, evidence, consent, personalization, proposal, service record, and human handoff;
- the opening situation disappears and the module becomes a glossary or product tour;
- the work product has no source, role, limitation, review, or next-action fields;
- a current section, proposal, subtopic, enhancement, or contributor record is omitted or moved
  without provenance;
- a visual has no teaching conclusion or an interaction has no consequence;
- assessment does not test the stated outcomes or provide feedback and retry;
- a role example changes the standard instead of the context;
- marketing makes an unsupported outcome claim;
- accessibility and phone use are left for later; or
- the evidence, learning-design, practitioner, technical, or release gate remains unresolved.

## Evidence and scope boundary

Do not fabricate testimonials, savings, clients, certifications, regulatory claims, or product performance. Protect contact data and honor procurement boundaries.

## Definition of a successful blueprint

A staff member can explain why the module exists, what the learner decides, what must be researched,
what the learner produces, what each graphic teaches, how learning is assessed, what content may
move, what claims are prohibited, and which human reviews remain before production.
