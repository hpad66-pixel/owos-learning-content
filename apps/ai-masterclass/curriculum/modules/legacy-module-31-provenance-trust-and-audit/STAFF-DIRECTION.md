# Staff Direction for M31: Provenance, Trust, and Audit

## Your assignment

Teach provenance, explanation, transparency, and audit as the ability to trace an answer or action through sources, versions, identities, transformations, system behavior, approvals, limitations, decisions, and corrections.

Open with this situation: An assistant cites a procedure, but the review team cannot tell which revision was retrieved, what text supported the answer, or who approved its use.

Ask the learner to decide before teaching begins: **Decide whether an artificial intelligence-supported record provides the explanation, evidence, trace, provenance, justification, uncertainty, and human approval required for its consequence and audience.**

Use this analogy once as the front door, then return to the utility work: A laboratory chain of custody protects the standing of a sample by recording every handoff. AI-supported evidence needs the same traceability.

The learner must leave with a saved **explanation, provenance, and decision-justification record**. If the module does not produce that
record and evidence, the module is not complete.

## Non-negotiable learner outcomes

1. Explain Provenance, Trust, and Audit in plain English and connect it to a real water, wastewater, stormwater, or One Water decision.
2. Distinguish explanation, transparency, interpretability, traceability, source, lineage, provenance, evidence, version, transformation, approval, audit, decision justification, uncertainty, and correction without using one term to hide several different jobs.
3. Analyze the module situation, identify missing evidence, and name the professional consequence of a weak decision.
4. Compare how the same question changes across drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, administration, engineering, finance, technology, and public leadership.
5. Evaluate sources, assumptions, limitations, permissions, and human authority using this boundary: A fluent explanation can be wrong. Provenance can show origin without proving accuracy, completeness, applicability, or professional judgment. Keep what remains unknown visible.
6. Create a usable explanation, provenance, and decision-justification record with evidence, roles, limits, review steps, and the next accountable action.
7. Defend the recommendation to Foundation, Practitioner, and Leader audiences while keeping disagreement and uncertainty visible.

### `M31.CE01` Explainability, transparency, trace, and justification

Teach the difference among an understandable explanation, a source trace, an interpretable model, a decision justification, and an unknown mechanism. Match the evidence burden to the consequence and audience.

- Required work product: **explanation, evidence, and decision-justification record**
- Secondary module connections: M22, M24, M47, M63
- Learning objectives:
  - Distinguish explanation, transparency, interpretability, traceability, provenance, evidence, and decision justification.
  - Identify what a model, application, vendor, reviewer, and accountable decision maker can and cannot explain.
  - Match explanation and evidence needs to the task, consequence, affected person, professional standard, and decision right.
  - Reject an explanation that is fluent but unsupported, incomplete, inapplicable, or disconnected from the actual system record.
  - Create an explanation, evidence, and decision-justification record.
- Assessment evidence:
  - term distinction matching
  - explanation-quality critique
  - consequence-based evidence decision
  - completed justification record
- Evidence boundary: A plausible explanation can be wrong. Provenance can show origin without proving accuracy. Human justification remains separate from model output.
