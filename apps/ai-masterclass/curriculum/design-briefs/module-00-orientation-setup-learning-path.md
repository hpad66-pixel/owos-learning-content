## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What consequential situation opens the lesson? | A learner has been invited into a large AI curriculum and must decide where to begin without wasting time, exposing restricted information, or selecting a pathway that does not fit their work. |
| What must the learner decide before teaching begins? | Which real One Water decision, service problem, or professional opportunity will anchor their learning. |
| What professional consequence makes this matter? | Without a clear starting problem and learning agreement, the curriculum becomes a collection of interesting topics instead of a usable capability-building program. |
| What should the learner be able to do afterward? | Explain the Academy promise and boundaries, complete a readiness baseline, choose a learning emphasis, identify required support, and create a personal One Water AI Learning Charter. |
| What usable work product will the learner create? | A versioned One Water AI Learning Charter containing the learner's role, anchor problem, pathway, readiness baseline, evidence rules, support needs, target work product, and first accountable action. |
| What evidence is required for completion? | Orientation acknowledgement, readiness diagnostic, pathway choice, scenario-based safety check, saved Learning Charter, and first-action commitment. |

## Outcome framework

The module uses backward design. Staff begin with the Learning Charter and completion evidence, then
select teaching, activities, and assessment that produce that evidence. Outcomes use observable
verbs from the revised Bloom framework. The learner must identify, explain, classify, choose, and
create. Universal Design for Learning informs multiple ways to understand the program, participate,
and express the Learning Charter without lowering the completion standard.

### Non-negotiable learner outcomes

By the end of M00, the learner can:

1. explain in plain English what One Water AI teaches, what it does not authorize, and why human
   accountability remains visible throughout the program;
2. identify one real water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, administrative, financial, technical, or
   public-service problem that will anchor their learning;
3. complete and interpret a readiness baseline covering role, AI fluency, data and evidence,
   governance, technical confidence, and organizational support;
4. select the shared core and the role lens most relevant to the decisions they influence, while
   recognizing that the complete curriculum remains connected;
5. classify information that is safe to use, restricted, uncertain, or in need of approval before
   it is placed into an artificial-intelligence system;
6. create a One Water AI Learning Charter with a target work product, support needs, success
   evidence, and first accountable action; and
7. locate optional technical preparation without mistaking terminal or code-editor proficiency for
   the Academy's universal entry requirement.

### Curriculum outcomes

- Establish one common participation, evidence, privacy, and human-authority contract.
- Capture a useful baseline that later modules can revisit.
- Connect the learner to one shared curriculum and one relevant role lens.
- Create the first Fieldbook artifact and a durable starting record.
- Identify support and accessibility needs before production assumptions become barriers.
- Route technical preparation to optional prerequisite labs or the modules where it is actually used.

### Practical and professional outcomes

- The learner can tell a supervisor or colleague why they are taking the program and what work
  product they intend to produce.
- The learner begins with a named utility or organizational problem instead of generic interest in AI.
- The program team can see who needs technical preparation, evidence support, accessibility support,
  or a different learning emphasis.

### Internal marketing outcomes

- The landing page and enrollment conversation can promise clarity, a personal starting point, and a
  governed path to a useful professional work product.
- Staff may say that M00 helps learners choose a relevant pathway and create a Learning Charter.
- Staff may not promise job advancement, financial savings, regulatory compliance, certification,
  implementation success, or mastery from completing M00.

  diagnostic, safe participation, pathway choice, and the Learning Charter.

  Detailed editing and validation belong with the modules that use those formats.

## Learner FAQ plan

| Likely learner question | Direct answer and utility example | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do I need to know how to code? | No. Every learner needs enough fluency to ask good questions, inspect evidence, and understand limits. Optional technical preparation is available for learners who will build. | Do not promise that no later elective or studio uses technical tools. |
| Why do I need one anchor problem now? | It gives the curriculum a real place to land. A finance learner may follow a capital-plan decision while an operator follows a shift handoff. | The anchor problem is a learning context, not authorization to change live work. |
| Am I locked into one pathway? | No. The pathway changes emphasis and examples. The shared curriculum stays connected, and learners may deepen or cross-skill later. | Pathway recommendations do not grant credit or waive prerequisites. |
| Can I use real utility records in course tools? | Only when the source, permission, privacy, and system rules allow it. Use approved or de-identified material when required. | M00 does not make a legal, privacy, cybersecurity, or records determination. |
| Is the readiness diagnostic a test? | It is a support and baseline tool. It identifies where you may need explanation, technical preparation, or review help. | It is not a credential, employment assessment, or performance rating. |
| What if I do not know my final project yet? | Name the best current problem and mark uncertainty. The charter is versioned and may be revised as evidence improves. | Revision does not erase the original baseline. |
| What happens to my Learning Charter? | It becomes the first Fieldbook record and a reference point for later modules, coaching, and the capstone. | Storage, consent, and sharing follow the approved learning-record policy. |

## Diversity check

- Adjacent module reviewed: M01 AI and Utility Work.
- Opening pattern intentionally different: M00 begins with personal orientation and a choice of anchor
  problem. M01 begins with an AI-supported utility recommendation requiring an authority decision.
- Dominant visual intentionally different: M00 uses a role-and-decision network. M01 uses an
  accountability decision map.
- Ordered visual-shape sequence differs: network, radar, decision tree, sorter, builder.
- Interaction pair intentionally different: readiness diagnostic and charter builder.
- Quiz sequence intentionally different: flip card, self-classification, scenario multiple choice,
  applied charter check.
- Work-product format intentionally different: personal learning charter rather than use-case boundary card.
- Structural fingerprint remains pending rendered production.
