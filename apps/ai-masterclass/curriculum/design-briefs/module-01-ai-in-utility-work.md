# Module 01 Design Brief: AI in Utility Work

## Identity

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Course and module ID | owos-ai-masterclass-series / module-01-ai-in-utility-work |
| Working title | AI and utility work: what changes, what does not, and who remains accountable |
| Learner roles | utility leaders, operations, engineering, data, technology, and governance roles |
| Competencies | distinguish an AI-supported task from a delegated decision; identify an accountable human owner |
| Controlled sources | pending research intake |
| Evidence boundary | authoring scaffold only, not yet a factual learner lesson |

## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What consequential situation opens the lesson? | A utility team receives an AI-generated recommendation concerning a service or operating decision and must decide what to do next. |
| What must the learner decide before teaching begins? | Whether the recommendation can be acted upon, checked, escalated, or rejected. |
| What professional consequence makes this matter? | Confusing assistance with authority can create operational, safety, customer, compliance, or trust consequences. |
| What should the learner be able to do afterward? | Describe the role of AI in a bounded task and name the person responsible for the decision. |
| What usable work product will the learner create? | An AI use-case boundary card. |
| What evidence is required for completion? | Complete the scenario decision, accountability sorting, final check, and boundary card. |

## Experience plan

| Teaching idea | Natural shape | Selected visual | Learner action | What becomes visible | Quiz type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI can support work without owning a decision | boundary | accountability decision map | place responsibilities | where machine support ends and human authority begins | classification |
| Utility context changes whether output is useful | comparison | before-and-after context comparison | inspect two responses | why the same model can produce different usefulness | misconception flip card |
| Responsibility travels through a workflow | sequence | handoff sequence | reorder the handoff | who checks, approves, and records the result | ordering |
| A safe starting point is bounded | canvas | boundary card builder | complete a first-use case | scope, owner, sources, review, and stop condition | applied decision |

## Module fingerprint

- Narrative architecture: consequential scenario to clear distinction to learner-owned boundary card.
- Visual types: decision map, comparison, handoff sequence, boundary canvas, role illustration.
- Interactions: accountability sorting and use-case boundary builder.
- Quiz sequence: question flip cards, classification check, ordered handoff, final application decision.
- Connected learning: header Graph, Community, and Start controls open the shared right-side drawer.
- FAQ: six learner questions at the end of the module before evidence and connected learning.
- Motion: handoff sequence may reveal one accountable transition at a time. Reduced-motion mode shows the full sequence with numbered order.
- Mobile: cards stack; graph and community use one full-screen drawer.
