# The One Water AI Fieldbook

## Companion workbook blueprint

**Working subtitle:** From First Question to Governed Utility Pilot

**Format:** printed lay-flat workbook, accessible fillable PDF, and private participant workspace

**Target length:** 220 to 260 pages
**Ownership:** the participant owns their entries; organizational and confidential material remains
private unless the participant explicitly shares it for review

**Curriculum source:** `../SYLLABUS.md`

**Synchronization rule:** A change to a course, module, learning job, applied result, or capstone
requirement must update the curriculum prospectus and Fieldbook in the same controlled build. See
`../CURRICULUM-SYNCHRONIZATION-CONTRACT.md`.

## The purpose

The Fieldbook is the connective tissue of the six-month fellowship. It prevents the program from
becoming 64 separate learning moments. Each module adds one decision, map, test, reflection, or
professional record. By Module 64, those records form a governed AI implementation portfolio.

It is part workbook, part laboratory notebook, part decision record, and part executive playbook.
It should look and feel like something a participant keeps on their desk after the program.

## What the participant receives

- one numbered printed Fieldbook with the participant's name;
- an accessible fillable PDF for offline use;
- a private digital workspace for saved versions and instructor feedback;
- downloadable blank templates for organizational reuse;
- QR codes or short links to approved graph views, source collections, examples, and studios;
- a progress map showing how each activity contributes to the final portfolio; and
- a final clean export that excludes private notes the participant does not choose to share.

## The repeating four-page module rhythm

Each of the 64 modules receives a compact working section:

1. **Before:** What do I currently believe, and what decision would I make?
2. **Learn:** What relationship, mechanism, or distinction matters?
3. **Apply:** What does this mean for one utility task or organizational situation?
4. **Keep:** What record enters my implementation portfolio?

Some studio and capstone modules receive additional pages for diagrams, testing evidence, and review.

## Front matter

| Section | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| How to use the Fieldbook | Explain private notes, shared work, feedback, and portfolio export |
| My professional context | Record role, organization type, responsibilities, and learning objective |
| My confidentiality boundary | Identify information the participant must not enter or share |
| My six-month commitment | Name available time, sponsor, peer, and practical opportunity |
| Baseline AI fluency | Capture the learner's starting confidence and current practices |
| Initial organizational readiness | Record the starting condition for comparison after six months |

## Fieldbook design principles

- Use plain English and generous writing space.
- Begin with a real decision, not a definition.
- Show one completed example beside every important blank template.
- Use diagrams when relationships or sequences are easier to see than describe.
- Keep confidential organizational content out of shared systems by default.
- Separate private reflection, instructor-review material, and capstone evidence visibly.
- Make every digital form keyboard accessible and printable without losing meaning.
- Never require a participant to disclose protected, security-sensitive, client, personnel, or
  operational information.
- Give every record a stable ID so it can connect to the learning record without storing its
  confidential contents.

## Participant portfolio at graduation

The final export should contain:

1. an executive summary;
2. the selected utility problem and baseline;
3. the prioritized opportunity portfolio;
4. the selected architecture and reason;
5. data and knowledge readiness;
6. governance, security, permissions, and human authority;
7. evaluation, cost, and success measures;
8. workforce and operating-model implications;
9. the 90-day pilot brief;
10. the one-year roadmap; and
11. unresolved questions and the next accountable decision.

## Production plan

1. Build the visual identity and page system from the OWOS Graphite standard.
2. Prototype the first eight module sections.
3. Test the printed and fillable forms with five utility professionals.
4. Build the private digital workspace and feedback boundary.
5. Connect template completion events to the learning record without ingesting confidential text.
6. Complete accessibility, print, mobile, privacy, and instructor-workflow QA.
7. Release the Fieldbook version with the exact course cohort and curriculum version.
