# Module 4 Video Script: The Handoff

Intended length: 8 to 11 minutes

Status: working recording draft

## Spoken words

This lesson is about what happens between specialized agents and people. That boundary is called a handoff. A good handoff transfers more than a finished sentence. It transfers the goal, current status, evidence, limitations, open conflicts, the next role, and the authority that role has.

Our scenario is Harbor County. The utility must prepare a stormwater evidence packet, but its inspection log, map, maintenance record, photographs, and field notes do not agree.

[Show the opening decision.]

The first question is how to organize the work. One general agent may hide how it reached its conclusion. A fixed workflow may not know how to respond to a record conflict. Specialized roles can help, but only if the handoffs are controlled.

[Play the orchestration simulation.]

The orchestrator routes the task. The source librarian finds approved records. The analyst compares them. The critic challenges unsupported statements. The drafter prepares the packet. The human approver accepts, rejects, or returns the work. The shared-state ledger carries the approved task record across those roles.

Watch the evidence funnel. A candidate record does not become proof simply because it contains relevant words. The system needs a locator, the correct asset identity, the applicable time and version, and a person with authority to accept the conclusion.

[Open the repair laboratory.]

Next, break one handoff. Notice how a missing source locator or unresolved conflict can affect several later roles. Select the repair that fixes the original cause. Do not merely rewrite the final packet.

[Show the authority console and contract builder.]

Finally, assign the lowest useful permission to each role and build the orchestration and handoff contract. Name the owner, shared state, required evidence, stop conditions, and prohibited actions.

The takeaway is that orchestration earns trust only when responsibility stays visible. In the next lesson, we will decide when work should be automated, agentic, or left with people.
