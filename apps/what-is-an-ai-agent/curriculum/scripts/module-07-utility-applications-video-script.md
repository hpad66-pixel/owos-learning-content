# Module 7 Video Script: Where Utilities May Become Agentic

Intended length: 7 to 9 minutes

Status: working recording draft

## Spoken words

This lesson asks where bounded agent behavior may help a utility and where another approach is better. We will examine work across maintenance, customer service, water quality, stormwater, capital programs, data stewardship, and regulatory evidence.

[Show the role and domain filters.]

For each opportunity, ask six questions. What value would the user receive? What happens if the system is wrong? Are the sources ready? Can the result be measured? What will the design cost to operate and review? What is the simplest architecture that can perform the job?

A maintenance planning assistant might gather approved records, identify missing information, and draft a work package for review. A customer-service assistant might retrieve approved policies and prepare a response. A stormwater evidence assistant might reconcile records and flag conflicts. Each application has a different consequence and different source requirements.

[Show one rejected opportunity.]

You must also reject at least one idea. Rejection is useful when the data is not ready, the action is too consequential, the outcome cannot be measured, or a fixed workflow can do the job more clearly. A portfolio filled only with approved ideas is not a decision tool.

Your work product is a utility opportunity portfolio. Rank ideas by value, consequence, readiness, cost, and measurability. Record why each idea advances, waits, changes architecture, or stops.

The takeaway is that agentic applications should be selected, not assumed. In the final lesson, you will turn one selected opportunity into a bounded pilot design.
