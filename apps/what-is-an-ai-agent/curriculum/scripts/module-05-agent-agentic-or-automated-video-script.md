# Module 5 Video Script: Agent, Agentic, or Automated?

Intended length: 6 to 8 minutes

Status: working recording draft

## Spoken words

Not every useful AI application needs an agent. This lesson helps you choose the simplest operating structure that can perform the job.

Automation follows defined rules or steps. An agent can select among approved actions while working toward a goal. Agentic describes the amount of delegated decision-making in the overall process. A process can have one agentic step and several fixed steps. It can also require human approval before every consequential action.

[Show the autonomy spectrum.]

Read the spectrum as delegated authority, not as a maturity ladder. More autonomy is not automatically better. A calculation with stable inputs may belong in deterministic software. A document search may need retrieval. A changing investigation may benefit from bounded agent behavior. A regulatory certification still belongs with an authorized person.

[Show the consequence matrix.]

Now compare consequence and reversibility. A low-consequence draft that a person reviews is different from a system changing a governed maintenance record. The second action needs stronger identity, permissions, evidence, testing, monitoring, and approval.

Your work product is an autonomy and consequence decision record. For one proposed use case, state the simplest suitable architecture, the maximum authority, the required human checkpoint, and the reason a more complicated design is not needed.

The takeaway is that architecture should follow the job and its consequence. In the next lesson, we will design the controls that keep the chosen system inside its authority.
