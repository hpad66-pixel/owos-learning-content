The capstone mandate

A pilot is a testable operating contract, not a product wish list.

Instructor explanation

Your design must name the goal, approved evidence, tools, allowed and prohibited actions, task state, evaluation cases, cost limits, stop conditions, accountable owner, and human escalation. If fixed rules or retrieval can do the job, say so.

The first pilot should narrow consequence, begin read-only or recommendation-only, and retain enough evidence to reconstruct what happened.

Ninety-day roadmap

Expand only when evidence earns the change.

Days 1 to 30

Baseline current work. Prepare historical cases. Confirm sources, owner, and read-only boundary.

Days 31 to 60

Run the pilot with human review. Record accuracy, failures, cost, latency, rework, and review effort.

Days 61 to 90

Challenge results. Continue, simplify, revise, or stop. Do not expand from enthusiasm alone.

Evidence and credential boundary
The capstone demonstrates foundational agent fluency and a governed pilot design. It does not certify engineering competence, cybersecurity approval, operational authority, regulatory compliance, procurement approval, or production readiness.
