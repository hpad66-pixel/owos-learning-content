# Tooltip and Plain-Language Standard

## Purpose

Tooltips reduce friction for new learners. They reinforce an explanation but never carry the only explanation a learner needs.

## Writing rule

At first use:

1. say the idea in ordinary language;
2. give the technical term;
3. connect it to the current utility situation; and
4. attach a short tooltip to the term.

Example:

> The system needs to remember which inspection records it has already checked. That current task record is called state.

## Tooltip requirements

- Use the definitions in `GLOSSARY.md` as the controlled starting point.
- Use one tooltip element per lesson.
- Open on mouse hover, keyboard focus, and touch.
- Close on Escape, focus departure, outside touch, or a second activation.
- Keep the tooltip within the visible screen.
- Associate the term and definition for assistive technology.
- Never use a second browser title tooltip.
- Do not place buttons, links, or required instructions inside a tooltip.
- Do not use unexplained technical terms inside the definition.

## Acronyms

Spell out the term at first use, followed by the acronym in parentheses. Later uses may use the acronym, but the tooltip remains available where repetition supports the novice.

## Reading test

A learner must be able to understand the page with every tooltip closed. A tooltip is successful when it answers a quick reminder without interrupting the lesson.

## Component teaching rule

Every major diagram, animation, simulation, assessment set, and work-product interface needs visible instructor explanation outside the tooltip system. The explanation tells the learner what the component is, how to use it, what to watch, why it matters in utility work, and how to interpret the result.
