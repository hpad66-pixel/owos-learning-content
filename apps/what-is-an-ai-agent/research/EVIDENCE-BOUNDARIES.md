# Evidence Boundaries

## What the evidence supports

- Executive explanation of agents acting for users or systems
- Model-directed workflow execution and tool selection as a useful technical boundary
- Distinction between code-directed workflows and dynamically directed agents
- Use of models, tools, instructions, memory, feedback, orchestration, and human escalation
- Multi-agent specialization and handoff patterns
- Need for layered guardrails, access control, evaluation, and human oversight
- Retrieval-augmented generation as a possible agent capability rather than an agent by itself
- Least-functionality, least-privilege, human-approval, and rate-limit controls for excessive agency
- Outcome-based agent evaluation using several grading and review methods
- Water-sector operational technology as a distinct high-consequence environment
- Emerging standards work on agent identity, authorization, auditing, and non-repudiation

## What the evidence does not support

- A universal definition or taxonomy accepted by every vendor and research community
- Safety or effectiveness of an agent in a specific utility environment
- Autonomous control of water or wastewater operations
- Regulatory, legal, cybersecurity, procurement, labor, or professional approval
- Accuracy of McKinsey's economic figures without review of the underlying studies
- Transfer of enterprise examples or productivity results to utilities
- Reproduction rights for McKinsey text, branding, or exhibits
- A claim that the emerging NIST agent identity initiative is a completed technical standard
- A claim that general information-technology guardrails are sufficient for operational technology

## Licensing and permitted use

The uploaded McKinsey PDF remains a private research source. Internal extraction and page-level analysis are complete. Public course language, diagrams, simulations, and examples must be original and must cite permitted sources. McKinsey exhibits will not be reproduced. Any short quotation requires a specific instructional reason, source attribution, and release review.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and NIST materials may be cited as public primary guidance subject to their published terms. Their definitions are evidence, not OWOS endorsements or a substitute for utility practice review.

## Utility safety boundary

The foundational course teaches advisory, analytical, knowledge, and coordination applications. It does not authorize autonomous treatment, pumping, dosing, safety-system, emergency-response, permit-certification, procurement, or financial decisions. Humans retain named authority, and deployed systems require environment-specific engineering, cybersecurity, legal, regulatory, privacy, labor, and operational review.
