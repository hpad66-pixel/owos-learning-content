# SRC-001 Page Notes

Source: McKinsey & Company, `What is an AI agent?`, March 2025  
Original: `research/originals/what-is-an-ai-agent.pdf`  
Coverage: 11 of 11 PDF pages reviewed  
Use: private research and curriculum planning only

## Page 1

Cover and broad description. The subtitle presents agents as tools for interacting with artificial intelligence and performing complex tasks. This language is accessible but broader than the technical definitions needed for the course.

## Page 2

Defines an agent as software acting for a user or system. Introduces orchestration, multiple agents, planning, collaboration, memory, and movement from knowledge toward action. Begins a five-part business taxonomy with individual augmentation agents.

Course use: establish the executive-level idea and show why a chatbot may be agent-like without necessarily satisfying a stronger technical definition.

## Page 3

Completes the business taxonomy: workflow automation, domain solutions, enterprise operating models, and virtual workers. Begins the operating explanation. Agents may use human-facing tools such as browsers and machine-facing tools such as application programming interfaces.

Course use: tool use and business patterns. Limitation: several categories describe platforms or operating models rather than mutually exclusive agent types.

## Page 4

Presents a four-stage flow: user task, planning and allocation, specialist execution, user review, refinement, and action. The exhibit shows a manager agent coordinating analyst, checker, and planner agents while connecting to internal and external systems.

Course use: a starting orchestration model. Rebuild as an original utility simulation rather than reproducing the exhibit.

## Page 5

Discusses feedback loops, creator and critic roles, human escalation, model selection, large language models, business value, and software modernization. Notes that not every agent uses a large language model for every decision.

Course use: critic pattern, model selection, and the distinction between an agent and its underlying model. Economic estimates and productivity figures require review of their original cited research before public use.

## Page 6

Explains three proposed advantages: handling less predictable situations, natural-language direction, and use of existing tools. Introduces emergent behavior and reports Lenovo productivity examples.

Course use: contrast deterministic automation with model-directed execution. Limitation: reported company results and emergent-behavior language should not become utility claims without independent evidence.

## Page 7

Provides hypothetical enterprise use cases and identifies trust, hallucination checking, human-centered governance, organizational change, security, operations, and data protection as adoption concerns.

Course use: guardrails, review agents, human handoff, and trust. The examples are not water-sector evidence.

## Page 8

Describes possible movement toward multi-agent technology architectures and three enablement patterns: third-party platforms, wrappers around enterprise services, and custom agents using fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented generation.

Course use: explain that retrieval-augmented generation can supply context to an agent but is not itself an agent. Explain wrappers as controlled interfaces around utility systems.

## Page 9

Completes the custom-agent example and presents an architecture exhibit. Begins implementation guidance: question large proposals, focus on consequential problems, and anticipate talent and operating-model changes.

Course use: architecture patterns and implementation judgment. Avoid repeating claims that large projects are always the best starting point. Utility pilots must match consequence, evidence, and operational readiness.

## Page 10

Concludes the implementation guidance and lists referenced McKinsey articles.

Course use: bibliography routing. The cited underlying articles must be reviewed separately before their figures or conclusions are used.

## Page 11

Author and expert information plus copyright notice.

Course use: attribution and licensing boundary only.

## Extraction assessment

The text layer is complete and readable. Both exhibits were visually inspected. The source supports an executive overview of agent concepts, orchestration, tool use, feedback, architecture patterns, and adoption concerns. It does not provide a water-utility taxonomy, technical implementation specification, safety case, evaluation method, or evidence for autonomous operational control.
