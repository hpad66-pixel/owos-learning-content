# Foreword | Why I wrote this

Data governance has to leave the conference room and enter the work.

For 30 years, I have worked where public responsibility meets the physical reality of water. I have seen the work from the regulatory side and from the operator's chair. From both seats, the same truth keeps returning: a utility can own excellent systems and still struggle to explain which answer is authoritative, who accepted it and what evidence stands behind the decision.

That is why I wrote this paper. I did not want to add another abstract definition of data governance. I wanted to make the work operational. If we use the word One in One Water, we should be able to show what One means across control systems, assets, laboratories, models, customers, regulation and the decisions that connect them. If we say artificial intelligence (AI) governance or data governance, we should be able to name the person, the control, the evidence and the outcome.

> **The point**
>
> A transaction closes a task. A transformation changes the way a utility decides, operates and learns. Our work should create transformational systems, not a larger collection of disconnected transactions.

AI will change utility work. The harder question is whether utilities will prepare their data before placing more decisions on top of it. If the data cannot be found, joined, understood, traced and challenged, AI will not create transformation. It will automate uncertainty and make weak decisions move faster.

In critical infrastructure, this is not a minor technology problem. Data shapes service, safety, compliance, capital, customer and emergency decisions. When a serious incident occurs, people will ask what information was used, where it came from, who approved it and what warning was missed. The utility should be able to answer before the incident, not after it.

This paper joins the world of standards, provenance, controls, quality, privacy, cybersecurity and AI to the physical and public work of a utility. A standard becomes useful when it changes a handoff, a decision, a control or a result. Your data is part of your utility. Govern it with the same seriousness as the assets, money and public trust that depend on it.

# Executive summary

Utilities do not need another governance slogan. They need an operating system for accountable data decisions.

The central finding is direct: a utility becomes AI-ready by becoming data-ready first. The work is not a technology cleanup exercise. It is the operating discipline that makes consequential decisions explainable, testable and improvable.


## Three conclusions

1. No single standard completes the job. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) supplies governance direction. The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) supplies portable provenance and data descriptions. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) supply control, risk and assurance methods. Law, regulation and utility context determine what must operate.
2. The utility is one decision system. Control, operational history, mapping, modeling, maintenance, laboratory, meter, customer, finance, regulatory and AI data must connect through shared identity, meaning, ownership, lineage, quality and rights.
3. A score without evidence is an opinion. Maturity, evidence confidence, consequence and urgency must remain visible. Progress is demonstrated by recurring operation, measured results, verified closure and better decisions over time.


## What a utility should be able to prove

- Which source was authoritative for the material decision and why it was fit for that use.
- How identity, time, units, location, version and quality survived each system handoff.
- Who owned the data, who could change it and who accepted any exception or residual risk.
- How the answer moved from physical source through transformation, model or spreadsheet to action and outcome.
- What a regulator, auditor, operator, customer or incident team can review later.

> **Executive decision**
>
> Treat data readiness as utility readiness. Fund it as an operating capability tied to service, safety, resilience, affordability, compliance and capital delivery. Do not leave it as a technology cleanup project.


## The application in one sentence

Build a governed evidence chain from the physical source to the public outcome, use it to locate the current maturity and twin phase, then improve the highest-consequence decisions first.

# 1. Why utility data governance matters now

The issue is not whether a utility has data. The issue is whether it can use the data with accountability.

Most utilities already own the source systems. They have supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA), operational historians, a geographic information system (GIS), advanced metering infrastructure (AMI), customer and billing platforms, a computerized maintenance management system (CMMS) or enterprise asset management (EAM) platform, a laboratory information management system (LIMS), finance tools and many local spreadsheets. The weakness sits above and between them. The organization often cannot answer, quickly and consistently, what data exists, what it means, how it connects, who owns it and whether it is safe to use for a specific decision.

> **More data is not better data**
>
> The volume of data created, captured, copied and consumed globally was estimated at 64.2 zettabytes in 2020, while a pre-2025 European Commission forecast placed 2025 at 175 zettabytes, nearly three times as much in five years. One zettabyte equals 1,000 exabytes, 1 million petabytes or 1 billion terabytes; the correct term is petabyte, not penderbyte. Yet quantity is not quality: without identity, context, ownership, lineage, controls and fitness for purpose, more data creates more noise, duplication and risk rather than better decisions. [DG01] [DG02]

![Figure 1. The missing layer above utility systems](figures/01_missing_layer.png)

**Figure 1. The missing layer above utility systems**

**What this shows.** Utilities usually have the source systems. The gap is the governed layer that identifies data, states what it means, names who is accountable, records how it changed, and tells a user whether it is fit for a decision.

## Governance is the way a decision becomes accountable

A policy is part of governance, but it is not the whole of governance. Governance is visible when a utility can point to a decision right, a named owner, an operating control, a retained record and a way to correct failure. If any one of those parts is missing, the utility has a statement of intent, not a dependable capability.

> **A practical test**
>
> Can a qualified new team member find, access, join and understand the data needed for a material decision without relying on one person who knows where everything is hidden? If the answer is no, the organization has knowledge risk and data risk at the same time.


## The utility consequences

| Decision area | What weak governance looks like | What good governance changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations | Alarm, sensor or network data is used without time, unit, calibration or source context. | The operator sees source, quality state, freshness, exception and the approved rule. |
| Assets and capital | GIS, drawings, EAM and field records disagree about the same asset. | A governed identifier and version process connects the physical asset to the work and decision. |
| Customer | Interval use, estimates, corrections and eligibility decisions cannot be reconstructed. | Purpose, source, estimation, approval, notice and correction evidence travel with the output. |
| Regulatory | A reported number is rebuilt through spreadsheets and personal knowledge. | The filing traces to authoritative data, transformations, reconciliations and sign-off. |
| AI and analytics | A model is deployed before the data is shown to be suitable and representative. | The use case has named accountability, data provenance, tests, limits, monitoring and retirement rules. |


## The risk of moving faster on weak foundations

AI can reduce effort, improve pattern detection and help people ask better questions. It can also create a faster mess. When a model draws from data that is late, incomplete, misjoined, mislabeled or outside its approved purpose, the error can travel farther than the original record ever could.

A utility should expect questions after any material failure: Which version was used? Which source was authoritative? Was the data fit for this use? Who approved the exception? What changed? Was the result challenged? The governance program should be designed to answer those questions as part of normal work.

# 2. The terms that matter

Clear terms prevent weak controls from hiding behind broad language.

![Figure 2. Lineage, provenance and integrity answer different questions](figures/02_three_questions.png)

**Figure 2. Lineage, provenance and integrity answer different questions**

**What this shows.** Lineage shows the route. Provenance explains the history and responsibility. Integrity provides evidence that the data and its history were not altered outside the approved process.

*Source: APAS AI synthesis of the W3C Provenance Data Model (PROV) and NIST control concepts.*


## Plain-language definitions

| Term | Plain meaning | Utility example | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data governance | The decisions, responsibilities, controls and proof used to direct data work. | The utility decides which network model is authoritative and who can approve a change. | Charter, decision, owner, control result and review. |
| Data management | The work that creates, stores, protects, moves, improves and disposes of data. | A data team validates AMI files, resolves exceptions and publishes a governed product. | Run record, rule result, issue, version and service level. |
| Lineage | The route from source through transformation to use. | A regulatory leakage value traces to sensors, work records, mappings and calculations. | Source, job, input, output, version, time and consumer. |
| Provenance | The fuller history of the data, the activity and the responsible actor. | A laboratory result records sample, method, instrument, analyst, approval and amendment. | Entity, activity, agent, purpose, time, version and relationship. |
| Integrity | Confidence that data and evidence remain complete and protected from improper change. | A control detects an unauthorized change to an approved reporting dataset. | Authorization, validation, audit log, hash or signature, recovery test. |
| Data quality | Fitness for a stated use, not a claim that data is perfect. | Fifteen-minute meter data may fit a customer trend but not a second-by-second control decision. | Use, rule, threshold, result, exception and accepted limit. |
| Traceability | The ability to follow a requirement, record or decision backward and forward. | A customer complaint connects to the bill, estimate, meter event, correction and notice. | Linked identifiers and dated records across the chain. |
| Data product | A governed dataset, application programming interface (API) or feature set with an owner and service promise. | A network-risk feature set used by capital planning and field operations. | Contract, owner, schema, quality, lineage, rights and lifecycle. |


## The minimum provenance event

A useful provenance record does not need to be complicated. For a critical transformation, retain the input identifiers, output identifier, activity or run identifier, responsible person or service, time, code or configuration version, quality result, purpose, approval and integrity evidence. The W3C Provenance Data Model (PROV) provides the entity, activity and agent pattern for this work. OpenLineage provides a practical job, run and dataset pattern. [W01] [W02] [U06]


## A utility-specific rule for quality

> **Fitness comes before score**
>
> Do not ask whether a dataset is good. Ask whether it is fit for this decision, at this time, with this consequence. Record the answer and the accepted limits.

Quality is contextual. A location that is adequate for customer communication may be unsafe for excavation. A meter interval that is adequate for billing analysis may not be adequate for operational control. A governance program should tie each quality requirement to the use, criticality and consequence. [I05] [I06] [I10] [W04]

# 3. How the standards fit together

Use the standards as one control system. Do not force one source to answer every question.

![Figure 3. No single standard does the whole job](figures/03_authority_stack.png)

**Figure 3. No single standard does the whole job**

**What this shows.** Law and policy set obligations. ISO sets governance and management direction. W3C provides machine-readable evidence models. NIST and IEC provide control and assurance methods. Industry frameworks help organize capability.

*Source: APAS AI standards crosswalk.*


## ISO gives the governing and management structure

ISO/IEC 38505-1 sets principles for the governance of data. The ISO/IEC 5259 series brings data quality into analytics and machine learning (ML), including measures, management, process and governance. ISO/IEC 11179 supports metadata registries and dataset registration. ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894 address AI management and risk. ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701 and ISO 15489 connect security, privacy, personally identifiable information (PII) and records. ISO 55001 and ISO 24516 connect the data work to assets and water utility performance. [I01] [I04-I08] [I14] [I16-I18] [I20-I26]


## W3C makes the evidence portable

W3C PROV models provenance through entities, activities and agents. The Data Catalog Vocabulary (DCAT) describes catalogs and datasets. The Data Quality Vocabulary (DQV) expresses quality. The Shapes Constraint Language (SHACL) tests graph constraints. The Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL) expresses rights and duties. Verifiable Credentials Data Integrity adds cryptographic proof patterns. The Resource Description Framework (RDF), Simple Knowledge Organization System (SKOS) and JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data (JSON-LD) support identifiers, relationships and shared meaning. These are technical building blocks for evidence that can move between systems. [W01-W11]


## NIST organizes risk, controls and assurance

NIST does not publish one formal standard called data governance. Its contribution is spread across the Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF), the Research Data Framework, the provenance glossary, Special Publication (SP) 800-53 controls, SP 800-53A assessment procedures, the Cybersecurity Framework, the Privacy Framework, supply-chain guidance and the Open Security Controls Assessment Language (OSCAL). Together, these sources help a utility define risk, select controls, test them and exchange assurance information. [N01-N10]

# 4. The utility operating model

Govern the physical decision chain, not only the information technology estate.

Information technology (IT) manages business and information services. OT monitors or controls the physical process. Utility governance must connect both because a material decision often begins in physical equipment and ends in a business, customer or regulatory record.

![Figure 5. The governed data lifecycle](figures/05_lifecycle.png)

**Figure 5. The governed data lifecycle**

**What this shows.** Governance must stay with the data from creation through use and disposal. Every handoff needs identity, responsibility, quality, change, access, purpose and retention evidence.

*Source: APAS AI synthesis of ISO, W3C, NIST and records-management sources.*


## The lifecycle evidence rule

At each lifecycle stage, the utility should retain enough evidence to answer seven questions: What is it? Who is accountable? What changed? Is it fit for this use? Who can access it? Why is it being used? How long should it remain? The answers do not have to live in one tool, but they must connect through stable identifiers.

![Figure 6. The utility data estate is one connected system](figures/06_utility_estate.png)

**Figure 6. The utility data estate is one connected system**

**What this shows.** Operational, asset, laboratory, customer, work, regulatory and AI data depend on common identifiers and controlled handoffs. Governing only the enterprise warehouse leaves the most consequential utility decisions outside the program.

*Source: APAS AI utility operating model.*


## The connective tissue is shared identity

The systems do not need to become one platform. They do need to agree on the identity of the asset, event, obligation and decision. Stable identifiers allow a person to move from a physical condition to the supporting record without rebuilding the story by hand.

- An asset identifier connects the geographic information system, maintenance record, telemetry tag and engineering model to the same physical thing.
- Event identifiers and trusted time connect an alarm, sample, customer impact, work response and operating outcome.
- Rule and obligation identifiers connect a permit limit or operating rule to the calculation, approval, filing and corrective action it governs.

![Figure 14. One utility question crosses the entire data estate](figures/14_utility_question.png)

**Figure 14. One utility question crosses the entire data estate**

**What this shows.** An overflow-risk, water-quality, compliance or capital question rarely belongs to one system. It depends on controlled connections among SCADA, the historian, GIS, hydraulic models, CMMS or EAM, LIMS, customer systems, weather and regulatory records.

*Source: APAS AI utility decision-chain model.*


## Four examples of fragmented data becoming an accountable outcome

| Utility decision | Systems that must connect | Governed outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wet-weather overflow response | SCADA levels and alarms; historian time series; rainfall; GIS collection model; CMMS pump condition and work; fats, oils and grease (FOG) inspections; permit and consent requirements. | A cited risk signal, operator decision, response record and regulatory evidence chain. |
| Drinking-water quality release | LIMS sample and method; instrument calibration and maintenance; SCADA treatment conditions; historian trends; laboratory approval; distribution model; regulatory limits. | A defensible release or escalation decision with custody, method, condition, approval and exception evidence. |
| Capital renewal priority | GIS asset identity; CMMS or EAM failure and work history; hydraulic or process model; AMI demand; customer impact; climate stress; cost and regulatory commitments. | A ranked investment decision that can be traced from physical condition and modeled consequence to funding. |
| Customer leak or billing correction | AMI interval data; meter events; customer information system (CIS) account and billing; GIS service location; field work order; estimation rule; notice and correction history. | A fair, reconstructable customer outcome with purpose, source, estimate, review and redress. |

> **The fragmentation tax**
>
> When these systems do not share identifiers, time, meaning and evidence, staff rebuild the truth by hand. The delay is expensive; the inconsistency is a governance risk; and the resulting AI learns the fragmentation rather than resolving it.

## Use a material decision as the unit of proof

The fastest way to test governance is to follow one consequential output. Select a regulatory filing, an operational alert, a customer outcome, a capital recommendation or an AI-supported decision. Trace it backward through every transformation to the authoritative source. Then trace it forward to the action, outcome, issue and correction.

## The scoring method

A key performance indicator (KPI) shows whether a service, control or product is producing the intended result. The assessment keeps those outcome measures separate from the maturity score so activity is not mistaken for value.

| Measure | Scale | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maturity | 0 to 5 | Record the level of repeatable operating practice. |
| Evidence confidence | 0 to 4 | 0 absent, 1 asserted, 2 documented, 3 operating sample, 4 independently verified. |
| Adjusted score | 0 to 5 | Maturity multiplied by evidence confidence divided by 4. Exclude not-applicable criteria. |
| Consequence | 1 to 5 | Rate service, safety, customer, regulatory, financial and trust impact. |
| Urgency | 1 to 5 | Rate deadline, incident, audit, project and dependency pressure. |
| Priority | Relative | Combine the target gap, consequence, exposure and urgency. Keep the factors visible. |

> **Score protection**
>
> A criterion cannot score above Level 2 without recurring operating evidence. Independent assurance raises confidence in the proof. It does not raise maturity when the underlying practice is weak.

# 6. From finding to funded roadmap

An assessment is useful only when it changes ownership, sequence and delivery.


## The consulting method

1. Frame the decisions. Confirm service outcomes, material risks, regulatory commitments, projects and AI use cases that make the work necessary.
2. Map the estate. Identify systems, interfaces, physical assets, critical data, owners, suppliers, jurisdictions and current programs.
3. Test the evidence. Review documents, interview by seat and trace selected decisions from source to outcome.
4. Score and challenge. Record maturity, evidence confidence, consequence, urgency, legal applicability and competing views.
5. Design the target. Set decision rights, standards, products, controls, platforms, roles, metrics and assurance.
6. Build the roadmap. Name an owner, action, dependency, cost range, decision date and closure evidence for every material gap.
7. Implement and verify. Put controls into the work, measure adoption, retest failures and report residual risk.


## The roadmap must distinguish transaction from transformation

| Transactional output | Transformational outcome |
| --- | --- |
| A catalog tool is purchased. | Critical data is owned, described, connected to decisions and kept current. |
| A governance policy is approved. | People know their decision rights and use them at real handoffs. |
| A lineage diagram is produced. | Critical pipelines emit usable lineage and support impact analysis and incident review. |
| A quality dashboard is launched. | Quality requirements are tied to use, owners correct causes and consumers see limits. |
| An AI review form is added. | Use cases have data provenance, suitability tests, human accountability, monitoring and stop rules. |
| An audit finding is closed. | The cause is corrected, recurrence is tested and the operating evidence remains available. |


## The first roadmap horizons

| Horizon | Management question | Typical work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 90 days | Where is the immediate decision and evidence risk? | Name owners, select critical decisions, inventory AI uses, control high-risk spreadsheets and trace three to five evidence chains. |
| 3 to 12 months | What operating capability must become repeatable? | Metadata profile, identifiers, data products, stewardship, quality rules, lineage capture, supplier clauses and assurance plan. |
| 12 to 24 months | What should become integrated and measured? | Cross-domain model, automated signals, product portfolio, adoption metrics, regulatory packs and continuous control testing. |
| 24 months and beyond | What can the utility safely scale? | Trusted AI, digital twins, sector data sharing, automated evidence and adaptive governance. |

> **Roadmap rule**
>
> Do not create one large data governance project. Build a governed pattern around a material decision, prove the value, then repeat the pattern across the utility.

NEOM and TONOMUS provide public signals around consent-based data, digital infrastructure, private cloud, local requirements and privacy. > **Case rule**

## The repeatable operating loop

![Figure 20. Operationalize one decision chain, then repeat](figures/20_operational_loop.png)

**Figure 20. Operationalize one decision chain, then repeat**

**What this shows.** The operating loop starts with a consequential decision, connects its evidence, puts controls into normal work, releases a governed product, monitors use and outcomes, and turns what is learned into the next improvement cycle.

*Source: APAS AI implementation method.*

1. Select one consequential decision. State the user, service outcome, consequence and current pain in plain English.
2. Map the evidence chain. Connect the physical source, system record, transformation, model, rule, person, action and outcome.
3. Define the data contract. Name the owner, identifiers, meaning, units, quality rules, approved use, access, freshness, retention and change process.
4. Put controls into normal work. Automate checks where practical and keep a clear human decision where judgment or public accountability is required.
5. Release the governed product. Show source, time, quality, limits and lineage to the user instead of hiding them in technical logs.
6. Measure adoption and outcome. Confirm that people use the product and that service, safety, compliance, affordability or delivery improves.
7. Retest the decision chain. Sample real cases, verify closure, record residual risk and decide what must change.
8. Reuse the pattern. Carry the same records, controls and evidence methods into the next Pump OS, Pipe OS or Reg OS use case.

| Purpose | Can a utility user explain the decision this feature improves and the harm it helps prevent? | Named user, decision statement, outcome, consequence and product owner. |
| Data | Can the team explain every important input in simple English and connect it to an approved source? | Data contract, identifier map, source register, quality rules, rights and lineage. |
| Control | Does the feature detect bad, missing, late, changed or unauthorized data before it misleads a user? | Control design, owner, test results, exceptions, alerts and recovery procedure. |
| Decision | Is the recommendation separate from the person or rule that has authority to act? | Decision rights, human review, approval record, override reason and escalation path. |
| Evidence | Could the utility reconstruct the answer during an incident, audit or regulatory challenge? | Retained source, transformation, model or rule version, user action and integrity record. |
| Outcome | Does the team know whether the feature is used and whether it improves the utility result? | Adoption measure, outcome measure, baseline, target, review cycle and product decision. |


## Definition of done

- A utility user can understand the feature, its data and its limits without a data-science translator.
- The same asset, event, rule and decision identifiers work across the three products where the records overlap.
- Every important answer displays its source, time, quality state and approved purpose.
- The responsible, accountable, consulted and informed (RACI) roles are named for product ownership, data ownership, control operation, approval and support.
- A failed control creates an owned action, a due date, closure proof and a retest.
- A release has adoption and utility-outcome measures, not only technical completion measures.
- Support, incident response, data correction, model rollback, supplier exit and record retention are tested before scale.

> **The usefulness test**
>
> If a page, score, model or feature does not help a named utility person make, explain, control or improve a real decision, it is not finished. Operational usefulness is the release standard.

# 11. A practical 90-day start

Begin with consequential decisions, visible ownership and proof that can be reviewed.


## Days 1 to 30 | Frame and select

- Appoint an executive sponsor and a working owner with authority across operations, data, security, legal and AI.
- Select three to five material decisions: one operational, one regulatory, one customer, one capital and one AI or analytics use case where applicable.
- Create the first system, data, interface and owner map for those decisions.
- Inventory current AI use cases and suppliers. Do not limit the inventory to centrally approved tools.
- Record immediate legal, regulatory, contract and records obligations for the selected scope.


<div style="page-break-after: always;"></div>


## Days 31 to 60 | Trace and test

- Trace each selected decision from source to outcome and identify manual steps, spreadsheets, estimates and unowned changes.
- Test identity, meaning, quality, lineage, access, retention and approval evidence at each handoff.
- Name an owner and steward for each critical data product or domain in scope.
- Define the quality requirements by use and consequence, then run the first recurring measures.
- Review supplier data rights, training-use restrictions, locations, assurance and exit terms.


## Days 61 to 90 | Decide and fund

- Score the selected scope using maturity and evidence confidence. Challenge unsupported ratings.
- Separate immediate control fixes from operating-model changes and platform work.
- Approve a 12-month roadmap with owners, dependencies, funding, decision dates and closure evidence.
- Publish a small executive dashboard: critical decisions covered, evidence gaps, open high-consequence findings, issue age and control results.
- Schedule the next evidence review and select the next set of decisions to bring into the pattern.


## Questions for the executive team

1. Which utility decisions would cause the most harm if the underlying data were wrong, late or used outside its purpose?
2. Can we trace those decisions to authoritative sources and reconstruct what happened?
3. Which person owns the fitness of each critical dataset, not only the system that stores it?
4. Where are manual transformations and local spreadsheets part of the official decision chain?
5. Which AI uses could affect service, safety, customers, employees, capital or regulatory outcomes?
6. What evidence would we need if a regulator, auditor, customer or incident team challenged the decision tomorrow?
7. Which actions will change the operating system of the utility, and which actions merely close a task?


## The 90-day deliverables

| Deliverable | Management use |
| --- | --- |
| Decision and data map | Shows critical sources, handoffs, owners, suppliers and evidence gaps. |
| AI and analytics inventory | Shows use, consequence, owner, data source, human role and review state. |
| Evidence-backed scorecard | Separates maturity from evidence confidence and makes disagreement visible. |
| Immediate control plan | Names high-consequence fixes, owners, dates and closure proof. |
| Twelve-month roadmap | Sequences operating model, data products, controls, platforms, adoption and assurance. |
| Executive risk decision | Records what is funded, accepted, stopped, transferred or scheduled for more work. |

# Closing | The work is to make data usable and accountable

Transformation begins when a utility can explain the decision before it has to defend the incident.

The opportunity in front of utilities is real. AI can help people see patterns, reach information and act with greater speed. The opportunity will not be realized by placing AI on top of data that the organization cannot explain.

Data governance is how a utility turns responsibility into a repeatable system. It gives the operator context, the engineer traceability, the customer a fair process, the executive a clear risk decision, the regulator evidence and the AI system a controlled foundation.

This is not a one-time project. Standards will change. Laws will change. Systems, suppliers, people and risks will change. The living document, the assessment and the application must change with them. The constant is the discipline: write the decision, act through the control, keep the proof and close the gap.

> **The commitment**
>
> We will keep trying to build transformational solutions because the public work deserves more than disconnected transactions. Data-ready before AI-ready. Evidence before assurance. Accountability before scale.

# Appendix D | Validation protocol, worked example and release gates

Use the method without allowing a publication score to become an unsupported product, benchmark or assurance claim.

![Figure 21. Four release gates prevent one score from making four different claims](figures/21_release_gates.png)

**Figure 21. Four release gates prevent one score from making four different claims**

**What this shows.** Publication readiness, assessment-method validation, application security and operational readiness, and independent assurance are separate decisions. Passing one gate does not pass the others.

*Source: APAS AI release-governance model.*


## Four separate release decisions

| Gate | Decision | Minimum evidence | Accountable approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publication readiness | May the paper be published with its stated scope and limits? | Verified sources, complete references, resolved production defects, accessibility review and change record. | Publication owner and editorial reviewer. |
| Assessment-method validation | May scores be compared, benchmarked or used without facilitation? | Diverse pilots, scoring calibration, inter-rater reliability, sensitivity tests, utility overlays and worked examples. | Method owner with independent technical challenge. |
| Application readiness | May the product accept utility evidence or connect to utility systems? | Threat model, privacy assessment, records and residency design, supplier controls, safe integration boundary, incident plan and user testing. | Product, security, privacy, records and utility-operations owners. |
| Independent assurance | May APAS issue certification, compliance or assurance conclusions? | Defined criteria, competence, independence, sampling, evidence retention, quality controls and external review authority. | Qualified independent assurance authority. |

> **Release rule**
>
> A pass at the publication gate does not pass the method, application or assurance gates. No average score can override a failed safety, privacy, legal, evidence or independence condition.


## Utility-type overlays

The 60 core criteria remain stable, but the critical data, consequence and required evidence must change by utility context. The overlay does not create a separate governance system. It changes the decision samples, operating risks and evidence depth used to test the common system.

| Overlay | Priority decisions | Critical data and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drinking water | Treatment release, pressure, water quality, source protection and customer notice. | SCADA and historian context; laboratory custody and method; distribution model; asset condition; permit limits; operator authorization. |
| Wastewater and stormwater | Overflow response, wet-weather operation, collection risk, treatment performance and discharge reporting. | Rainfall; levels and flows; pump state; sewer model; blockage and FOG work; event timeline; consent or permit evidence. |
| Electric | Switching, outage restoration, load and voltage control, asset health and reliability reporting. | Network model; telemetry; protection settings; outage and work records; asset hierarchy; NERC applicability; operator and control evidence. |
| Multi-utility | Cross-service customer, emergency, capital, cyber and shared-infrastructure decisions. | Common asset and location identity; service boundaries; shared suppliers; access segregation; coordinated incident and records evidence. |


## Worked assessment example with missing and conflicting evidence

Example decision: a wet-weather overflow response. The assessment begins with an asserted maturity of Level 4 because a dashboard, procedure and monthly review exist. The evidence sample shows that one SCADA tag lacks a governed unit, rainfall time is offset by one hour, the spreadsheet transformation has no version record, and operator approval is not linked to the regulatory response.

| Measure | Initial answer | Evidence result | Recorded outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maturity | 4, measured | The process operates, but critical handoffs are not controlled. | 2, defined. A score cannot exceed Level 2 until recurring evidence supports operation. |
| Evidence confidence | 3, operating sample | Two sources verified, two partial, one missing and one conflicting. | 2, documented. Conflict and missing approval remain visible. |
| Consequence | 5 | Potential service, environmental, regulatory and public-trust harm. | 5. Consequence is not reduced by a lower maturity score. |
| Urgency | 4 | Wet-weather season begins in 45 days. | 4. Fund immediate control correction and a retest. |
| Finding | None | Authoritative time, transformation version and approval evidence are incomplete. | High priority. Owner, action, date and closure proof required. |

> **Missing-evidence rule**
>
> Absence is recorded as absence. It is not converted into a neutral answer. Conflicting evidence lowers confidence, triggers adjudication and remains linked to the score until resolved.


## Scoring sensitivity test

Sensitivity testing asks whether a reasonable change in weights or evidence confidence changes the roadmap. If a priority disappears only because one weight changed, the recommendation is unstable and must be challenged before funding.

| Scenario | Maturity weight | Evidence weight | Result | Management interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 50% | 50% | Overflow evidence chain is Priority 1. | Act now. |
| Practice-heavy | 70% | 30% | Overflow evidence chain remains Priority 1. | Priority is stable. |
| Evidence-heavy | 30% | 70% | Overflow evidence chain remains Priority 1 with a larger gap. | Weak proof increases urgency. |
| Critical-failure protection | Not averaged | Not averaged | Missing operator approval forces escalation. | A critical failure cannot be hidden by strong scores elsewhere. |


## Minimum data contract and provenance event

| Record | Required fields | Control purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data product contract | Product ID, purpose, owner, consumers, schema, units, source, quality rules, access, rights, lineage, service level, retention and version. | Makes the approved use and service promise explicit. |
| Provenance event | Event ID, input IDs, output ID, activity, responsible agent, time, code or configuration version, purpose, quality result, approval and integrity evidence. | Reconstructs what happened, when, why and by whom. |
| Control result | Control ID, design owner, test time, sample, expected result, actual result, exception, reviewer and retained evidence location. | Shows whether the control operated and what failed. |
| Assessment finding | Finding ID, criterion, sampled decision, evidence state, cause, consequence, owner, action, due date, closure proof and retest result. | Keeps the score connected to accountable improvement. |


## Calibration and assessor-reliability protocol

1. Select at least three utilities that differ in size, ownership, service type and regulatory setting.
2. Choose the same minimum decision samples and record where an overlay changes the evidence requirement.
3. Have at least two qualified assessors score the same evidence independently before discussion.
4. Compare criterion agreement, domain agreement, evidence-confidence agreement and finding severity.
5. Adjudicate disagreements using the retained evidence and scoring rules, then update examples without hiding the original result.
6. Run weight and missing-evidence sensitivity tests and publish any change to priority or roadmap sequence.
7. Approve a benchmark only after the sample, method, limitations and uncertainty are visible and repeatable.


## Conflict, correction and portability rules

- Disclose when the assessor may sell implementation work arising from the assessment.
- Allow the utility to challenge a score and retain the evidence, reasoning, decision and resolution.
- Keep client evidence portable through exportable records and stable identifiers.
- Publish material corrections, affected criteria, reason, approver and effective date.
- Retain prior versions so a result can be reconstructed under the method used at the time.
