# Data Ready Before AI Ready, Version 2.3 learning map

## Audience

Utility executives, operators, engineers, data professionals, regulators, technology teams and consulting partners.

## Learning outcomes

After completing the learning package, a participant should be able to:

1. Explain data governance, lineage, provenance, integrity and fitness for purpose in plain English.
2. Connect supervisory control and data acquisition, geographic information systems, maintenance, laboratory, customer, regulatory and artificial intelligence data to an accountable utility decision.
3. Distinguish publication readiness from method validation, application readiness and independent assurance.
4. Use the twelve-domain, sixty-criterion assessment method without allowing an average score to hide missing evidence or a critical failure.
5. Design a ninety-day start and convert findings into owned, funded and verifiable improvement work.

## Suggested modules

| Module | Publication sections | Applied activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Why governance matters | Foreword, executive summary and Chapter 1 | Select one consequential utility decision and name the harm if its data is wrong. |
| 2. The governed evidence chain | Chapters 2 through 4 | Trace a decision from outcome to authoritative source across utility systems. |
| 3. Assessment and maturity | Chapter 5 and Appendix A | Score a sample decision while recording maturity and evidence confidence separately. |
| 4. Roadmap and operating change | Chapters 6 and 11 | Build a ninety-day action plan with owners, dependencies and closure evidence. |
| 5. Law, policy and public evidence | Chapters 7 and 8 | Create a jurisdiction applicability record and distinguish public signals from maturity proof. |
| 6. Product and continuous improvement | Chapters 9 and 10 | Map governance records into Pump OS, Pipe OS or Reg OS without duplicating the source of truth. |
| 7. Validation and release | Appendix D | Apply the four release gates and challenge missing or conflicting evidence. |

## Assessment approach

Use scenario questions, evidence-chain exercises, a facilitated criterion sample and a final release-gate decision. Completion is a learning record, not certification, legal advice, a utility benchmark or an independent assurance opinion.
