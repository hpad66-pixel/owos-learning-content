A contractor wants to count inactive pumps. The export contains asset_id, status, and updated_dt. There is no definition, unit, source owner, valid-time rule, or change history.

1 | What metadata actually does

Metadata turns a value into usable evidence

Metadata is information about information. That definition is accurate, but it is too thin for utility work. Operational metadata should let a person answer practical questions: What is this value? Which real thing does it describe? Who is allowed to define it? Where did it come from? When was it true? Which unit and code list apply? Who may use it, and for what decision?

A data catalog is where those answers can be found and connected. A catalog entry is not trustworthy merely because it exists. It becomes useful when its claims point to approved definitions, actual schemas, measured coverage, named owners, change events, and consumer notices.

Keep three jobs separate. A glossary defines business language. A catalog locates and describes data assets. A semantic model connects concepts and relationships so systems can interpret the same meaning consistently. One tool may support all three jobs, but the jobs remain different.

A business term such as “available pump” should not be mapped casually to every field named status. The mapping must state which source field implements the term, which transformation is allowed, which code list is authoritative, and where the mapping does not apply.

Worked Riverbend example

Reconstruct one disputed field

Riverbend’s reliability dashboard reports 47 unavailable pumps. Priya, the steward, opens the catalog and finds three candidate fields. She does not begin by choosing the cleanest table. She begins with the decision: “Which installed pumps could not perform their assigned service at 06:00 on July 17?” That wording supplies the entity, capability, time, and use.

EAM asset statusDescribes administrative lifecycle: proposed, active, retired. It does not prove operating availability.
SCADA run stateDescribes an observed control state at a timestamp. A stopped pump may still be available.
Operator availability flagDescribes whether operations considers the pump available for service, including approved overrides and limitations.

Priya maps “available for service” to the operator flag, then links the Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) state as supporting observation and the Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) status as asset context. The dashboard count changes from 47 to 11. Nothing was “fixed” in the raw values. The team corrected the meaning of the question and the mapping used to answer it.

2 | The metadata case file

Inspect evidence, not labels

Here is how to read the case file. Select each row and compare the label with the evidence needed to support it. Notice that a technical field name can be present while its business meaning, authority, time behavior, or consumer warning is absent. Your job is to decide whether the catalog claim is strong enough for the pump-availability decision.

Read across each row from field to meaning, authority, and use. A blank or generic cell is not harmless when the downstream decision is consequential.

The best field is not the one with the shortest name or newest timestamp. It is the field whose governed meaning fits the stated question.

Debrief. Catalog completeness is not a percentage of populated boxes. Riverbend can have 95 percent of fields described and still fail if the five fields used for regulatory reporting lack owner, meaning, time, or change evidence. Coverage should be measured against important decisions and critical data elements.

3 | Semantics and change

A mapping is a controlled promise

Semantics answers what something means. A taxonomy organizes labels into categories. An ontology goes further by defining concepts, relationships, and rules independently of one database. In this course, the practical starting point is not to build the largest possible ontology. It is to control the meaning needed by a utility decision and connect that meaning to authoritative fields and records.

Now imagine the SCADA supplier changes run_state from an integer to a text code and adds maintenance_hold. The data pipeline may still load. The dashboard may still display. Yet the meaning has changed. A governed schema-change notice identifies affected terms, mappings, measures, consumers, models, reports, tests, owners, effective dates, compatibility rules, and rollback conditions.

Use this before-and-after view as a change review. Move the divider and compare a field-only catalog with a decision-aware catalog. Pay attention to the extra connections, not the extra prose. Meaning becomes operational when a field is tied to a concept, owner, code list, time rule, consumer, and change event.

The left side inventories syntax. The right side connects syntax to business meaning, authority, time, use, and controlled change.

Metadata earns trust when a consumer can reconstruct why a value means what the utility says it means.

Questions people actually ask

Metadata and semantics FAQ

Do we need a large enterprise catalog before we start?

No. Start with the important decisions and the fields they depend on. Record the minimum profile, ownership, meaning, use, and change controls there. Expand deliberately without pretending the rest of the estate is already governed.

Is a data dictionary the same as a glossary?

No. A data dictionary usually describes technical fields and formats. A glossary defines business concepts. The mapping between them is where Riverbend shows that OPS.available_flag implements “available for service” for a stated scope.

Does an ontology replace the catalog?

No. The ontology can define shared concepts and relationships. The catalog identifies real datasets, fields, owners, versions, rights, and operational evidence. They should connect.

Who approves a definition?

The accountable business or data owner approves material meaning within assigned authority. A steward prepares and maintains the definition, mappings, examples, issues, and change evidence. The exact roles depend on the utility’s operating model.

What should happen when two departments disagree?

Preserve both proposed meanings and the affected uses. Bring the decision to the named owner or forum with examples and consequences. Do not silently select the definition favored by the team that owns the software.

How do we measure catalog quality?

Measure critical decision coverage, approved definitions, owner response, mapping tests, stale entries, unresolved conflicts, consumer notice, and whether users can reproduce the intended interpretation. A raw field count can hide the risk.

Evidence boundary and release truth. This working lesson teaches a governance method and uses an instructional Riverbend scenario. It does not certify a catalog, establish legal compliance, or approve a semantic model. Apply the controlled method, current utility authority, contracts, local law, and reviewed evidence. Technical anchors include the World Wide Web Consortium Data Catalog Vocabulary, the Simple Knowledge Organization System reference, and the PROV-O provenance ontology.
