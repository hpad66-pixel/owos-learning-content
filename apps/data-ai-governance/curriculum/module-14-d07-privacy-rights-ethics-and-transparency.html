Part C | Domain D07 | Rights review panel

Allowed to collect does not mean allowed to reuse

Riverbend collected customer contact and payment information to provide water service. A supplier now proposes using payment history, call notes, property records, and neighborhood indicators to rank customers for collection action.

Convene the panel
Instructional review | not legal advice or use approval
Panel referral

May the pilot proceed next week?

The proposal says the utility already possesses the data and the model does not use a protected-class field. No approved purpose statement, minimization analysis, impact review, customer explanation, correction path, or appeal process exists.

1 | Meet the person behind the record

The decision reaches a household, not a row

Maria Gomez calls Riverbend after a high bill caused by a concealed leak. The account has two late payments, notes from a language-access request, and a temporary hardship-plan application. The proposed score marks the account “high collection priority.” A staff member sees the score but cannot tell which evidence drove it, whether the leak adjustment is reflected, or how Maria can challenge the record.

Privacy is not only secrecy. It includes appropriate purpose, data handling, access, correction, sharing, retention, and effects on people. Purpose limitation asks whether this new use fits the approved reason for collecting and holding the information. Data minimization asks whether each field is needed and whether a less intrusive method can achieve the service goal.

“No protected field” is not a complete ethical test. Location, property, language, payment, contact, and service history can reproduce existing disadvantage or act as proxies. Review the whole decision and its effects.
Worked panel conversation

Reframe the proposal as a utility decision

The original proposal asks, “Can the model predict which customers are most likely to pay late?” The panel reframes it: “How should Riverbend identify and support accounts that may need timely, fair, and lawful assistance while protecting revenue and service obligations?” That change matters. It opens safer options such as earlier leak review, benefit eligibility checks, language access, payment-plan outreach, and human confirmation before collection action.

Customer ServiceExplains current workflow, correction, communication, and hardship options.
Privacy and LegalReview authority, purpose, rights, notices, contracts, and required safeguards.
Community and EquityChallenge burden, access, proxy effects, language, disability, and redress.

The panel does not assume that every beneficial use is permitted or that every permitted use is wise. It documents the intended service outcome, authority, necessary data, affected people, alternatives, likely benefits and harms, human authority, explanation, monitoring, and stop conditions.

2 | Operate the rights review

Change the proposed use and watch the review change

Select each proposed use. The panel tests purpose, necessity, effect, and redress. Notice that the same account information can support routine billing, require additional review for supportive outreach, and be rejected for an unsupported collection score. The conclusion depends on verified authority and facts, not this teaching tool.

Read all four tests together. A positive service intention does not cancel missing authority, unnecessary data, harmful effect, or absent redress.

A defensible data use connects public-service purpose with necessity, rights, impact evidence, explanation, correction, and accountable action.

Debrief. The panel is not a checkbox committee. It changes the use, data, workflow, authority, notice, and monitoring until the proposal is supportable, or it stops the proposal. Record dissent and unresolved uncertainty rather than converting them into a generic “approved with caution.”

3 | Minimize before protecting

Security does not justify unnecessary collection

A well-secured dataset can still be excessive. Riverbend asks which information is necessary for each step. Billing may need account identity, service, meter, rate, and payment records. Leak-assistance outreach may need a high-use signal and contact preference, but not free-text call notes or unrelated neighborhood attributes. Collection action may require a controlled legal and policy basis, current balance, notice history, protections, exceptions, and human authority.

Move the divider to compare the supplier proposal with a minimized support workflow. Notice that minimization changes the fields, purpose, access, retention, and decision path. It is not simply deleting columns after the model is built. Read the human-review and correction steps as part of the minimization design because they reduce reliance on unsupported inferences.

The governed alternative changes the service question and uses fewer, more relevant facts with human review.

Minimization can improve both rights protection and decision quality by removing unsupported proxies and stale context.

4 | Explanation and redress

A person needs a usable path, not a model lecture

Transparency means telling people what matters in language they can use. It does not require exposing security-sensitive details or every mathematical coefficient. Maria needs to know the action being considered, the main information used, the responsible utility role, significant limitations, how to correct the record, how to obtain human help, and how to challenge the decision.

Redress must work in practice. A web form that requires English, broadband, and a customer portal may exclude the person most affected. Riverbend tests phone, mail, in-person, language, disability, timing, identity verification, status updates, and escalation.

Follow the redress lane from notice to closure. Each handoff should preserve the person’s question, disputed fact, evidence, deadline, decision authority, explanation, correction, and communication. The path is a service process, not a disclaimer. Ask whether Maria could understand where the case sits, reach an authorized human, and receive the corrected outcome in time to avoid harm.

CustomerReceives notice and raises question
Service agentExplains, records, and pauses unsafe action
Data stewardChecks identity, source, definition, and correction
Decision ownerConfirms, changes, or escalates the action
CustomerReceives outcome, reason, and next appeal

Read across the roles. The question and evidence should not disappear at a handoff.

Redress is governed when a person can reach an authorized human, correct evidence, receive a reason, and escalate within a meaningful time.

Panel classification

Place each proposal in the right review state

Classify the proposals by what the current evidence supports. “Review required” is a legitimate answer. It keeps uncertainty visible while the utility gathers authority, necessity, impact, and redress evidence.

Foundation viewAsk why the data was collected, why it is needed now, who is affected, and how a person can correct or challenge the result.
Practitioner viewMaintain purpose, authority, minimization, impact, transparency, redress, monitoring, incident, and retirement evidence.
Leader viewSet boundaries for consequential uses and require affected-community, legal, privacy, operational, security, and independent challenge where appropriate.

Final applied check

Questions people bring to the panel

Privacy, rights, ethics, and transparency FAQ

Is anonymized data outside privacy review?

Not automatically. Review how it was transformed, whether people or small groups can be re-identified, linkage risk, purpose, access, release, retention, and the consequences of error.

Does consent solve every new use?

No. Consent may be inappropriate, unavailable, or insufficient depending on authority and power. Review necessity, fairness, rights, contracts, public duties, safeguards, and alternatives with authorized professionals.

Do we have to reveal the entire model?

No. Provide a useful explanation of purpose, principal information, action, limitations, responsible role, correction, human review, and appeal while protecting security, privacy, and legitimate confidential information.

What if the model is accurate overall?

Overall accuracy can hide worse errors or burdens for particular groups, locations, languages, disabilities, or service conditions. Test segments and consequences relevant to the use.

Who should sit on the review panel?

Include the decision owner and the operational, privacy, legal, security, data, customer, accessibility, records, supplier, and community perspectives needed by the consequence. Preserve independent challenge.

Evidence boundary and release truth. Maria and Riverbend are instructional. This lesson does not determine legal authority, discrimination, privacy compliance, or approval for a specific data use. Jurisdictions and duties vary. Use current law, policy, contracts, public-records and retention rules, community engagement, and authorized review. Anchors include the National Institute of Standards and Technology Privacy Framework, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development privacy and data protection resources, and the United States Federal Trade Commission privacy and security guidance. D07 remains subject to legal, privacy, community, practitioner, and source-owner review.
