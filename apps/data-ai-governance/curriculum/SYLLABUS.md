## The promise

This course teaches a utility team how to make data usable, accountable, and safe for consequential
decisions before it scales analytics or Artificial Intelligence (AI). A learner will not finish with
only a vocabulary list or a policy template. The learner will finish with a governed decision chain,
an evidence-backed baseline, named ownership, working controls, a 90-day launch plan, and a funded
roadmap that can be put into use at a utility.

The course is written for drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, and integrated One Water
organizations. The same operating patterns can be tailored for other infrastructure utilities.

## What a successful learner can do

By the end, the learner can:

1. Explain data governance in plain language to an operator, engineer, executive, board member,
   customer-service leader, regulator, attorney, or data professional.
2. Identify the material utility decisions that depend on data and state the consequence of failure.
3. Map data across Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA), historians, Geographic
   Information Systems (GIS), Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI), Computerized Maintenance
   Management Systems (CMMS), Enterprise Asset Management (EAM), Laboratory Information Management
   Systems (LIMS), customer and billing systems, models, cloud services, spreadsheets, and AI tools.
4. Connect physical assets, events, samples, customers, work, rules, evidence, decisions, and outcomes
   through stable identifiers and minimum records.
5. Define ownership, stewardship, custody, decision rights, forums, escalation, and independent
   challenge that fit the utility's size and operating model.
6. Build metadata, lineage, provenance, quality, integrity, security, privacy, records, sharing,
   supplier, operational technology, and AI controls that leave evidence behind.
7. Assess all 12 domains and 60 criteria using maturity and evidence confidence without hiding
   critical weaknesses inside one overall number.
8. Turn findings into owned, funded, sequenced work with closure proof and retesting.
9. Define a governed data product and release it with source, quality, rights, limits, lineage, and a
   measurable service or operating outcome.
10. Decide whether the evidence supports analytics, an operational twin, a digital twin, or a
    Decision Twin, and stop unsupported claims before deployment.
11. Start the work in 90 days around three to five material decisions and scale the pattern across the
    utility.

## Source authority and truth rules

The course uses the controlled Version 2.3 package in
[`hpad66-pixel/utility-data-ai-governance`](https://github.com/hpad66-pixel/utility-data-ai-governance).
That package contains 217 unique sources, 60 criteria across 12 domains, 21 figures, and 30 Quality
Assurance and Quality Control (QA/QC) findings.

The course follows these rules:

- Version 2.3 is the active baseline. Version 2.1 is not an active course source.
- A policy proves intent. It does not prove that a control operates.
- A criterion cannot rise above maturity Level 2 without recurring operating evidence.
- Maturity, evidence confidence, consequence, urgency, and legal applicability remain separate.
- No overall score can override a critical safety, legal, privacy, security, evidence, or independence
  condition.
- A public case provides a pattern to test. It does not prove enterprise maturity.
- A course-completion credential is not a certification, legal opinion, compliance opinion, or
  independent-assurance opinion.
- Laws, regulations, permits, and standards must be checked for edition, jurisdiction, coverage, and
  effective date before a utility relies on them.
- International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and International Electrotechnical Commission
  (IEC) documents may require licensed copies. The course may teach their operating role without
  reproducing licensed text.

## Who should take it

### Primary learners

- Governing-body members, general managers, and utility executives.
- Data owners, data stewards, data office staff, and program managers.
- Operations, maintenance, engineering, asset management, GIS, laboratory, customer, finance, and
  regulatory leaders.
- Information Technology (IT), Operational Technology (OT), architecture, cybersecurity, privacy,
  records, legal, procurement, and supplier-management staff.
- Analytics, data science, automation, and AI owners.
- Internal audit, independent assurance, consultants, and implementation partners.

### Prerequisites

No data-science or coding background is required. Learners should understand one utility process or
decision well enough to use it in the studios. Teams receive more value when operations, business,
data, technology, legal, and assurance seats learn together.

## Three learner lenses

Every chapter supports three switchable views:

| Lens | For | What it emphasizes |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | A smart learner who is new to the topic | Plain definitions, one utility example, the minimum safe practice |
| Practitioner | The person who must put the control into daily work | Methods, fields, evidence, exceptions, tools, and handoffs |
| Leader | The person who must approve, fund, challenge, or accept risk | Decisions, value, consequence, gates, ownership, and measures |

The lenses change depth and emphasis. They do not create three different methods.

## Utility overlays

Every chapter includes tailoring notes for:

- small or rural utilities with limited staff and shared roles;
- medium utilities building a repeatable program;
- large or multi-service utilities with formal data, cyber, legal, and assurance teams;
- drinking water, wastewater, stormwater, reuse, and integrated One Water services;
- public agencies, investor-owned utilities, authorities, and contracted operators;
- utilities working under incident, consent-order, capital-program, merger, or major AI-change pressure.

## The running utility case

The course follows **Riverbend Water and Reclamation Utility**, a fictional composite created for
instruction. Riverbend serves 280,000 people and operates:

- two drinking-water treatment plants;
- one water-reclamation facility and a reuse network;
- 68 wastewater lift stations;
- 2,200 miles of water and wastewater mains;
- SCADA and historian platforms from different eras;
- GIS, EAM, LIMS, AMI, customer billing, finance, document, and regulatory systems;
- a hydraulic model, a collection-system model, and locally managed spreadsheets;
- cloud analytics, vendor dashboards, and several AI use cases that were never inventoried together.

Riverbend's recurring decisions include wet-weather overflow response, drinking-water release,
capital renewal, customer leak and billing correction, regulatory reporting, pump maintenance, smart
meter sharing, and AI-assisted risk prioritization. The utility has capable people and many systems,
but it cannot always reconstruct which source, transformation, version, owner, and approval produced
an answer.

Riverbend is not presented as a real utility. Real cases from Anglian Water, Singapore PUB, and NEOM
are used only as public patterns to test against internal evidence.

## How every chapter is taught

Each chapter keeps the same ten-part learning rhythm:

1. **Promise:** what the learner will be able to do on a utility job.
2. **Working lens:** Foundation, Practitioner, or Leader.
3. **Plain terms:** every new term and acronym explained where it first appears.
4. **Riverbend case:** one concrete decision, failure, or handoff.
5. **Show it:** an interactive process, model, reveal, or simulation when a mechanism is taught.
6. **Deployment Studio:** the learner builds or improves a real utility artifact.
7. **Check yourself:** at least three different quiz or judgment types.
8. **Evidence gate:** what proof would support the claim and what remains unverified.
9. **Takeaway and leader's call:** the operating lesson and the decision that follows.
10. **Provenance:** controlled source, criteria, figures, and limits.

Implementation must follow `core/standards/WRITING-STANDARD.md` and select visuals through
`core/standards/VISUAL-ARSENAL.md`. A chapter should use at least four fitting visual types, at least
three quiz types, one tooltip system, and a simulation whenever the learner would otherwise have to
take a mechanism on faith.

## Assessment and completion model

| Assessment | Purpose | Proposed completion rule |
|---|---|---|
| Entry diagnostic | Establish role, utility context, current vocabulary, and confidence | Required, ungraded |
| Chapter checks | Test terms, sequence, evidence, and utility judgment | Complete all, retry with explanations |
| Deployment Studios | Produce the 20 connected launch-pack artifacts | Required artifacts saved with source and version |
| Part assessments | Test integrated decisions across chapters | 80 percent proposed, with remediation |
| Final scenario assessment | Apply the method to 40 utility scenarios from a controlled bank | 80 percent proposed |
| Capstone | Defend a 90-day governance launch around one material decision chain | Pass the rubric and every critical gate |

A learner cannot pass by averaging away a failed critical condition. The capstone fails until safety,
legal applicability, privacy, security, evidence integrity, accountable authority, and independent
challenge are handled at the level required by the use case.

The proposed completion credential is **OWOS Utility Data and AI Governance Practitioner**. It records
course completion only. It does not certify the learner or utility against ISO, IEC, National Institute
of Standards and Technology (NIST), legal, regulatory, or assurance requirements.

## Chapter 03 | Standards, law, and the applicability record

**Type:** Core with jurisdiction deep dive

## Chapter 07 | Roles, stewardship, forums, and the operating rhythm

# APPLIED CAPSTONE | Deploy one governed utility decision chain

## Capstone promise

The learner or utility team selects one consequential decision and builds a reviewable governance
pattern around it. The work must be specific enough to start, complete enough to defend, and small
enough to prove before the utility scales it.

Suitable capstone decisions include:

- wet-weather overflow risk and response;
- drinking-water release or escalation;
- regulatory or board reporting;
- customer leak, billing estimate, correction, or assistance eligibility;
- asset renewal or capital prioritization;
- pump maintenance or operating recommendation;
- smart-meter data sharing;
- open-data release;
- AI-assisted risk, inspection, dispatch, or maintenance recommendation;
- supplier or cloud data flow with material rights or exit risk.

## Required capstone evidence

1. Named user, service outcome, consequence, owner, authority, and scope.
2. Source, system, interface, supplier, jurisdiction, and decision map.
3. Stable-identifier and minimum-record design.
4. Business meaning, metadata, and data-product contract.
5. Source-to-outcome lineage and provenance event.
6. Fitness-for-purpose rules, measures, exceptions, and consumer limits.
7. Access, change, integrity, logging, recovery, and incident controls.
8. Privacy, rights, records, sharing, supplier, and location applicability.
9. AI, analytics, model, or twin suitability and release gate when applicable.
10. Assessment score with evidence confidence and competing views.
11. Findings, root causes, immediate controls, funded actions, and closure proof.
12. 90-day launch, 12-month roadmap, measures, review cadence, and next decision chain.

## Capstone rubric

| Dimension | Weight | Pass evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Decision value and consequence | 10 | Named utility user, outcome, consequence, authority, and success measure |
| Scope and data-estate map | 10 | Systems, interfaces, manual work, suppliers, jurisdictions, and boundaries |
| Ownership and operating model | 10 | Accountable seats, forums, escalation, time, competence, and separated duties |
| Identity, meaning, and product contract | 10 | Stable identifiers, minimum records, metadata, schema, use, and service promise |
| Lineage and provenance | 10 | Reconstructable backward and forward trace, including manual steps and versions |
| Quality and control operation | 10 | Use-based requirements, results, exceptions, root cause, and visible limits |
| Rights, security, records, and suppliers | 10 | Applicable controls, evidence, retention, location, terms, and exit |
| AI, analytics, or twin gate | 10 | Suitability, human authority, validation, monitoring, rollback, and honest phase claim |
| Findings, funding, and roadmap | 10 | Prioritized actions, owners, dependencies, dates, cost range, and decisions |
| Evidence, retest, and learning | 10 | Evidence register, confidence, closure test, residual risk, outcome review, and next cycle |

**Proposed pass rule:** at least 80 of 100, plus a pass on every applicable critical gate. Safety,
legal coverage, privacy, security, evidence integrity, decision authority, and independent challenge
cannot be averaged away.

## Capstone review

The learner presents to a panel that represents utility executive, operations, data, technology,
legal or privacy, and assurance seats. The panel may approve, approve with conditions, require
rework, or reject the scale decision. The presentation ends with the exact next decision, owner,
date, and evidence required.
