The missing layer above utility systems

Your utility may own every source system it needs and still be unable to defend one material answer. This chapter shows what sits between systems and accountable decisions.

Riverbend, 5:42 a.m. during heavy rain

A high-level alarm reaches the duty manager. The Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system shows a rising wet well. Rainfall data uses a different clock. The Geographic Information System (GIS) and collection model disagree on the lift-station identifier. A work order sits in the Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS), but the operator does not know whether it affects the alarm. The systems are working. The decision chain is not.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain why system count and data volume do not prove decision readiness
  • Identify the governed layer that connects systems to an accountable decision
  • Trace the consequence of a weak handoff through a utility decision chain
  • Estimate the direct labor part of fragmentation and write an executive case for action

Start with this simple test: can a qualified new team member find, access, join, and understand the data needed for the decision without depending on the one person who knows where everything is hidden? If not, the utility has data risk and knowledge risk at the same time.

You will leave with a repeatable diagnostic: inventory the system handoffs, name the missing governance record at each handoff, estimate the direct effort spent rebuilding the answer, and assign the first corrective action.

Your job is to fund an operating capability tied to service, safety, compliance, customers, capital, and trust. Do not approve a general data-cleanup program without a named decision, consequence, owner, and measurable change.

The utility has systems, but not always accountable data

A source system is built to do a job. SCADA controls and monitors processes. GIS maps assets. CMMS manages maintenance work. Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) records meter activity. A Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS) manages samples and results. Each can work well inside its own boundary while the answer breaks between systems.

SCADA and historian

Show signals and operating history. They do not automatically explain the approved use, asset match, or quality limit.

GIS and network model

Show place and modeled behavior. They may use different asset identifiers, versions, and update dates.

AMI and customer systems

Show meter events, use, accounts, and bills. They do not settle purpose, estimation, correction, or sharing rights by themselves.

CMMS and asset records

Show work and asset history. They may not reveal whether an open defect changes today's operating decision.

LIMS and compliance records

Show samples, methods, and results. They still need approval, amendment, reporting, and retention rules.

Spreadsheets and vendor portals

Often bridge gaps quickly. Their formulas, copies, owners, and exceptions may be hard to reconstruct later.

1
System count is not decision readiness.

Decision readiness means the people using an answer can identify its sources, meaning, owner, route, quality limits, rights, retained evidence, and correction path for the stated use.

The governed layer has eight jobs in the controlled method: identity, meaning, ownership, lineage, quality, rights, retention, and evidence. It is not another data warehouse. A platform may support it, but the layer also includes decision rights, operating controls, records, and correction.

2 / 3

Governance becomes visible in a decision

A policy is useful, but it is a statement of intent. Governance becomes dependable when the utility can point to five connected things for a real decision.

1. Decision right

Who has authority to choose, approve, stop, or escalate?

2. Named owner

Who is accountable for the rule, outcome, and accepted exception?

3. Operating control

What check runs during normal work, and what happens when it fails?

4. Retained record

What lets another person reconstruct the input, reason, action, and result?

5. Correction path

Who fixes the source, amends the answer, notifies users, and confirms closure?

A decision right is not the same as access to a screen. An operating control is not the same as a policy. A retained record is not the same as an undated screenshot. A correction path keeps the utility from fixing one display while the bad source continues to spread.

Leader's call

Decide who owns this decision chain and which condition requires a stop, escalation, or accepted exception. Fund the smallest governed slice that changes the decision now, then expand it using the evidence from operation.

3 / 3

Find the fragmentation tax

The fragmentation tax is the cost of rebuilding the same truth. It appears in searches, manual joins, copied spreadsheets, phone calls, repeated explanations, corrections, delayed work, and dependence on one specialist. The direct labor is only the easiest part to estimate. Service, safety, compliance, customer, and trust consequences stay separate until the utility has defensible evidence for them.

Do not blame the spreadsheet. A spreadsheet can be a controlled tool when its owner, source, formula, version, access, review, exception, and retention are managed. A large platform can be an uncontrolled shadow system when those facts are hidden. Measure the handoff, not the brand of tool.

The model uses a transparent planning formula: cycles per month multiplied by 12 months, people, and minutes per person, then adjusted for added rework. Multiply the resulting hours by a locally approved loaded labor cost. Keep every input visible. If the team cannot support an input, label it as an estimate and name the record needed to verify it.

Deployment Studio: write the executive case for action

Use your Chapter 00 material decision. Keep protected infrastructure, customer information, security detail, legal advice, and controlled evidence in a utility-approved repository. This browser tool saves only your draft text on this device.

Decision and consequence

Name the trigger, authorized decision, current failure pattern, and the service, safety, compliance, customer, financial, environmental, or trust consequence.

Fragmentation evidence

State the people, cycles, minutes, manual joins, exceptions, delays, and record source. Separate measured facts from planning assumptions.

First governed change

Name the identifier, definition, owner, control, record, or correction path that would improve this decision without waiting for a full platform program.

Value and review

Name the expected operating change, baseline measure, target, owner, due date, and the gate for funding the next slice.

Chapter 01 evidence gate

  • The executive case begins with one material decision and a named utility consequence.
  • The system handoff map identifies at least one missing identity, meaning, ownership, lineage, quality, rights, retention, or evidence record.
  • Measured facts, planning estimates, and unverified claims are labeled separately.
  • The fragmentation estimate records its formula, inputs, source, owner, and validation plan.
  • The first governed change has an accountable owner, operating measure, exception path, due date, and next review.

Leader's call

Approve a 30-day governed slice around the material decision. Require the team to show the baseline, the control result, the exceptions, and the decision outcome before asking for a larger platform or program investment.
