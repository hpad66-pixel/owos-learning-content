Roles, stewardship, forums, and the operating rhythm

Put authority, day-to-day responsibility, advice, control operation, and independent challenge in the right seats. Give each forum a clear decision boundary, then make governance part of daily operations and normal management review.

Released lesson · roles, forums, and cadence

The main lesson stays visible. The lens changes role-specific guidance. A small utility may combine compatible roles, but it still needs clear authority and independent challenge for material decisions.

⚑

Riverbend, who can accept this risk?

A quality exception affects an overflow dashboard, a permit report, and a vendor model. Operations wants the dashboard restored. The data team can explain the defect. Cybersecurity sees an access-control issue. Legal and compliance want the filing impact assessed. The supplier says its model still runs. Assurance asks whether the evidence is sufficient. None of those facts alone names the person authorized to accept the operational, regulatory, security, or model risk.

5
The accountability rule

Name five things for every material decision: who governs the boundary, who is accountable for the outcome, who performs and controls the work, who must advise, and who independently challenges the evidence. Being consulted does not transfer decision authority.

People need to know who decides, who does the work, who checks it, and where an unresolved issue goes.

Write role letters, delegations, thresholds, forum terms, quorum, conflicts, decision records, escalation times, alternates, and measures into normal workflows.

The governing body sets direction and risk boundaries. Management implements them. Assurance reports independently on whether the system is designed and operating as claimed.

1 / 3

Put accountability in the right seats

An accountable owner is answerable for the domain, product, or outcome and can make or escalate decisions within an approved delegation. A data steward performs the ongoing meaning, quality, metadata, issue, and evidence work. A custodian operates technical or records controls. A control operator runs a named control. An independent assurance role tests and reports without approving its own work.

Governing, owning, stewarding, operating, securing, advising, assuring, and approving are functions, not always job titles. In a small utility, one person may hold several compatible functions. The utility must still prevent conflicting duties. The person who changes a material formula should not be the only person who approves its release and certifies that the change control worked.

Governing body

Sets direction, principles, risk boundaries, delegations, oversight, and accountability.

Accountable owner

Owns the outcome, accepts or escalates risk within delegation, and ensures resources and control.

Steward

Maintains meaning, rules, metadata, quality, issues, evidence, and change recommendations.

Operator or custodian

Creates, processes, stores, transports, protects, monitors, and supports records and services.

Security and privacy

Set or advise on protection, access, incident, privacy, and approved-use requirements within authority.

Legal, compliance, and records

Advise or decide where delegated on obligations, filings, rights, contracts, holds, and retention.

Independent assurance

Tests design and operation, challenges evidence, reports findings, and avoids self-review.

Decision approver

Authorizes a release, exception, use, filing, investment, or risk response under an explicit delegation.

The familiar Responsible-Accountable-Consulted-Informed (RACI) grid is a conversation aid. It is not enough by itself. Add the exact decision, scope, thresholds, authority source, inputs, required advice, evidence, output, service time, alternate, conflict rule, and escalation route. One accountable role per decision is usually clearest, but different risk types may require separate authorized decisions.

2 / 3

Design forums and escalation around real decisions

A council should not become a meeting where every data problem waits. Its charter should name the decisions the forum owns and the decisions it does not. Routine operations stay with operations. Legal conclusions stay with authorized legal counsel. Cybersecurity decisions stay with delegated security authority. The executive team or board receives only matters that cross approved consequence, risk, funding, policy, or accountability thresholds.

3 / 3

Run governance through normal work

An operating rhythm connects controls and decisions at the speed they are needed. Daily work should detect and contain. Weekly review should remove blockers and age issues. Monthly domain review should approve definitions, rules, ownership, exceptions, and changes. Quarterly leadership review should compare risk, value, capacity, funding, and roadmap outcomes. Event-driven escalation should run immediately when a threshold is crossed.

Chapter 07 evidence gate

  • Every critical domain and product has a named accountable owner and operational steward with accepted role letters.
  • Stewards have authority, time, competence, objectives, measures, evidence duties, alternates, and escalation routes.
  • Decision rights distinguish governing, owning, stewarding, operating, securing, advising, approving, and assuring.
  • Conflicting duties across creation, change, approval, release, correction, risk acceptance, and assurance are separated or independently controlled.
  • Forums have approved purpose, scope, exclusions, delegation, membership, quorum, conflicts, inputs, records, thresholds, and review.
  • Daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, and event-driven work has defined inputs, decisions, outputs, owners, service times, and retained evidence.
  • Supplier responsibilities are explicit and enforceable, while utility accountability and exit capability remain clear.

Leader's call

Do not ask who owns data as a general question. Ask who can approve this definition, accept this exception, release this report, authorize this model use, fund this treatment, and attest that the control worked. If the answer changes by person rather than by written authority, the operating model is not ready.
