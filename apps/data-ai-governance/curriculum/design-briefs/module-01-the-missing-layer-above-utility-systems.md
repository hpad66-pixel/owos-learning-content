# Module Design Brief: Chapter 01

## Identity

Course `owos-master-data-governance-001`; learning object `dga001:01`; **The missing layer above utility systems**. The controlled method is Version 2.3. Riverbend facts and fragmentation estimates are instructional until locally verified.

## Learning job

At 5:42 a.m., the learner decides whether a wet-weather alert has enough governed evidence for response, diagnoses the first broken handoff, and builds an executive case for action.

## Module design fingerprint

Field-incident diagnosis. Signature mechanism is tracing a wet-weather answer across fragmented and governed handoffs. The existing layer stack, before-and-after view, handoff simulator, consequence tree, fragmentation model, builder, and tracker remain. The work product is an executive case for action. Graph, Community, Start, bottom anchor, focus return, responsive behavior, reduced motion, and existing learner events are preserved.

## Instructor explanation plan

The learner is told where to start in both visuals, what changes at the governed layer, how failure propagates, and why a dashboard cannot repair unsupported joins.

## Written-first review

More than 1,300 words teach the complete mechanism without video. The Riverbend wet-weather scene is marked as the worked example. The non-example is buying another dashboard without repairing evidence.

## Visual pacing review

The incident scene, stack, comparison, handoff simulation, consequence map, cost model, and executive-case builder prevent long prose runs. Dark surfaces use light text.

## Explanatory graphic plan

The `layered-stack` shows source systems below governed meaning and authority. The `failure-chain` shows identifier conflict moving into delay and unreconstructable decisions. Mobile stacks both vertically.

## Learner FAQ plan

Five questions address whether the layer is another database, why not begin with a catalog, dashboard limits, fragmentation-tax estimation, and the first practical correction.

## Recording script

Not required for this written-first lesson.
