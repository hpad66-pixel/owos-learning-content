Intake decision

May Riverbend automate flood alerts from this feed?

The feed is 93 percent complete over the last month. It is delayed by up to 35 minutes, two gauges have unresolved clock drift, and coverage is weakest in the neighborhood most affected by flash flooding.

1 | Diagnose the use

Quality is a relationship, not a permanent label

Fitness for use means that data is suitable for a specific purpose. A dataset can be fit for a monthly planning trend and unfit for immediate crew dispatch. That is not a contradiction. The use changes the consequence, time requirement, coverage, precision, and tolerated uncertainty.

Start with the decision and consequence. Then identify the critical data elements and define measurable requirements. Common quality dimensions include completeness, accuracy, validity, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, spatial coverage, and representativeness. Utilities also need operational evidence such as sensor identity, calibration, communication state, manual override, and known limitations.

An average cannot cancel a critical failure. If time quality must pass for a flood alert, strong completeness does not compensate for a failed clock. Record critical gates separately.
Worked quality clinic

One rainfall feed, three legitimate answers

Riverbend reviews gauge feed RAIN-NORTH-15M. For a public historical chart, 93 percent completeness may be acceptable if missing periods are visible and no real-time claim is made. For a drainage-capacity study, the team needs calibrated gauges, storm-event coverage, stable units, spatial representativeness, and a method for gaps. For automated warning, it also needs tight latency, synchronized time, live health status, fallback, escalation, and evidence that vulnerable areas are not systematically under-observed.

Public trendInformational. Show gaps, source, refresh, and limits.
Planning modelAnalytical. Require event, calibration, unit, spatial, and method evidence.
Automated alertConsequential. Add latency, live health, fallback, authority, and stop rules.

The lesson is not that every use needs perfect data. It is that Riverbend must name the requirement, measure actual evidence, decide what happens when it fails, and communicate the quality state to consumers.

Debrief. Changing the dropdown changes the decision context, not the raw data. That is why “the dataset passed quality” is an incomplete statement. Say which use was tested, when, with what thresholds, coverage, exceptions, and approval.

Observability must reveal which requirement failed, where, when, for whom, and what action follows.

Data observability helps teams detect and diagnose changes in freshness, volume, schema, distribution, quality rules, and pipeline behavior. It supports governance when alerts connect to owners, affected uses, consequence, containment, communication, root cause, correction, retest, and closure.

4 | Close the issue, not the ticket

Turn a signal into controlled learning

A quality alert is not the end of governance. Riverbend must determine whether the signal is real, identify affected decisions, contain unsafe use, notify consumers, correct the source or process, test the correction, and update the rule if the old rule was wrong. A closed ticket without root cause and retest can hide recurrence.

Step through the operating loop. Pay attention to the decision gates. A team may restore a pipeline and still need to correct affected reports, bills, models, or public notices. Technical recovery and decision correction are related but separate jobs. Use the final Retest stage to confirm both the source control and the downstream decision evidence before calling the issue closed.

Follow the loop from signal to affected decision and back through retest. Each stage leaves a different record.

Observability creates value only when a signal leads to an owned decision, correction, communication, and verified closure.

Estimate before calculating

How much does the average hide?

Make an estimate before checking. Riverbend has 20 gauges, but only 14 have current calibration and synchronized clocks. Estimate the supported coverage percentage. Then consider whether that percentage alone proves spatial fitness.

Questions from the quality clinic

Data quality and observability FAQ

Does data have to be perfect?

No. Requirements should be proportionate to the use and consequence. The utility may accept a known limitation for planning while prohibiting automated action. The limitation, authority, and response must be explicit.

Who sets quality thresholds?

The data and decision owners approve material requirements with steward, operator, technical, legal, safety, privacy, and consumer input as needed. A platform team should not invent business risk tolerance alone.

What is the difference between validation and observability?

Validation tests a rule at a point in the process. Observability combines rules, events, trends, and operating signals to understand changing condition and diagnose problems over time.

Can a dashboard quality score be useful?

Yes, for orientation, if users can see critical gates, dimensions, coverage, exceptions, time, and use. Do not let one number hide a failed safety, rights, regulatory, or service requirement.

What if a consumer finds the problem first?

Record the consumer signal as evidence. Contain affected use, investigate the gap in monitoring, correct impacted outputs, and improve the rule and communication path. Consumer reports are part of observability.

Evidence boundary and release truth. Thresholds and Riverbend values are instructional. This lesson does not certify data quality, approve public alerts, or establish engineering, legal, regulatory, or safety compliance. Use current utility requirements and verified evidence. Anchors include the International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission 25012 data quality model page, the National Institute of Standards and Technology Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework, and the United States Environmental Protection Agency quality program. D05 criteria remain subject to controlled-source and practitioner review.
