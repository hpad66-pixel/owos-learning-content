The language of trustworthy utility data

People cannot govern together if the same words mean different things in operations, the laboratory, engineering, information technology, records, and management. This chapter gives your utility a plain working language and turns it into records you can use.

Riverbend, 2:18 p.m. before a drinking-water result is released

A laboratory analyst has a finished result for sample RB-24-017. The Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS) shows the value and the analyst's name. The operations log shows the treatment condition when the sample was collected. The instrument has a current calibration record. A supervisor must decide whether the result may be released for its intended use. The number looks ordinary. The real question is whether Riverbend can show what the number is, where it came from, what happened to it, who acted, whether it stayed intact, and whether it is fit for this release.

What you will be able to do

  • Separate governance decisions from the management work that carries them out
  • Explain lineage, provenance, integrity, and traceability without using the terms as synonyms
  • Write a minimum provenance event for a utility record
  • Reject a quality claim that has no stated use, time, consequence, evidence, or owner
  • Package a first governed data product with a clear service promise and limits

Use the one-question test throughout this chapter: what question does this word answer? Governance answers who decides and under what rule. Management answers how the daily work gets done. Lineage answers where the data traveled. Provenance answers what created or changed this specific record. Integrity asks whether the record stayed complete and protected. Traceability asks whether another qualified person can follow and reconstruct it.

Your practical goal is a controlled glossary that is short enough to use in procedures, data contracts, catalog entries, issue records, and design reviews. Every definition should point to a minimum record or control.

Approve one utility language for material decisions, then require programs and vendors to map their local terms to it. Do not let a vocabulary debate delay control of a high-consequence record. Record the definition, owner, scope, source, version, exceptions, and review date.

1 / 3

Governance and management do different jobs

Data governance sets direction and accountability. It decides why data matters, who may decide, which rules apply, what risk is accepted, what evidence is required, and how performance is challenged. Data management carries out the daily work. It creates, describes, stores, moves, protects, checks, corrects, retains, and disposes of data.

The two must connect. Governance without management produces approved documents that nobody can operate. Management without governance produces activity without clear authority, priorities, or accountability. A small utility may assign both kinds of work to the same person. That does not make the jobs identical. The person should know which authority they are using, which record they must leave, and who reviews the result.

1
Ask whether the item sets the rule or performs the work.

Approving who may release a drinking-water result is governance. Checking the result against the approved release rule is management. Recording the check is evidence. Accepting an exception is a governance decision again.

A data owner usually makes or sponsors governance decisions for a defined data area. A data steward connects those decisions to daily definitions, rules, issues, and evidence. A data custodian operates technical controls. Analysts, operators, engineers, laboratory staff, records staff, and automated services create and use records. Titles vary. The utility still needs the responsibilities and escalation path to be explicit.

You do not need a large committee to begin. For sample RB-24-017, name who owns the release rule, who performs each check, who may approve, who handles a failed check, and where the evidence is kept.

Write the separation into the procedure and the data-product contract. Include the decision right, responsible work, control frequency, evidence, exception owner, service target, and review date. If one person fills several roles, add an independent review where the consequence requires it.

Review governance through decisions and evidence, not meeting counts. Ask which material rules changed, which exceptions remained open, which data products missed their service promise, which consequences occurred, and what management action followed.

2 / 3

Lineage, provenance, integrity, and traceability answer four questions

These terms overlap in everyday conversation, and standards do not always draw the boundaries in exactly the same place. Your utility does not need to win a vocabulary argument. It needs controlled definitions that make requirements, records, and tests unambiguous. Record any mapping to a required external definition. Then use the same working language in procedures, contracts, catalogs, incident reviews, reports, and artificial-intelligence use cases.

RouteLineage

Where did the data start, where did it travel, which systems and people handled it, and which transformations changed it before this use?

Origin and productionProvenance

Which identified inputs, activity or run, person or service, time, method, instrument, and version produced this specific record?

Trust in the recordIntegrity

Did the record remain complete, consistent, authentic, and protected from accidental or unauthorized creation, change, substitution, or loss?

Follow and reconstructTraceability

Can a qualified person move backward to sources and forward to uses, then reproduce or explain the result, approval, change, and consequence?

Data lineage is often wider than one event. It can show sample collection, receipt, preparation, instrument run, calculation, approval, release, dashboard use, and later amendment. Data provenance is the evidence behind a particular creation or change. Data integrity depends on controls such as authorized creation, validation, versioning, audit history, protection, reconciliation, and recovery. Traceability is the usable ability that the connected evidence gives an authorized person.

A timestamp is not provenance by itself. A file date may show when a copy was saved. It does not necessarily identify the source version, activity, actor, method, configuration, reason, quality result, approval, or earlier record that the copy replaced.

The controlled method sets a useful minimum for a transformation event: identified inputs, identified outputs, an activity or run, the actor or service, time, and the code or configuration version. Riverbend adds the method and instrument when the laboratory process requires them. It also records quality state, approval, reason, and amendment link when those facts matter to the release. The minimum is not the smallest number of fields that software can store. It is the smallest defensible record for the stated process and consequence.

Input

Stable identifier and version for each source entity used.

Output

Stable identifier and version for the record produced or changed.

Activity

Run, transformation, review, approval, correction, or other action.

Agent

Authorized person, system service, instrument, or organization role.

Time

When the activity occurred, with the approved time rule.

Version

Method, code, model, rule, schema, and configuration used.

Result and quality

Outcome, validation state, exception, and accepted limit.

Authority and reason

Approval, release, correction, or rejection decision and basis.

Amendment link

Prior version, affected uses, notice, and closure when changed.

If you remember only one example, remember this: lineage is the whole route from sample to release. Provenance is the evidence for the specific run and approval. Integrity controls protect each record. Traceability is Riverbend's ability to walk the route and explain it.

Test the event with a reconstruction exercise. Give a qualified alternate only the released result and approved access. Record whether they can find the input, effective method, instrument state, activity, agent, time, quality result, approval, downstream uses, and amendment history. Every stop becomes a controlled issue.

Require provenance in contracts for material data products, models, analytics, and artificial-intelligence uses. Specify the evidence, export format, retention, access, change notice, and exit rights. A vendor statement that lineage is available is not the same as Riverbend being able to retrieve and use it.

3 / 3

Quality belongs to a stated use, and a data product carries the promise

Data quality is not a permanent adjective attached to a dataset. It is a conclusion about whether data meets stated requirements for a particular use. The same laboratory result could be useful for an internal morning trend, not ready for a treatment adjustment, and prohibited from a regulatory release. Change the use, time, or consequence and the quality requirement may change.

A defensible quality statement names at least six things: the intended use, the user or decision authority, the time window, the quality dimensions and rules, the consequence of failure, and the evidence plus owner. It also states what happens when the data does not meet the rule. Words such as clean, trusted, certified, gold, accurate, complete, or high quality are not enough without those facts.

2
Finish the sentence before you accept the claim.

"This data is fit for [named use], by [authorized user], during [time window], when [stated dimensions and thresholds] pass; failure could cause [consequence], so [owner] must [hold, correct, escalate, qualify, or approve] and retain [evidence]."

Do not average away a critical failure. A result with excellent timeliness, completeness, and format may still fail the release because the sample identity is unresolved or the approval is missing. Use an explicit gate when one dimension must pass. A weighted score can help prioritize work, but it should not turn a failed critical rule into a passing average.

A data product turns recurring data use into an accountable service. It can be a governed table, feed, application programming interface, report dataset, file package, model input, or linked set of records. It does not have to be a new platform. The product has named consumers and intended uses, an accountable owner, a contract that defines meaning and structure, a service promise, quality and observability, rights and protection, lineage and provenance, version and change rules, limits, support, and lifecycle.

A data contract makes expectations testable between producer and consumer. A service promise states what normal delivery looks like and how failure is handled. Observability makes the product's operating state visible. None of these claims should be stronger than the evidence and support Riverbend can sustain.

Do not rename a shared file and call the work finished. A product name does not create ownership, service, quality, rights, provenance, support, or change control. Start with a small recurring use and make every promise visible.

Chapter 02 questions

Are lineage and provenance the same?

No. Lineage follows movement and transformation. Provenance identifies origins, activities, agents, versions, and times behind a record.

What does integrity add?

Integrity asks whether the record remained complete and protected from unauthorized or undetected change.

Can data be high quality for one use and poor for another?

Yes. A value may support trend screening but still be insufficient for a regulatory release.

What is a minimum provenance event?

It is the smallest retained record that identifies inputs, outputs, activity, actor or service, time, and relevant version.

What makes a data product governed?

It has a purpose, owner, contract, service promise, limits, quality state, lineage, and lifecycle.
