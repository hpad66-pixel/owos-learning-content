Part D | Ninety-day launch simulation

Do not launch a framework. Launch a working cycle.

Riverbend has approval to begin, but not to create a new bureaucracy or one large technology program. In thirteen weeks, the team must govern a small set of consequential decisions, produce evidence, and earn the next funding decision.

Start week one
Working lesson | local mobilization approval required
Monday, 8:00 a.m.

What is the first mobilization move?

The sponsor asks for an enterprise data inventory before any governance meeting. Operations asks for help with overflow response now. An AI team is already testing capital-risk recommendations.

Launch rule

Ninety days is enough to prove a pattern, not finish governance

A material decision keeps governance connected to utility work. A sponsor creates authority. A working owner keeps the cycle moving. A governance ritual turns policy into routine.

The launch also needs a decision backlog, a leader dashboard, and an escalation trigger. These are not ceremonial documents. They help the team know what to do on Tuesday morning when evidence conflicts or an owner cannot resolve a risk.

Protect the scope. Riverbend begins with overflow response, regulatory reporting, customer correction, capital renewal, and AI-assisted risk prioritization. It does not promise to govern every dataset in ninety days.
Worked launch setup

Five decisions, one cross-functional operating team

Riverbend appoints the chief operating officer as sponsor and a governance program lead as working owner. Operations leads overflow response. Compliance owns the reporting decision. Customer service owns correction and redress. Asset management owns capital renewal. The analytics owner is accountable for the AI use case, while the business decision remains with the approved capital forum.

The first evidence request is deliberately small. For each decision, the team asks for the actual source records, latest approved rule, one normal sample, one exception, one manual handoff, one supplier dependency, and the retained approval or outcome. This reveals identity, meaning, quality, lineage, rights, access, retention, and authority problems without demanding a perfect enterprise inventory.

1 | Days 1 to 30

Frame, select, and make the first thirteen weeks visible

Select a week on the ribbon. Each week has a decision, a deliverable, and an evidence test. Notice that the schedule alternates between mapping, operating, reviewing, and deciding. It does not spend twelve weeks writing policy and reserve the final week for implementation. Compare the output of one week with the input to the next, and ask who must act before the handoff can occur.

Read the ribbon as an operating sequence. Every week leaves a useful record and changes the next decision.

A fast launch moves from scope to evidence to control to funding, with review built into the cycle.

Sequence check

Which order creates the first evidence-backed cycle?

Choose the sequence that starts with accountable scope and ends with a funding decision. A launch can gather information early, but it should not score maturity or announce solutions before the team has inspected actual decisions and evidence.

2 | Days 31 to 60

Trace and test one real decision at a time

For each selected decision, the team follows source, transformation, manual activity, review, action, communication, outcome, correction, and retained evidence. It tests shared identity, business meaning, time, quality, access, change, lineage, supplier rights, retention, and approval. A failed test creates a bounded finding and immediate control, not an invitation to rewrite the entire enterprise architecture.

Read the five decision cards as one portfolio. Each card shows a different consequence and evidence problem. The launch team can reuse methods and identifiers, but the control threshold must still fit the use. The yellow card is paused because its evidence or authority is not ready.

The weekly evidence ritual

Make governance happen in a repeatable hour

Operate the loop from evidence to next action. Select any stage to reveal the question the team asks. The ritual is short because detailed work happens between meetings. The meeting resolves exceptions, verifies closure, and routes decisions to the right authority. Follow one Riverbend issue through every stage and notice that it cannot disappear merely because an update was discussed or a ticket changed status.

Move clockwise through evidence, exception, consequence, owner, action, and retest.

The ritual keeps governance close to operating evidence and prevents unresolved issues from disappearing between meetings.

Scope defense

Which request belongs outside the active launch?

Choose the request that would overwhelm the first cycle without protecting an immediate decision. The team can place useful ideas in the backlog. Saying “not in this cycle” is disciplined governance when the reason and review path are visible.

3 | Days 61 to 90

Decide, fund, and establish the next operating cycle

The team calibrates practice and evidence separately, challenges unsupported ratings, converts findings into immediate controls and funded actions, and presents a small executive view. The sponsor sees decisions, consequence, evidence state, unresolved critical conditions, owners, due dates, funding questions, and outcome measures. A large count of cataloged fields is not the main result.

Read this dashboard from decision to action. Green means the stated control has evidence for the current use, not that the utility is perfect. Yellow means a bounded issue has an owner and date. Red means a critical condition blocks the proposed action or requires escalation. Unknown remains visible.

Executive decision

What should Riverbend approve on day 90?

Choose the decision that preserves momentum and control. The sponsor should approve a bounded next cycle, funded actions, accountable roles, evidence review dates, and conditions for any held use case. A ninety-day launch is evidence for the next decision, not proof of enterprise maturity.

Final applied check

Questions before week one

Ninety-day launch FAQ

Why choose only three to five decisions?

That range is large enough to reveal shared governance needs and small enough to trace actual evidence. Adjust it to capacity, but keep the first cycle bounded and consequential.

Do we need a chief data officer first?

No. You need executive sponsorship, a working owner, accountable decision owners, practitioners, and access to legal, security, records, privacy, supplier, and assurance review as the scope requires.

What if people already have full-time jobs?

Protected time is a funding decision. State the hours, duties, backfill, and conflicts. A launch that assumes invisible labor will fail even if its framework is excellent.

Should the team begin with policy writing?

Record immediate duties and use existing authority, but test policy against real decisions. Write or revise policy from observed operating needs, exceptions, and evidence.

What are the six minimum day-90 deliverables?

A bounded decision portfolio, named operating team, traced evidence samples, findings and immediate controls, a funded roadmap, and an executive view with the next review cycle.

How do we keep the launch from becoming an IT project?

Keep decision owners, operators, service teams, data stewards, technology, legal and rights reviewers, records, suppliers, and assurance connected. Technology enables the operating model but does not own every business decision.

Evidence boundary. Riverbend, staffing, weeks, and dashboard values are instructional. This lesson does not appoint roles, establish labor duties, approve a program, accept risk, or authorize spending. Tailor the cycle to actual utility authority, emergencies, bargaining obligations, legal requirements, cyber and privacy controls, procurement, and practitioner capacity. Anchors include the controlled course syllabus, the EPA Effective Utility Management resources, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. Human review remains required before launch.
