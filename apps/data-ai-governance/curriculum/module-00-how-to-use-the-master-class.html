The promise and the boundaries

This course helps a utility design governance for one material decision, build reusable records, check whether evidence supports a claim, and plan a 90-day start. It does not grant legal approval, certify a system, replace a licensed standard, approve a security design, or issue an independent opinion.

Foundation lens

Learn the language and see each idea on a utility decision.

Practitioner lens

Build the records, roles, controls, and evidence path for your own utility.

Leader lens

Set decision rights, accept or reject risk, fund the work, and state what can be claimed.

The six claim levels below are not interchangeable. The work, evidence, independence, and authority increase as you move from learning toward formal third-party conclusions.

Learning builds capability. Screening flags where to look. An assessment compares evidence with criteria. An audit tests records or controls. Certification is a formal third-party conformity decision under a scheme. Assurance adds independent confidence within a defined scope.

Boundary rule. If the decision depends on licensed standards, law, permits, cybersecurity, privacy, public health, labor obligations, engineering judgment, or an external claim, bring in the authorized utility role and qualified adviser. Record the source, version, scope, and decision. Do not turn course language into an approval it cannot provide.

Use the gate sequence to separate four claims that are often blended together. Select each stage and ask what new evidence and authority it requires. In utility work, a sound method can still be untested in your jurisdiction, and a successful internal pilot can still fall short of an independent assurance conclusion.

Open the gates in sequence and compare the evidence required at each boundary.

A stronger public claim requires a stronger and more independent evidence package.

Leader's call

Decide which gate you are funding. A published method, a piloted assessment, a utility deployment, and an independent conclusion are four different products with different budgets, authorities, and risks.

This check asks you to name each claim at the narrowest level the evidence can support. Work from the words actually used, not the confidence of the person saying them. If your answer is wrong, use the explanation to identify which evidence, authority, or independence requirement was missing, then retry.

Choose your material decision

A material decision gives governance a real job. It has a named user, a choice or action, a meaningful consequence, and an evidence problem. Starting with a data platform or policy alone often hides who needs the data and what is at stake.

Capstone decision statement

When [trigger], [named role] decides [action] for [service, asset, customer, or obligation], using [governed evidence], because a wrong or late decision could [consequence].

Riverbend trigger
Heavy rainfall and rising collection-system levels.
Named user
The duty manager responsible for wet-weather response.
Decision
Whether to escalate crews, storage, notifications, and incident coordination.
Consequence
A late or wrong response could increase overflow, safety, permit, customer, and environmental harm.
Evidence problem
Rainfall, sensor readings, mapping, model output, work orders, customer calls, and permit conditions use different identifiers, times, owners, and quality checks.

Riverbend's Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) readings show what instruments reported. Its Geographic Information System (GIS) shows where assets are believed to be. Neither source owns the response decision. The duty manager needs the sources joined, qualified, and presented with limits.

Set up the Launch Pack

The Launch Pack is the working record you build throughout the Master Class. Chapter 00 creates the index and decision statement. Later chapters add the governance charter, decision chain, source records, issue controls, risk records, AI controls, 90-day plan, and evidence package.

Deployment Studio: create an evidence-safe workspace

Use a utility-approved repository for real records. This browser lesson stores only tracker status on this device. Do not enter customer information, credentials, protected infrastructure detail, security weaknesses, legal advice, or controlled operating data into a course field or public repository.

1. Utility context

Service type, operating scale, jurisdiction, and the decision boundary.

2. Roles

Learner, decision owner, data owner, steward, system roles, reviewer, and approver.

3. Source baseline

Method name, Version 2.3, source commit, retrieval date, local authority, and approved storage location.

4. Learning goal

The decision or governance capability you will be able to demonstrate.

5. Claims register

Believed-but-unverified statements, source needed, owner, due date, and current status.

6. Evidence rules

Naming, versioning, access, retention, review, and links to approved records.

Chapter 00 evidence gate

  • Your decision has a trigger, named user, action, consequence, and evidence problem.
  • Your Launch Pack index has an owner, version, status, approved location, and evidence-link field.
  • The source baseline records Data Ready Before AI Ready, Version 2.3, plus applicable local sources.
  • Unconfirmed statements are marked believed-but-unverified and assigned to an owner.
  • The workspace rule prevents protected or sensitive utility evidence from being copied into unsafe locations.

Leader's call

Approve the decision boundary, accountable owner, workspace, and source baseline before the team expands the Launch Pack. That small approval prevents a large body of polished work from being built around the wrong decision or an unsupported claim.

Questions learners ask before they begin

Do I need to be a data specialist?

No. Start with one utility decision you understand. The course teaches you how to name its sources, owners, controls, and evidence.

Can a small utility use this course?

Yes. One person may hold several compatible roles, but the decision authority and evidence still need names.

Does completing the course certify my utility?

No. Completion records learning and applied work. Certification or assurance requires a separately authorized scope, method, evidence review, and provider.

What makes a good capstone decision?

Choose a decision with a named user, a real consequence, and an evidence problem, such as wet-weather response or drinking-water release.

Where should I store my Launch Pack?

Use a utility-approved workspace with access, version, retention, and review controls. Do not enter protected information in the browser exercises.
