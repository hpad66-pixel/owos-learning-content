Strategy, value, and decision rights

Turn data governance into an approved utility management system. Define what it covers, which public-service outcomes it supports, how much risk the utility will tolerate, who can decide, and which funded sequence will produce measurable change.

Released lesson · criteria D01.1 through D01.5

The main lesson stays visible. The lens changes role-specific guidance. Governance starts from utility decisions and outcomes, not from a software catalog.

⚑

Riverbend, fund prediction or fix the decision foundation?

The board is considering artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted pump-failure prediction. The demonstration is convincing. Yet the same pump has competing identities across systems, work history does not join reliably, failure outcomes are inconsistently recorded, and model users have no approved action threshold. The board needs a value case and funded sequence that protects service, safety, affordability, resilience, fairness, compliance, and public trust.

D01
The strategy rule

Approve governance for the utility decisions and outcomes it must protect. State scope, principles, measures, risk boundaries, decision rights, funding, and review together. A charter without resources is an aspiration. A project list without outcomes and thresholds is an activity plan.

Strategy says what governance covers, why the utility is doing it, who decides, how success is measured, and what happens first.

Translate the charter into scoped domains, products, controls, outcome measures, risk thresholds, delegations, a funded portfolio, change triggers, and retained review evidence.

The governing body should see service and public-value outcomes, material risks, capacity, exceptions, funding choices, and evidence that management is learning and changing course.

D01.1 Scope

Approved charter, principles, minutes, and scope across information technology, operational technology, analytics, AI, and suppliers.

D01.2 Value

Outcome map and measures tied to reliability, safety, affordability, resilience, commitments, fairness, and trust.

D01.3 Risk

Risk appetite, tolerances, escalation thresholds, and accepted-risk evidence for data and AI uses.

D01.4 Rights

Delegations that separate governing, owning, stewarding, operating, advising, approving, and assuring.

D01.5 Roadmap

Funded, sequenced portfolio reviewed against outcomes, risk, capacity, adoption, and changing obligations.

1 / 3

Set scope and principles around utility decisions

The charter should cover information technology (IT), operational technology (OT), analytics, AI, records, data products, interfaces, cloud services, suppliers, and manual work where they support material utility decisions. It should name included services, domains, products, people, places, assets, records, lifecycle stages, and jurisdictions. It should also state exclusions, dependencies, and the authority for approving changes to scope.

Principles guide choices when a detailed rule does not yet exist. Riverbend might require accountable use, identified sources, one stated meaning, quality fit for purpose, minimum necessary access, security and privacy by design, retained evidence, human authority for consequential decisions, challenge and correction, supplier accountability, and measurable service value. Each principle needs an owner and a way to test whether practice matches the statement.

2 / 3

Tie governance to utility value and risk

Value is a measurable change in a utility outcome, not the number of records cataloged or people trained. Those activities can enable value, but the outcome might be fewer unplanned pump outages, faster safe response, fewer repeat customer adjustments, a reproducible filing, reduced exposure, a more affordable capital choice, or better continuity during a storm. Start with a baseline, target, owner, timeframe, data source, measurement method, decision that will change, and possible unintended effect.

Risk appetite gives management direction. A risk tolerance turns that direction into a measurable range. An escalation threshold tells people when ordinary delegation stops. A prohibited condition names what cannot proceed, such as an unidentified model making an automatic safety-critical control change.

3 / 3

Fund and govern the roadmap

Decision rights make the strategy executable. The governing body approves direction, appetite, major delegations, and oversight. Executives allocate resources and accept material business risk within authority. Owners approve domain rules and uses. Stewards maintain definitions, evidence, and issues. Operators run controls. Security, privacy, legal, compliance, records, engineering, and procurement roles decide or advise within their authority. Independent assurance tests and reports without owning management's choices.

A funded roadmap should sequence foundations, decision controls, adoption, and scaling. Each initiative needs an owner, outcome measure, risk treatment, dependencies, deliverables, staffing, supplier need, training, assurance, total cost, operating cost, start and finish conditions, and review trigger. Do not fund a model pilot without the source identity, outcome labels, action workflow, monitoring, fallback, and accountable roles needed to use it safely.

Leader's call

Ask for the line from a funded governance initiative to a changed utility decision and measurable service outcome. Then ask which risk threshold, authority, and evidence gate can stop or redirect the work. If neither line is visible, the portfolio is not yet governable.
