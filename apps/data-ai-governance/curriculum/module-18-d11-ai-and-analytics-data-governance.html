A convincing demo can still be unfit for utility work

A model predicts sewer blockages with impressive accuracy. Then the team learns that the training labels were created after the incidents, protected customer complaints were copied into a vendor workspace, and no one has defined what an operator may do with the score. You will decide whether the use can move forward.

The demo review

Approve the ninety-day field pilot?

The model reports 91 percent precision on historical incidents. The slide deck has no label definition, population coverage, data rights, temporal split, subgroup results, runtime context design, operating procedure, or monitoring plan.

1
Purpose gate

Define the decision before selecting data

Intended use should be written in operational language. Riverbend is not building “an AI for sewer maintenance.” It is testing whether a weekly risk score helps planners prioritize non-emergency inspection candidates. Operators remain responsible for work selection, safety review, access constraints, and dispatch. The score does not authorize excavation, customer contact, or an emergency classification.

This boundary affects every later choice. A planning aid can tolerate a different delay and error profile than a real-time control. False negatives may allow a blockage to remain unseen. False positives may waste crew time or concentrate inspection in already over-observed neighborhoods. The utility must name the consequence, affected people, accountable owner, and appeal or correction path before optimizing a metric.

2
Worked Riverbend evidence review

The model learned from the future

The original dataset used closed work orders to label whether a segment was “high risk.” Several fields, including final root cause and completed repair type, were created after the planner would have needed the prediction. That is target leakage. The reported accuracy describes an impossible information environment.

Riverbend rebuilds the training snapshot using only evidence available at each weekly decision point: prior cleaning, age, material, rainfall, upstream complaints with approved treatment, sensor history, and known construction. The score falls to 73 percent precision. That lower number is more honest. The team also finds that complaint-based features underrepresent renters and customers who do not report problems. They add field inspection and hydraulic evidence, document coverage, and measure performance by service area and asset class.

3
Data and model gate

Test fitness, not just volume

Data fitness asks whether the evidence represents the decision population, period, conditions, and outcome. It includes provenance, rights, lawful and ethical treatment, quality by use, missingness, measurement process, label governance, transformations, feature lineage, and exclusions. More records cannot repair a systematically wrong label.

Read the heat grid by consequence, not color alone. Each cell combines an evidence condition with the use being proposed. Select the scenario later and notice how the same model can be acceptable for a low-consequence planning aid yet unacceptable for autonomous action. The utility governs the whole use, not an isolated algorithm.

AI governance evaluates the complete decision system. A good model cannot compensate for unauthorized data or an unsafe action.

4
Runtime context gate

The model needs current, governed context

Training data prepares a model. Runtime AI context tells it what matters now. For the sewer use, context includes the asset identity, current rainfall, active road restrictions, recent inspection, operating procedure, crew capability, permissions, and the exact action being considered. A context engine is not the context itself. It assembles the package from authorized sources.

Riverbend weekly context packet
  • Planner identity and role
  • Current date and service area
  • Candidate pipe identifiers
  • Risk score and model version
  • Feature and source timestamps
  • Known limitations and confidence
  • Road, permit, and safety constraints
  • Allowed actions and escalation

Retrieval-augmented generation can bring documents or records into a model's prompt, but retrieval does not make every source correct. The system still needs authority, version, access, time, relevance, citation, and conflict rules. Generative output can vary across runs. The governed evidence and action boundary should remain inspectable even when wording changes.

5
Release gate simulation

Change the proposed use and watch the gate move

Use this phase gate as a release conversation. Select a proposed use. The board tests purpose, data, model, context, and action in order. A later pass never erases an earlier failure. The result is deliberately conservative because the lesson does not have real Riverbend evidence or authority.

Follow the proposed use through five gates. Stop where evidence, rights, or authority no longer supports it.

A release decision belongs to a particular use, population, context, action, and version. It is not a permanent approval of “the AI.”

Now test your own judgment. Choose the statement that treats accuracy as evidence while preserving the wider governance boundary. The feedback will explain why a performance result cannot by itself establish rights, representativeness, operating authority, or safe action.

Questions from AI review boards

AI and analytics governance FAQ

Is AI governance separate from data governance?

It has additional model, context, action, and monitoring concerns, but it depends on data governance. Identity, meaning, provenance, quality, rights, access, retention, and accountability remain foundational.

Does human review make a high-risk use safe?

Not by itself. Reviewers need time, competence, evidence, authority, alternatives, and a way to challenge the output. A ceremonial click is not meaningful oversight.

Why can an AI answer differ between runs?

Generative models may sample from multiple plausible outputs, and retrieved context can change. Control the evidence package, versions, permissions, tool actions, evaluation, and acceptance criteria rather than promising identical wording.

What is the difference between RAG and a knowledge graph?

Retrieval-augmented generation supplies relevant content at runtime. A knowledge graph represents identified entities and relationships with governed meaning. They can work together, but neither automatically proves a claim.

How much data is enough?

Enough means fit for the use, not simply large. Examine population, time, conditions, labels, measurement, rights, missingness, rare events, and consequences.

When must we reassess an AI use?

When purpose, population, data, label, feature, model, context source, supplier, action, law, operating process, or observed performance materially changes.

Evidence boundary. The sewer model is instructional. This lesson does not validate a model, authorize data use, establish legal or ethical compliance, or approve operational release. Apply current utility, safety, privacy, security, records, procurement, labor, accessibility, and jurisdictional review. Reference points include the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, the NIST Privacy Framework, and the ISO/IEC 42001 AI management systems overview.
