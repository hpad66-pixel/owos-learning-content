Shared identity and minimum governed records

The same pump, place, event, customer, sample, work order, obligation, evidence item, decision and outcome must remain recognizable across systems and time. Connect local identifiers through governed mappings, preserve uncertainty, and retain the smallest record that still makes the work defensible.

The identity rule

Never merge records because names look similar. Use governed identifiers, approved crosswalks and evidence-based match rules. Preserve source values, confidence and history. Send ambiguity, collision, split and merge cases to a named exception queue.

A shared identity is a bridge. Each system can keep its local number while the utility knows that the numbers refer to the same approved thing.

Control identifier format, namespace, issue authority, lifecycle, match evidence, effective time, mapping status, merge and split rules, source precedence, exceptions and measures.

Make shared identity a funded utility control, not an analyst clean-up task. Tie it to service, safety, capital, customer, regulatory and model decisions.

The same thing must mean the same thing

A governed identifier (ID) should be unique within its stated namespace, stable across ordinary name and attribute changes, never silently reused, and resolvable to an approved record. It should not encode facts likely to change unless the utility accepts the resulting lifecycle burden. Display names, labels, serial numbers and local application keys can remain useful attributes without becoming the cross-enterprise identity.

An identity bridge does not declare that one database owns every attribute. It states which records refer to the same governed entity and under what evidence. Attribute authority remains field-specific: one system may own approved geometry, another work status, another raw telemetry and another engineering configuration.

Start shared identity with assets and locations, then extend the pattern to customers and service points, samples and instruments, observations and events, work orders, obligations, evidence, decisions and outcomes. Use a distinct namespace and issue authority for each entity type. Record relationships such as located at, serves, measured by, generated from, fulfills, evidences, decided by and produced outcome. The relationship also needs identity, status, effective time, provenance and owner.

Define the minimum governed record for reconstruction

Minimum does not mean the fewest columns a system accepts. It means the smallest approved record that allows an authorized person to identify the thing or event, interpret it, connect it, evaluate its use, and reconstruct the material decision. Requirements grow with consequence, authority, complexity, uncertainty, lifecycle and downstream reuse.

Every critical record needs a common envelope: stable identity and type; version and state; effective and occurrence time; owner and responsible agent; source and creation activity; approved definition and schema; material relationships; provenance and integrity evidence; quality state and intended-use limit; rights and protection; retention and disposition; change, supersession and review. Type-specific fields then make the asset, observation, work, rule, evidence, decision or outcome understandable.

Proportionate minimum, explicit extensions. A low-consequence inspection note and a drinking-water release should not have identical requirements. Publish a common envelope, type profile and consequence-based extensions. Do not use proportionality to remove a field required by applicable law, permit, contract, safety rule, laboratory method, records schedule or approved utility policy.

Reconcile identities without replacing every system

Riverbend can implement shared identity through a governed registry, master-data service, knowledge graph, integration layer, catalog or controlled crosswalk, depending on architecture and risk. The technology is secondary to the operating rules. Each mapping should identify source namespace and local ID, governed identity, relationship status, match method, supporting evidence, confidence or quality state, steward, approval, effective time, supersession and affected uses.

Use a match ladder. First accept valid governed IDs. Second apply deterministic approved keys within a stated scope. Third use approved composite rules, such as serial plus commissioned location plus asset class. Fourth allow probabilistic or artificial-intelligence suggestions only as review inputs where approved. Never let a threshold silently convert an uncertain high-consequence case into a merge. Measure precision as well as coverage because a false match can be worse than an unresolved one.

Chapter 05 evidence gate

  • Every governed identifier has a namespace, issue authority, uniqueness, stability, non-reuse and lifecycle rule.
  • Local identifiers remain preserved with field-specific authority and versioned, time-bounded mappings.
  • Automatic matching is limited to approved rules, tested evidence and acceptable consequence.
  • Conflicts, duplicates, orphans, merge and split cases enter a named steward queue with no forced guess.
  • Minimum records combine a common envelope, type-specific content, authority requirements and consequence extensions.
  • A qualified alternate can trace an identified event through work, evidence, decision and outcome, and can explain a later correction.

Leader's call

Ask how many high-consequence decisions depend on analyst-created identity joins. Turn the recurring joins into a governed shared service with steward capacity, measurable precision and a visible exception queue. Do not reward a high match rate that hides false merges.

Chapter 05 questions

Must every system use one identifier?

No. Preserve local identifiers and govern their mappings to a shared identity.

Can fuzzy matching approve a merge?

Only under tested, approved rules and acceptable consequence. Otherwise queue it.

What happens after a split or merge?

Retain history, effective time, authority, and affected relationships.

What is a minimum governed record?

The smallest record that preserves identity, meaning, authority, evidence, and change.

Why preserve source values?

They are needed for traceability, correction, and later reconstruction.
