The incident ticket

What do you tell Customer Service?

The warehouse contains today’s rate table and today’s customer records. The invoice PDF shows the disputed amount, but the transformation run, manual exception file, and approval record are missing.

The reconstruction question

Which exact records and events produced this bill?

Keep that question visible. It prevents the investigation from drifting into a general diagram of systems.

1 | Three related ideas

Route, history, and the ability to follow

Data lineage shows the route from source to use. Provenance records what happened to a specific entity or record, when it happened, which version was involved, and which person or system performed or authorized the activity. Traceability is the ability to use that evidence in both directions.

A pipeline picture can show that billing reads customer and rate data. That is useful architecture lineage. It does not prove which customer row, rate-table version, code release, exception spreadsheet, execution time, and approval produced one disputed bill. Decision-grade lineage combines the route with run-level provenance and retained evidence.

Do not confuse a current diagram with a historical replay. A diagram says how the process is designed. A replay shows what actually ran for the decision being challenged.

The disputed amount becomes reproducible only after the manual exception is included. That is the practical point: manual work is not outside lineage. If it changes the answer, it belongs in the evidence path with identity, time, reason, authority, before-and-after values, and retention.

2 | Rebuild the broken path

Watch the proof assemble one event at a time

This network is a reconstruction, not a decoration. Step through it from the retained invoice to the source snapshots and then forward through calculation, review, and customer action. Watch for the agent and activity nodes. They explain who or what changed the records and why the resulting bill can be defended or corrected.

Blue nodes are records, gold nodes are activities, and green nodes are agents. A complete proof connects all three.

Lineage becomes decision evidence when it identifies the exact versions and run events, including manual changes and authority.

Debrief. If the code release or exception approval is absent, the route may still look complete while the bill remains unreproducible. Record-level gaps should be visible, not smoothed over by a polished diagram.

3 | Govern the handoffs

Carry the proof with the answer

Every handoff should carry enough context for the next person to use the result without inventing meaning. At minimum, include the decision or purpose, record identity, source and version, transformation or activity, time, quality state, limitations, authority, and escalation route.

Switch between the fragmented and governed handoffs. Step from metering to billing to Customer Service. Notice how the governed path does not merely send more data. It sends the identifiers, limitations, run evidence, and authority needed to interpret and challenge the result. At each handoff, ask whether the next role could reproduce the answer without calling the previous person for missing context.

Step across the same organizational route in both modes. Compare the information needed to reproduce, challenge, and correct the answer.

A governed handoff preserves the proof and the limits of the answer, not only the value.

Reconstruction check

Put a defensible replay in order

This ordering check tests investigation discipline. Start with the challenged output and its exact time. Then recover the actual run, inputs, activities, agents, and outcome. A generic source-to-report diagram is not enough.

Questions from the investigation room

Lineage and provenance FAQ

Is lineage the same as an audit log?

No. An audit log records events in a system. Lineage connects records across sources, transformations, uses, and outcomes. Logs may supply provenance evidence, but they must be linked to the entities and decision being reconstructed.

How detailed should lineage be?

Match detail to consequence. A planning trend may need dataset and transformation lineage. A disputed bill, permit filing, safety decision, or automated action may require record-level versions, activities, agents, approvals, and replay.

What if part of the process is a spreadsheet?

Include it. Record the file identity and version, source inputs, formulas or logic, person making the change, review, approval, output, access, and retention. Calling it manual does not remove it from the evidence chain.

Can we reconstruct lineage after an incident?

Sometimes, but missing versions and logs may make full replay impossible. Preserve what exists, document the gap and consequence, correct the control, and avoid claiming more certainty than the evidence supports.

Does blockchain solve provenance?

It may protect some recorded events from alteration, but it does not prove that the original value, identity, meaning, authority, or human action was correct. Good capture and governance still come first.

Who owns lineage?

Ownership is shared by job, not ambiguous. Data and decision owners define required evidence. Stewards maintain meaning and trace rules. Technical custodians capture system events. Process owners control manual activities. Assurance independently tests selected traces.

Evidence boundary and release truth. Riverbend billing facts are instructional. This lesson does not prove a billing amount, legal obligation, compliance state, or assurance conclusion. Use approved source systems, current retention and privacy rules, authorized reviewers, and utility-specific evidence. Technical anchors include the World Wide Web Consortium PROV-O Recommendation, the PROV data model, and National Institute of Standards and Technology Special Publication 800-53 Revision 5. D04 criteria remain subject to source-owner and practitioner review.
