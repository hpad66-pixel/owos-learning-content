Sharing is a designed relationship, not a file transfer

A smart-meter vendor asks Riverbend for the full interval dataset. The promised benefit sounds useful. The request says nothing about customer purpose, training reuse, hosting location, subcontractors, deletion, or what happens when the contract ends. You will turn that vague request into a governed decision.

Enter the negotiation
Working lesson | publication and credential events remain disabled
The supplier is waiting

What do you authorize today?

The vendor wants three years of fifteen-minute meter readings, customer identifiers, service addresses, and outage flags. Its proposal says the data may improve a forecasting product used by other clients.

1 | Start with purpose

Open, shared, and closed are decisions, not personality traits

Utilities sometimes treat openness as automatically good and restriction as automatically safe. Neither shortcut works. Open data can create public value, transparency, research, and innovation. It can also reveal vulnerable customers, security-sensitive locations, or misleading measurements. Controlled sharing can support mutual aid, regional planning, contractors, regulators, and research while preserving accountability. Closed access may be right for privileged, security-sensitive, or highly identifying information.

The first question is not, “Can we send it?” Ask, “What public or operational purpose are we trying to achieve, and what is the least data needed?” That is data minimization. A forecasting pilot may need feeder-level aggregates, weather, and a short historical window. It may not need names, exact addresses, or unrestricted reuse.

Purpose must travel with the data. A receiving party should not have to guess why the utility shared it, what it may do, what it must not do, and when that permission ends.
Worked Riverbend negotiation

Reduce the request without reducing the value

Riverbend restates the pilot as: “Test whether weather and feeder-level consumption improve next-day peak forecasts during the summer operating period.” The team removes customer names and addresses, aggregates readings to feeder and hour, excludes medically vulnerable customer indicators, supplies twelve months instead of three years, and creates a controlled workspace. The vendor may perform the pilot but may not train a general product, combine the data with brokered household information, or move it to an unapproved region.

The utility also names a business owner, security reviewer, privacy reviewer, contract manager, and technical custodian. Results must include lineage, quality limitations, model inputs, and deletion evidence. If the pilot succeeds, a new approval is required for production. The smaller dataset still answers the pilot question. Governance did not stop innovation. It made the experiment specific enough to evaluate.

2 | Draw the benefit-risk boundary

Classify the sharing case before writing contract prose

Read this matrix as a conversation starter, not an automatic legal answer. Begin with the intended benefit on one axis and the potential harm or obligation on the other. Then inspect the actual fields, granularity, identities, locations, recipients, combinations, and time period. The same dataset can move between cells when the purpose or detail changes.

High benefit, lower riskPublished treatment-plant energy totals with suppression and explanatory metadata.
High benefit, higher riskDetailed interval data for outage prediction. Use controlled access, minimization, review, and strong exit terms.

Lower benefit, higher riskCustomer-level data for unrelated product training. Stop unless a valid purpose and authority are established.

Locate the purpose first, then test whether identity, granularity, combination, location, and duration increase the consequence.

A sharing category is not permanent. Change the purpose, recipient, precision, or location and the classification must be reviewed again.

3 | Negotiate the complete relationship

Try three supplier positions

Data sovereignty is not simply the country printed on a data-center brochure. It includes jurisdiction, access, support personnel, subprocessors, backups, remote administration, and compelled disclosure. Exit readiness asks whether Riverbend can leave without losing history, meaning, security, or operational capability.

Use the gate board to expose what each supplier statement leaves unresolved. Select a position and read the four control results from purpose through exit. A green result does not excuse a red result. The sequence matters because the utility should not negotiate formatting details for a use that lacks authority in the first place.

Read left to right: authority and purpose, minimum data, location and supplier chain, then return or verified destruction.

Which condition must be resolved first?

This is a sequencing judgment. Select the condition that decides whether the proposed sharing has a legitimate, specific job. Final applied check

Questions heard in procurement rooms

Sharing and sovereignty FAQ

Does open data mean publishing every field?

No. Open publication needs purpose, lawful authority, quality explanation, privacy and security review, licensing, accessibility, update commitments, and a process for correction. Aggregation or suppression may preserve value while reducing harm.

Is de-identification enough for meter data?

Not automatically. Fine-grained time, location, household pattern, and linkage to other datasets can create re-identification risk. Test the method and control combinations, recipients, and use.

What if the supplier says it never owns our data?

Ownership language does not answer use rights. Ask whether the supplier may access, derive from, train on, retain, disclose, combine, benchmark, or transfer the information and its outputs.

Must utility data stay in one country?

The answer depends on law, contract, infrastructure classification, policy, risk, and operating need. Map hosting, backups, support, subcontractors, remote access, and transfer rather than relying on a single location label.

When should we test exit?

Before commitment and periodically afterward. Export representative data with metadata and configurations, verify completeness and usability, test deletion evidence, and confirm continuity for critical services.

Can a vendor reuse our data to improve its product?

Only when the utility has deliberately approved that purpose and the necessary authority, customer treatment, rights, safeguards, benefit, evidence, and exit terms. Product improvement is not a harmless default.

Evidence boundary. Riverbend and the vendor are instructional. This lesson does not determine legal authority, public-records duties, data residency, privacy compliance, critical-infrastructure restrictions, procurement terms, or a valid transfer mechanism. Use current utility counsel, privacy, security, records, procurement, operations, and jurisdictional review. Reference points include the NIST Privacy Framework, the CISA critical infrastructure resources, and the W3C ODRL Information Model.
