Map the utility data estate around decisions

A useful inventory is not a list of applications. It is a living map of the physical observations, operational technology, information technology, models, files, people, suppliers, interfaces and rules that support a material utility decision.

Released lesson · deployable estate map

The main lesson stays visible. The lens changes role-specific guidance. Start with three to five decisions, not an enterprise-wide catalog project.

⚑

Riverbend, capital renewal decision

The utility must decide which pumps, mains and facilities enter the five-year capital plan. The apparent source is the enterprise asset management (EAM) system. The actual decision also depends on geographic information system (GIS) geometry, work orders, failure history, inspections, supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) telemetry, advanced metering infrastructure (AMI) demand, hydraulic-model versions, customer consequence, water-quality context, climate stress, cost estimates, funding conditions, permits and prior commitments. Several links are exports, email attachments and analyst workbooks.

2
The mapping rule

Ask one material decision question. Follow every material fact backward to source and forward to outcome. Record the system, interface, dataset, supplier, jurisdiction, owner, manual step, version, time, quality and evidence at each stop.

Map what people really use, including field notes and spreadsheets. A box called "asset system" is not enough to explain a renewal recommendation.

Use stable identifiers for systems, interfaces, datasets, suppliers, decisions and evidence. Distinguish the system of record, authoritative source for a stated field, derived product and working copy.

Sponsor a bounded decision inventory and require owners to demonstrate one reconstructed decision. Fund the interfaces and stewardship gaps that repeatedly block high-consequence choices.

Map the physical and digital estate together

The utility data estate begins before a database. A pressure value starts with a physical condition, sensing point, instrument, calibration and time source. It may pass through operational technology (OT) and then through information technology (IT), including a programmable logic controller, historian, network, integration service, cloud platform, model and dashboard. An operator may add a field observation. An analyst may join it to an asset table. A manager may use the result with cost, customer and permit evidence.

Record seven object types: system or device; interface or handoff; dataset or record; supplier or external party; jurisdiction or authority link; decision or use; and outcome or obligation. For each, capture stable identity, name, description, owner, steward or support, location, environment, criticality, sensitivity, authoritative scope, upstream and downstream links, version, lifecycle, evidence and review date. Add manual steps even when the preferred future state is automation.

Identity and scope

Stable ID, object type, name, description, environment, location and authoritative scope.

Accountability

Owner, steward, custodian, supplier, decision authority, support and escalation.

Data and meaning

Dataset, field, identifier, definition, unit, schema, temporal and spatial reference.

Connections

Source, target, interface, method, frequency, manual work, transformation and dependency.

Risk and rights

Criticality, sensitivity, purpose, access, sharing, residency, security and consequence.

Quality and evidence

Use rule, validation, observability, provenance, issue, exception and control result.

Lifecycle

Version, effective time, change, retention, archive, disposal, recovery and exit.

Authority and review

Jurisdiction, applicable records, contracts, permits, owner review and next event.

2 / 3

Govern the minimum utility domains

A data domain helps Riverbend organize ownership and meaning across systems. It is not the same as an application. Asset identity may appear in geographic information, enterprise asset management, telemetry, drawings, models, work records and supplier files. The domain owns the shared meaning and rules; systems operate and store particular records.

At minimum, cover assets; telemetry and observations; water quality; customers and accounts; work and workforce activity; environment and climate; regulation and commitments; finance and commercial records; analytics, models and artificial intelligence. Add local domains where the utility's services require them. Name cross-domain relationships because high-value decisions usually cross several domains.

Authoritative for what? Avoid naming one system as the source of truth for an entire domain. GIS may be authoritative for approved geometry, EAM for work status, a historian for raw telemetry, a laboratory system for released results and a contract repository for the executed agreement. Record the field, scope, time and approval state for every authoritative claim.
3 / 3

Start with three to five material decisions

An enterprise inventory can consume months while the most consequential joins remain unknown. Begin with three to five decisions selected for consequence, uncertainty, change pressure and reconstruction difficulty. Include at least one operational or public-service decision, one planning or investment decision, and one sharing, analytics or artificial-intelligence decision when those uses are material.

Materiality is not only financial. A low-cost data gap can affect public health, environmental release, worker safety, critical service or public trust. Use a transparent selection method, then obtain leader approval and domain-owner confirmation.

For each selected decision, write one question in the form: "Who decides what, by when, using which governed evidence, under which authority, with which alternatives, uncertainty and consequence?" Then conduct a ninety-minute mapping session with the decision owner, domain stewards, operators, engineers, information technology, operational technology, records, security, privacy, finance, procurement and suppliers as needed. Bring real approved artifacts. Mark unknown, disputed, manual and inaccessible elements rather than smoothing them away.

Chapter 04 evidence gate

  • Three to five decisions are selected through transparent materiality and mandatory gates.
  • Each decision has an authority, deadline, evidence need, alternatives, uncertainty and consequence.
  • The map begins with physical reality and includes field, operational, enterprise, model, cloud, file, supplier and manual objects.
  • Every material interface shows source, target, method, identity, owner, schedule, version, failure handling and evidence.
  • Minimum utility domains and cross-domain relationships have named owners and authoritative scope.
  • Unknowns and disputes remain visible in an owned exception queue, and a qualified alternate has tested reconstruction.

Leader's call

Ask to see the decision map before approving another platform integration. If the team cannot show the physical source, shared identity, material transformations, authority, uncertainty, owner and outcome, the new connection may automate ambiguity.
