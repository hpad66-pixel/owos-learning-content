Standards, law, and the applicability record

A utility needs many authorities, but they do not all carry the same force or perform the same job. Build one controlled system that shows what governs, what guides, what has been incorporated, who reviewed it, and which evidence supports the conclusion.

Riverbend, cloud leak-detection pilot

A vendor will combine smart-meter intervals, customer-account attributes, work history, pressure telemetry, and geographic information system records in a cloud service. Procurement calls the pilot innovative. Operations wants results next month. Privacy, public-records, cybersecurity, procurement, retention, smart-meter, artificial-intelligence, contract, and local policy questions arrive in one email. The team cannot safely turn that email into a checklist until it separates the authorities and proves which ones cover this organization, service, system, data, people, location, and use.

1
The governing rule for this chapter

Classify the authority, verify the current controlled source, run the applicability gate on the facts, obtain the required reviewer, retain the basis, and only then translate an applicable requirement into a control and test.

You do not need to memorize every acronym. Learn to ask two questions: "What job does this source perform?" and "What makes it apply here?"

Design a register that preserves the exact source, edition, jurisdiction, incorporation path, factual trigger, reviewer, conclusion, evidence, exceptions, controls, and next review. A link alone is not an applicability record.

Authorize one cross-functional gate. Legal or regulatory counsel owns legal conclusions; security, privacy, records, procurement, engineering, operations, and data owners contribute facts and control design. No maturity score substitutes for that gate.

1 / 3

Use standards as one control system

Standards and frameworks are useful because they divide a large governance problem into repeatable jobs. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) publish standards across governance, security, privacy, records, assets, and operational technology. The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) publishes specifications for interoperable web data, provenance, catalogs, vocabularies, and policy expression. The United States National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) publishes risk and control frameworks and guidance. Water, wastewater, electric, environmental, records, privacy, procurement, and public-sector authorities add sector and jurisdiction context.

Do not combine those sources into one undifferentiated list. Use each for its job, map overlaps to common utility controls, and keep the source-specific wording and evidence. A source can inform governance without being binding. A source can also become binding through law, regulation, permit, contract, license, grant, tariff, procurement term, or adopted policy. The incorporation path must be visible.

2 / 3

Separate obligations, commitments, guidance, and policy signals

An authority register says what each source is, who issued or adopted it, which edition is controlled, and what job it performs. An applicability record says whether and how it covers a specific factual scope. A standards-to-control crosswalk says how applicable requirements and adopted guidance are implemented and tested.

The order matters. If the team scores a requirement before checking applicability, a polished dashboard can misstate duties. If it maps several sources straight to separate controls, the same access, retention, change, incident, supplier, or provenance control may be built five times. First preserve the authority-specific obligation. Then map common outcomes to one controlled implementation, with source-specific differences attached.

Binding law

Applicable constitutional, statutory, regulatory, order, or other public-law duty.

Permit or license

Conditions attached to an authorized service, discharge, facility, profession, method, or activity.

Contract or program

Executed procurement, grant, consent, insurance, accreditation, or other commitment.

Licensed standard

Controlled normative text, with edition and incorporation path recorded.

Adopted policy

Board, executive, delegated, or procedural rule within its authorized scope.

Framework or guidance

Structured practice used to design, assess, compare, or improve.

Policy signal

Proposal or direction tracked for readiness, not represented as current law.

Legal gate, not legal automation. Search, catalogs, artificial intelligence, and knowledge graphs can find candidates, compare text, flag changes, and assemble evidence. They should not make an unreviewed legal conclusion. The responsible reviewer must use current official or licensed text and verified facts.
3 / 3

Build and operate the applicability record

One authority may produce many applicability records because coverage differs by organization, service, system, data, person, location, trigger, and time. One cloud pilot may also need many authority records. Do not hide that many-to-many relationship in a single yes or no field. Record a conclusion such as applies, does not apply, partially applies, incorporated by another authority, superseded, future effective, or review required, together with the basis and approved scope.

At minimum, capture a stable record identifier; organization; service or system; data or person; location; factual trigger; authority and exact source; effective date; applicability decision; accountable owner; legal or authorized reviewer; evidence; next review; and status. Add requirement, control, test, exception, conflict, change event, and supersession links when the register moves into operation.

Chapter 03 evidence gate

  • Every source has a controlled identity, class, issuer, jurisdiction, edition, status, source and owner.
  • Binding authority, private commitment, adopted policy, licensed standard, guidance and policy signal are not collapsed.
  • Every applicability conclusion identifies exact organization, service, system, data, person, location, trigger and time.
  • The authorized reviewer, factual basis, cited authority and retained evidence are linked.
  • Requirements are scored only after applicability review and are mapped to controls, tests, evidence, gaps and exceptions.
  • Scheduled and event-driven change rules preserve prior decisions and successor links.

Leader's call

Stop asking for one universal compliance score. Ask for the three records: the controlled authority, the reviewed applicability conclusion, and the tested control evidence. Make uncertainty visible, assign a reviewer, and fund the change cycle.

Chapter 03 questions

Is a standard the same as a law?

No. It can become mandatory through law, permit, contract, incorporation, or policy, but that must be verified.

Can the course decide legal applicability?

No. The course structures the record. An authorized reviewer must determine applicability for the actual facts.

Why record the edition?

Requirements change. The edition and effective date show which text supported the conclusion.

What if coverage is uncertain?

Record “review needed,” name the reviewer and due date, and do not score the obligation as satisfied.

How often should the record be reviewed?

Use a scheduled review plus event triggers such as new law, changed purpose, new supplier, incident, or jurisdiction change.
