# BI, RAG, Graph, and Agentic Comparison Direction

## Original direction

Can you compare and contrast existing processes where we use data, we connect data, we join tables, we do Power BI tools, and then we show something? Obviously, over here, even if everything is the same and we have anomaly detections, everything is done differently because of reasoning semantics, deterministic vs. indeterministic, which is what RAG does.

Do a beautiful table, show examples, simulate it with examples, and show how you get a different response every time and why. If we do this, then connect it to the agentic application, and show how this then connects to an agentic application.

A lot of examples will be needed here: simulations of a real graph, even if it's a small graph. Show how the graphs are connected and bring it all together. That's practical examples where knowledge of an enterprise, knowledge of an individual, or knowledge of a company, all of that can be captured.

What are some of the solutions by simulating and showing that with small examples, and simulating how knowledge is captured, how it can work, and why an RDF graph is important? In great detail, you have to show the triples, subject-predicate-object, and work with examples in the utility sector.

## Technical interpretation to review

The course should compare three different mechanisms without presenting them as mutually exclusive:

1. A modeled business-intelligence path using tables, joins, measures, and dashboards.
2. Retrieval-augmented generation using retrieved passages plus probabilistic language generation.
3. A graph-grounded agentic application using explicit relationships, formal rules or inference where appropriate, validation, provenance, and human approval.

The lesson must not teach that all business intelligence is deterministic, all RAG is indeterministic, or all knowledge-graph applications are deterministic. Repeatability depends on the complete pipeline, including data versions, retrieval, query logic, rules, model settings, tools, and output generation.
