01
Workshop opening

The team agrees on the words and disagrees on the decision

Customer service says active means an open account. Billing says it means a billable agreement. Operations says a premise currently receiving service. Emergency management needs a contactable occupied location inside the affected zone. One label is carrying four decisions.

Do not begin by voting on a universal definition. Begin with the question the utility must answer, the date and jurisdiction, and the consequence of being wrong.

02
Question funnel

Competency questions define what the model must support

A competency question is a plain-language test for the model. "Which active critical customers are exposed to Pressure Event 77?" requires customer, service agreement, served premise, pressure zone, event, status, time, and criticality.

It does not automatically require every customer preference, payment history, meter register, or engineering drawing. Those may matter elsewhere. Scope follows the approved question.

Which concepts are required to answer which active hospital accounts are exposed to Pressure Event 77?

Context boundary check

Select every item the bounded task needs. Leave irrelevant or unauthorized material outside the packet.

03
Bounded Ontology Workshop

Include, exclude, and escalate meanings deliberately

Review each candidate meaning. Include it when the competency question requires it and the term can be defined. Exclude it when it belongs outside this module. Escalate it when domain authority or evidence is unresolved.

The goal is not the largest model. It is the smallest defensible slice that answers the questions, survives examples, and makes unresolved choices visible.

04
Practitioner view

Reuse what fits. Do not inherit meanings blindly.

An upper ontology can provide broad organizing concepts. A domain ontology can provide reusable industry meaning. An application profile can select and constrain what one implementation needs. Alignment records how concepts correspond.

Reuse saves work only when definitions, commitments, identifiers, licensing, governance, and change cadence fit. Importing a respected ontology does not make every class relevant or correct for your question.

05
Model lifecycle

Draft, test, review, version, and release

Test the slice against positive examples, counterexamples, edge cases, and the original competency questions. A model that cannot distinguish an inactive account from an active service agreement is not ready for exposure decisions.

Domain experts approve meaning. Technical reviewers check formal behavior. Data stewards check mappings and identifiers. Product owners approve the intended use. Publish versions, deprecations, migration notes, and correction routes.

Match each release job to its strongest reviewer.

Relationship matching

Match each ordinary-language question to the job that answers it. Check all rows together, then revise any row that needs another look.

06
Professional application

Produce a decision record another team can reopen

Document the question, model boundary, included concepts and relationships, exclusions, unresolved issues, reuse choices, examples, owner, and version.

A good decision record explains why the slice is shaped this way. It gives a future team enough evidence to challenge, extend, or retire it without reconstructing the workshop from memory.

Questions people actually ask

Can an ontology be wrong?

Yes. Definitions can be unclear

How much should we model?

Model enough to answer approved questions and support intended reuse. Record exclusions and expand through governed changes.

Should we reuse an industry ontology?

Evaluate semantic fit

Is a glossary an ontology?

A glossary supplies definitions. An ontology also represents concepts

Does the ontology move data?

No. Mappings

Module Glossary

ontology

A shared model of concepts relationships and selected semantic rules.

competency question

A question used to test whether a model supports its intended job.

ontology boundary

The concepts uses and meanings explicitly included or excluded.

upper ontology

A reusable model of very general concepts.

domain ontology

A model for concepts and relationships in a particular field.

application profile

A selected and constrained use of terms for one implementation.

alignment

A recorded correspondence between concepts in different models.

axiom

A formal statement accepted within an ontology.

deprecation

A governed indication that a term should no longer be used in new work.
