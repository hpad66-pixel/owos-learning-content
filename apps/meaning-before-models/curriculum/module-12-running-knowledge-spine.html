Running a Knowledge Spine

A knowledge graph becomes enterprise infrastructure when it connects governed meaning to real records, documents, applications, analytics, and agents every day. The goal is not to move everything into one graph. The goal is to resolve meaning and evidence across the utility with explicit controls.

Cross-system service question

Pump P-104 may fail tonight. Who and what could be affected?

The asset system knows the pump and open work orders. GIS knows the pressure zone and network. The customer platform knows premises and critical-customer designations. The document repository holds the current contingency procedure. The historian has current readings. No single table owns the answer.

A knowledge spine lets the utility ask one bounded question across those sources while preserving their ownership. It supplies shared identifiers, ontology meaning, mappings, validation, provenance, and governed access. The answer can then serve a dashboard, an application, a data steward, or an AI agent.

1
Operating definition

The spine is instantiated, queryable, governed, and connected

An ontology in a slide deck can help people agree. A running knowledge spine goes further: its ontology is instantiated, its mappings connect to real data, and its controls run during publication or query.

A semantic mapping might connect the CMMS asset row to util:Pump and the GIS service-area feature to util:PressureZone. Federation can combine source-backed answers. Virtualization can expose mapped relational data as RDF at query time.

Some information will be materialized. Some documents will be indexed for retrieval and have selected entities or clauses extracted. A context engine uses the spine to assemble AI context. The context is the package; the engine is the mechanism that assembles it.

A service-level objective makes the spine operable. If the critical-customer mapping is stale or the procedure source is unavailable, the answer path should degrade safely and say what is missing.

Architecture rule: Move the least data that meets the workload. Virtualize before replicating where practical, materialize hot or resilient paths when evidence justifies it, and preserve governance in either case.

Live answer route

Operate the path from question to evidence package

An agent asks, “If P-104 becomes unavailable, which critical facilities may be affected, what open work exists, and which approved response applies?” The spine should identify the pump, traverse its service relationships, retrieve current customer designations, find open work, retrieve the applicable procedure, validate required fields, enforce permissions, and return citations and uncertainty.

The language model may summarize that package. Deterministic graph traversal, access policy, validation, and stop conditions should constrain what it may claim or do. The agentic application is the consumer and workflow coordinator. The spine is the governed knowledge and evidence service underneath it.

Operations FAQ

Questions about running the spine

Is a knowledge spine just a large enterprise knowledge graph?

No. A graph may be part of it. The spine also includes ontology governance, mappings, query and reasoning services, validation, provenance, access policy, reliability, and operating ownership across sources and consumers.

Must all utility data be copied into a graph database?

No. Virtual mappings and federation can expose selected data in place. Materialize where latency, resilience, history, cost, or reasoning requirements justify a governed copy. Measure the workload before choosing.

How is this different from a semantic layer for BI?

A BI semantic layer usually standardizes business measures and dimensions for analytics. A knowledge spine can connect broader domain relationships, documents, provenance, ontology rules, applications, and agent context. The two can integrate rather than compete.

What happens when a source system is down?

The spine needs dependency health, cached-data policy, freshness indicators, and safe fallback. A response should name missing evidence and stop claims or actions that depend on it.

Who owns the enterprise ontology?

Use shared governance with domain ownership. Domain teams own water, wastewater, stormwater, customer, asset, and regulatory meaning. A shared core coordinates identifiers and cross-domain concepts. A platform team operates services.

How does the spine reduce hallucination?

It supplies task-specific evidence, definitions, provenance, and controls. That reduces ambiguity and unsupported generation. It does not make a generative model perfectly deterministic, so deterministic checks and human authority still matter.

Evidence boundary

The P-104 service-impact architecture is illustrative. Product selection, network design, performance, security, cost, source-system capability, and regulatory controls require local engineering. Virtualization is not always preferable, and materialization is not always wasteful. The workload and governance determine the right pattern.
