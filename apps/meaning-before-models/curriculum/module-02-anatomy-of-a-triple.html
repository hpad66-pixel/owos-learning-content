Anatomy of a Triple

A triple has only three positions. The hard part is deciding whether each position names a thing, a relationship, or a value, and then writing that decision without changing its meaning.

Bench ticket 204

A sentence can sound right and still be unusable

A laboratory analyst says, “Sample 44 has 0.2.” A person in the room may know that 0.2 means milligrams per liter of nitrate, measured on a particular date, from a particular location. A machine does not receive any of that shared background.

The first question is not “How do we turn the sentence into code?” The first question is “What exactly are we claiming?” A good triple is intentionally small. It should not hide several relationships inside one free-text object.

Separate the thing being described, the relationship, and the thing or value on the other end.

Decide whether the object needs an identifier, a typed literal, a unit relationship, a language tag, or a separate observation node.

Ask which shortcuts create downstream interpretation work and who owns the vocabulary used for the relationship.

Read the ticket like an investigator

Choose the most serious defect first. Then compare the three positions in the table. The point is not to memorize punctuation. It is to notice when one short sentence is carrying more meaning than its structure can safely hold.

B
Repair bay

Four defects, four different repairs

An Internationalized Resource Identifier (IRI) identifies a thing or property. A label helps people read it. They are not interchangeable. Two pumps may both be labelled “High Service Pump 1,” while their identifiers remain different.

A value can also carry a datatype. The text “12” and the number 12 may look alike on a screen but behave differently in a filter or calculation. A language tag distinguishes an English definition from its Spanish translation.

A blank node can represent a local structure without giving it a global name. Use it carefully. If a sampling location, pump, or permit clause must be reconciled across systems, it usually needs a stable identifier.

Serialization window

The statement stays; the writing format changes

A serialization is a way to write the graph. Turtle is readable for people. JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data (JSON-LD) fits many web applications. RDF/XML is another standard syntax. The same graph can be expressed in more than one serialization.

Changing the serialization should not change the underlying statement. Think of it like writing the same valve identifier on a map, in an asset register, and in an application message. The formats differ. The identity and relationship must survive.

Prepare a reviewed Triple Deck

A deck is a small review package, not a claim that your organization has finished an ontology. Include a relationship between two resources, a statement with a typed value, and one statement whose source or authority still needs review. That mix forces you to distinguish syntax quality from evidence quality.

Write for the next reviewer

Use Turtle-style statements, but add plain-English intent and the source boundary. A syntactically clean triple can still be unsupported, stale, or mapped to the wrong record. The deck should make that uncertainty visible.

Workbench FAQ

Questions about triple anatomy

Can a triple have more than three parts?

No. A triple always has subject, predicate, and object. If an observation needs value, unit, method, time, and reviewer, represent the observation as a resource and connect those details with additional triples.

Why are RDF identifiers so long?

Full IRIs are globally scoped so two organizations are less likely to name different things identically. Turtle prefixes let people write a shorter form such as ex:Pump_P104 while preserving the full identifier underneath.

When should the object be a resource instead of text?

Use a resource when the object has its own identity or relationships. Pressure Zone 3 should usually be a resource because it contains premises and is served by assets. A short operator note may remain a text literal.

Are blank nodes bad?

No. They are useful for local structures that do not need a shared identity. They become a problem when teams later need to reconcile that unnamed thing across laboratory, asset, geographic, or regulatory records.

Does valid Turtle mean the data is correct?

No. Valid syntax means a parser can read the file. It does not prove that Pump P-104 serves Zone 3, that the source is current, or that the relationship was approved. Syntax, validation, provenance, and truth are different review jobs.

Evidence boundary

The laboratory and pump statements are instructional examples. Standards define RDF terms and serialization behavior. Utility adoption still requires source, mapping, security, and practitioner review.
