Pressure Event 771

Which definition controls the answer?

Pressure drops in Zone 3. Operations asks for active critical-facility customers who may be exposed and the procedure that applies now. The Customer Information System knows accounts and service status. The Geographic Information System connects premises to the zone. Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition records the event. The policy library contains several procedure versions.

No single table owns the meaning of “active critical-facility customer exposed now.” The utility needs structure from source systems, shared definitions, approved mappings, current policy, user permission, and a human decision boundary.

Inspect schema, vocabulary, ontology, mapping, policy, time, provenance, and runtime assembly as separate contracts.

Assign owners for definitions, mappings, policy, permissions, and operational decisions. One platform should not quietly own every job.

Separate the jobs

Five questions prevent five category errors

A data model answers, “How is information structured for this system or use?” The customer system has account tables, fields, keys, and service-status codes. Its source schema is part of that job.

A taxonomy answers, “How are terms classified?” It may arrange customer classes into residential, commercial, industrial, and critical facility.

An ontology answers, “What do the concepts and relationships mean?” It distinguishes customer, account, premise, service agreement, facility, pressure event, and exposure.

A semantic layer answers, “How does that meaning connect to authoritative data?” It maps ActiveServiceAgreement to approved customer fields and effective-date logic through a semantic mapping.

Artificial intelligence context answers, “What does the model or agent need for this task now?” It contains Event 771, current evidence, jurisdiction, policy, user permission, unresolved conflicts, and output limits.

Context assembly

A context engine assembles context; it is not the context

A context engine performs work. It receives a task and user identity, resolves entities, retrieves evidence, applies policy and permission filters, checks freshness, preserves conflicts, and assembles a bounded package. The resulting package is the AI context.

A long prompt is not automatically good context. Good context is relevant, sourced, current enough for the task, permitted for the user, explicit about uncertainty, and constrained to allowed outputs and actions.

Five-layer FAQ

Questions that keep these terms separate

Is a semantic layer the same as an ontology?

No. The ontology defines shared concepts and relationships. The semantic layer connects that meaning to real fields, tables, metrics, interfaces, and source rules.

Is a taxonomy enough for artificial intelligence?

A taxonomy helps classification, but it normally does not express the richer relationships, mappings, evidence, policy, and runtime controls needed for a utility decision.

Is the prompt the AI context?

The prompt may carry part of the context. The full package can also include retrieved evidence, definitions, permissions, policy, tool boundaries, workflow state, and output constraints supplied through structured channels.

Can good context make a generative model deterministic?

No. It can reduce ambiguity and unsupported answers. Repeatability also depends on retrieval, ranking, model, decoding, tools, and external state. Module 17 compares those sources of variation.

Who owns the five layers?

Ownership is normally distributed. Source-system owners govern structure, domain stewards govern definitions, mapping owners connect sources, policy owners govern applicable rules, and operational authorities decide or approve actions.

Evidence boundary

Pressure Event 771 is instructional. The distinctions synthesize W3C semantic standards and enterprise architecture practice. They do not prescribe a vendor product or operational procedure.
