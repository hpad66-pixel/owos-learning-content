# OWOS Module Design Brief: Five Layers of Meaning

Retrofit implementation: decision room using `editorial-illustration`, `comparison-table`, and
`packet-anatomy`. The assessment sequence is `definition-authority`, `five-layer-classification`,
`failure-trace`, and `decision-defense`. The learner classifies artifacts by job and removes runtime
context controls to observe distinct failures. The rebuilt composition preserves the original
pressure-event teaching strength without preserving the former golden-lesson page mold.

Candidate status: rebuilt for working review on 2026-07-23. Hardeep Anand approved production of
the candidate, not its designation as the golden benchmark and not release.

## Learning job

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What consequential situation opens the lesson? | At 2:10 a.m., a Zone 3 pressure event creates an urgent request for an active critical-facility exposure list and the current procedure. |
| What must the learner decide before teaching begins? | What should define “active customer exposed to this event” across CIS, GIS, SCADA, and policy systems? |
| What professional consequence makes this matter? | Blended responsibilities create unstable definitions, wrong mappings, stale policy, excessive access, and confident but indefensible answers. |
| What should the learner be able to do afterward? | Identify the primary job of data models, taxonomies, ontologies, semantic layers, context engines, and AI context, then govern the boundaries. |
| What usable work product will the learner create? | Five-Layer Meaning Map and AI Context Contract |
| What evidence is required for completion? | Eleven visible requirements: opening decision, matching, 25-item classifier, process exploration, ordering, failure laboratory, myth check, context assembly, RACI, saved work product, and applied assessment |

## Instructor explanation plan

| Major component | What the learner sees | What the learner does | What to notice | Utility meaning | Debrief needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening decision and evidence desk | Four sources feeding a governed evidence desk and human authority | Decide what controls shared meaning, then read left to right | No single source owns structure, meaning, and authority | Utility exposure decisions need source records plus shared definitions and controls | The graph supports the decision; it does not make the operational decision |
| Five-job comparison | Questions, artifacts, examples, and missing-layer failures | Explore tabs and match questions to jobs | Similar terms cooperate but are not interchangeable | Teams can assign each responsibility to an owner and mechanism | Vendor product labels do not redefine the jobs |
| Artifact sorter | 25 realistic utility artifacts | Classify and retry item by item | File format does not reveal semantic purpose | A spreadsheet can be taxonomy, mapping, or runtime evidence | Judge primary job in the bounded scenario |
| Exposure process and packet anatomy | Six process states and six packet layers | Step through and order stages | Runtime context is assembled only after meaning and mappings are resolved | AI receives a bounded package with current controls | Human authority remains last |
| Failure laboratory and myth check | Four removable responsibilities and propagated effects | Remove a layer, inspect repair, judge four claims | Validation, inference, retrieval rank, and real-world truth differ | Repair belongs at the first broken boundary | A better prompt cannot replace missing architecture |
| Context assembler and RACI | Required and prohibited packet items plus decision owners | Select context and assign roles | More context is not automatically better; platforms do not own accountability | Relevance, time, permission, conflict, and authority are governed | Accountable and responsible are separate decisions |
| Work product and applied check | Eight-field Meaning Map with live preview and six criteria | Save, evaluate, revise, and retry | Specific mappings, relationships, reviewers, and limits make the artifact usable | Another utility team can inspect the contract before implementation | Passing the local check is not production approval |

## Learner FAQ plan

| Likely learner question | Why it may remain unclear | Direct plain-English answer | Utility example | Diagram, comparison, or worked sequence | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is a semantic layer the same as an ontology? | Both discuss business meaning | No. Ontology defines shared meaning; semantic layer resolves it to governed data | ActiveAccount mapped to CIS status and dates | Five-job comparison | Product terminology varies |
| Is a taxonomy enough? | Hierarchies can look like semantic models | No. Taxonomy classifies; ontology also defines relationships | Account serves premise; event affects zone | Comparison and graph | Scope ontology to reusable meaning |
| Is a prompt the same as AI context? | Context is often passed through prompts | A prompt may carry context, but the package also needs evidence, policy, time, permission, state, conflicts, and limits | Zone 3 packet | Packet anatomy | Runtime implementation varies |
| Does ontology replace source models? | Shared concepts may resemble schemas | No. Source applications retain purpose-built structures | CIS, GIS, and SCADA retain their models | Evidence desk | Keep source ownership explicit |
| Does good context make generation deterministic? | Better evidence can stabilize answers | No. It reduces ambiguity but does not eliminate probabilistic generation | Same evidence can yield varied wording | Myth check | Do not promise perfect repeatability |
| Does a graph guarantee truth? | Explicit relationships can feel authoritative | No. Stale or wrong facts remain possible | Stale premise-zone link | Failure laboratory | Validation and review remain necessary |
| When is RDF unnecessary? | Graphs can become a default architecture | Use a simpler governed table when one bounded source answers the question | Single-source report | Worked FAQ comparison | Choose architecture by relationship value |
