I
Executive review

A vendor proposes an enterprise graph of everything

The proposal promises to ingest every table, document, sensor tag, map feature, permit, email, and work order into one graph before choosing a first operational question. It has no agreed identity crosswalk, no named ontology owner, no source authority model, and no measure of decision improvement.

A Knowledge Spine is not a copy of the enterprise. It combines an ontology, governed identifiers, mappings, validation, provenance, query services, and operational workflows. A pilot should prove one reusable capability, not perform a ceremonial data migration.

Start with one question and the smallest relationship path that answers it.

Define competency questions, identifiers, mappings, shapes, provenance, access patterns, service levels, and test data before selecting platform features.

Fund a decision improvement with accountable owners and stop criteria, not an unbounded graph asset.

Challenge scope before technology

Choose the first executive response. Then read the proposal card for missing decisions. A large graph can still be semantically empty if identity, authority, and purpose remain unresolved.

II
Architecture table

Assemble the minimum viable spine

Use this capstone question: Which critical facilities could lose water service if Pump P-104 is unavailable for four hours? The path needs the pump, pressure zone, premises, service accounts, and critical-facility classifications. It also needs current asset state, customer status, authority, time, and a human decision boundary.

The competency question drives the ontology slice. shared identifiers connect source records. mappings keep data in place where practical. shapes catch missing required structure. provenance makes the answer challengeable.

III
Design review panel

Score value, evidence, governance, feasibility, and safety

A pilot should have a baseline, a target, a learning question, and a stop condition. Do not score only technical completion. Score whether operators answer faster, whether evidence is traceable, whether stewards can correct meaning, and whether unsafe action remains blocked.

Make the panel uncomfortable in useful ways

Read the four measure families, then score the proposed pilot. Low scores are not failure. They show what must be designed before funding or field use.

IV
Ninety-day canvas

Turn the course into a bounded utility experiment

Days 1 to 30 define the question, owners, source authority, ontology slice, and baseline. Days 31 to 60 map sources, build the graph path, validate shapes, and test provenance. Days 61 to 90 connect one consuming workflow, run practitioner and novice tests, measure results, and decide whether to stop, revise, or scale.

Make every box testable

Complete the canvas with named sources, roles, relationships, measures, and non-goals. The final defense checks whether another review panel could understand the decision and challenge the evidence without asking you to explain the missing pieces.

Questions before you sponsor the pilot

Do we need a graph database before starting?

No. Start with the question, meaning, identity, and mappings. Use simple RDF tooling for learning, then choose infrastructure from workload and governance needs.

How large should the first ontology be?

Large enough to answer the competency question and test reuse, but small enough for named domain owners to review. Do not model the enterprise by ambition alone.

What is a good first utility use case?

Choose a recurring, relationship-heavy question with visible pain, accessible sources, accountable owners, and a measurable decision improvement.

Can the lakehouse remain in place?

Yes. Map and query governed data in place where practical. Materialize only the slice justified by latency, cost, outage, or repeated computation.

Who owns the Knowledge Spine?

No single team owns all meaning. Domain owners govern concepts, source owners govern records, stewards coordinate quality, platform teams operate services, and leaders own decision risk.

How do we prevent the pilot from becoming another demo?

Connect it to one real workflow, establish baseline measures, assign operators and stewards, test correction paths, and decide in advance what evidence permits scale.

When should we stop the pilot?

Stop or redesign when the question lacks value, authority cannot be resolved, source access is unsafe, evidence quality is inadequate, or the proposed action boundary cannot be controlled.

Evidence boundary

The P-104 pilot is instructional. Production architecture, cybersecurity, privacy, public communication, safety, procurement, and operational decisions require utility-specific review and approval. A passed capstone is not deployment authority.
