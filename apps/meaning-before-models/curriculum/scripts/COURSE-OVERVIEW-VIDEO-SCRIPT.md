## Opening

### Spoken words

Most utilities do not have a shortage of data. They have a shortage of shared meaning.

The pump exists in SCADA. It exists again in GIS. It exists again in the maintenance system. A work
order refers to it one way. An operator calls it something else. Then an AI initiative arrives and
asks a reasonable question: which customers, assets, procedures, risks, and decisions are connected
to this event?

This course shows how to make those connections explicit and computable.

## Course sequence

### Spoken words

Part One starts small. You will build a subject, predicate, and object triple, resolve identity
across systems, and grow a utility knowledge graph.

Part Two separates structure, classification, shared meaning, enterprise connection, and runtime
context. You will learn the different jobs of data models, taxonomies, ontologies, semantic layers,
and AI context. Then you will build shared vocabulary and ask the graph with SPARQL.

Part Three deals with trust. You will separate explicit statements from inference, use SHACL to
check declared structural contracts, and preserve provenance, authority, effective time, version,
and supersession.

Part Four turns a model into an operating capability. You will map meaning to source data, choose
when to query in place or materialize, connect structured and unstructured evidence, and build a
context contract for a model or agent.

Part Five compares BI, RAG, graph grounding, and controlled agentic applications. You will close by
designing a bounded One Water Knowledge Spine pilot.

## Mastery and boundary

### Spoken words

You will not complete this course by scrolling. Each module asks you to decide, inspect a mechanism,
pass distributed checks, and save a professional work product.

The course does not teach that every utility needs one giant graph. It does not teach that RDF
replaces your existing systems. It does not teach that good context makes generative AI perfectly
repeatable. It teaches you to choose a bounded question, state the meaning, trace the evidence, and
keep human authority visible.
