Taxonomies, Vocabularies, and RDFS

One team uses “asset” for pumps and pipes. Another includes software. Finance includes land. Operations sometimes uses it for anything with a tag. Before a machine can classify utility knowledge, people must decide what their terms include and exclude.

Do not settle “asset” by counting votes

A controlled vocabulary gives approved terms and definitions for a defined use. A controlled vocabulary can say that “lift station” is preferred and “sewage pump station” is an alternative label in a particular organization.

A taxonomy adds broader and narrower categories. Pump may sit under Rotating Equipment, which sits under Physical Asset. The hierarchy supports search, reporting, and consistent classification. It does not by itself describe every relationship a pump can have.

Ask what the term means, which examples belong, and which near-misses do not.

Record preferred label, alternative labels, definition, broader class, owner, status, and version.

Create a decision forum for contested terms. Do not let the loudest application owner define enterprise meaning by default.

Hierarchy repair

A class is not an instance, and a part is not a subclass

A class is a category. An instance is one member of that category. Pump is a class. Pump P-104 is an instance.

A subclass relationship means every member of the narrower class is also a member of the broader class. Every Centrifugal Pump is a Pump. A motor is not a subclass of Pump; it can be a component of a pump assembly. Confusing “is a kind of” with “is part of” creates a broken hierarchy.

RDF Schema (RDFS) supplies terms for rdfs:Class, rdfs:subClassOf, labels, comments, domains, ranges, and property hierarchies.

Domain and range describe expected kinds of things

A property’s domain says what kind of subject normally uses it. Its range says what kind of object or value normally appears on the other end.

For “serves pressure zone,” the domain might be Pumping Asset and the range Pressure Zone. Be careful: in RDFS, domain and range can support classification inferences. They are not simply database validation constraints. Shapes Constraint Language later handles explicit record validation.

Classification FAQ

Questions about vocabularies and RDFS

Can one thing belong to several classes?

Yes. Pump P-104 can be a Centrifugal Pump, Critical Asset, and Electrically Powered Equipment if the classifications are defined and supported.

Who owns a definition used by several departments?

Use a named domain owner and a cross-functional review forum. Local system owners contribute requirements, but shared meaning needs an explicit decision right.

Does RDFS validate records?

Not in the same way as a form or database constraint. RDFS describes classes and properties and can support inference. SHACL is designed for explicit graph-shape validation.

Is “motor part of pump” a taxonomy relationship?

No. A taxonomy normally expresses broader and narrower categories. Part-of is a separate semantic relationship.

How detailed should the taxonomy become?

Detailed enough to support real search, reporting, policy, or operational questions. Stop when added categories have no governed use or owner.
