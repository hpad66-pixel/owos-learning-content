# Module 03 Design Brief: Which Pump Do You Mean?

Status: approved for production under standing owner authorization

## Learning decision

The learner must decide whether five records represent the same physical pump, a record about that
pump, or an unresolved candidate. Matching text is evidence, never proof by itself.

## Experience architecture

This lesson is a forensic identity room. It opens with five source records pinned to an evidence
wall. The learner first resists an unsafe merge, then separates identity from related records,
adjudicates each record in a docket, and runs the blast radius of a deliberately false merge.

The composition is intentionally different from Module 02's syntax workbench and Module 05's
decision room. Paper records, evidence stamps, contradiction marks, and a steward ledger provide
the visual grammar.

## Signature mechanism

The Identity Evidence Docket contains five records. The learner records one of three findings:
same physical asset, related record, or unresolved candidate. Explanatory feedback names the
evidence boundary. The false-merge laboratory then shows why a bad identity assertion changes
maintenance, operational, analytical, and agentic answers.

## Work product

The Identity Crosswalk and Conflict Queue records one approved mapping, one unresolved candidate,
supporting and contradicting evidence, authority, reviewer, next action, and an explicit limit.

## Evidence boundary

All Riverbend pump records are instructional. Match rules, thresholds, authoritative systems, and
approval roles are utility-specific governance decisions. RDF can represent identity assertions and
provenance, but RDF syntax does not decide that two records denote the same real thing.
