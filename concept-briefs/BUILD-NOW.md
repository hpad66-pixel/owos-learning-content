# Concept Briefs ready to build now

This is my list, not a filter of the existing one. The test I applied is narrower than "is this a
good idea": **can we start this week and finish it defensibly.**

Three things have to be true:

1. **A real misconception.** Competent people believe something wrong. If the correction is a
   definition, it belongs in the Dictionary.
2. **Federal authority we already know how to reach.** Either evidence already in this repository, or
   a regulation or live agency source I have concrete reason to believe exists and is retrievable.
   The I&I archive problem is the reason this criterion is now explicit.
3. **A graphic that can carry the idea.** If the only way to teach it is prose, the format adds
   nothing.

Ordered by what I would actually build first.

---

## 1. Nothing Leaves Without a Path

**The idea.** Every system has a boundary, and every drop crossing it goes somewhere you can name.
"It went away" is never an answer, it is an unfinished sentence.

**The misconception.** That water, solids, or a contaminant can leave a process without an
accounting. Staff routinely describe removal as disappearance, which hides recycle streams,
sidestreams, and the fact that removal usually relocates a problem rather than ending it.

**Why now.** This is the prerequisite for most of the rest, and it needs almost no new research. The
mass and water balance framing is already developed in the detention white paper. Cross-cutting, so
it serves water, wastewater, and stormwater at once.

**The graphic.** One boundary box. Everything in, everything out, and a running total that refuses to
balance until every path is named. Interactive: remove a path and watch the balance break.

---

## 2. A Lower Peak Is Not Less Water

**The idea.** Rate and quantity are different measurements, and almost every overstatement in
stormwater comes from reading one as the other.

**The misconception.** That a peak reduction is a volume reduction. This is held by people who should
know better and it appears in reports and public meetings.

**Why now.** Already built and verified. The corrected hydrograph in brief 003 satisfies reservoir
routing and computes 45 percent peak reduction against 1 percent volume reduction. The evidence is
EPA Three Keys, already retrieved and live. This is the fastest genuinely valuable build we have.

**The graphic.** Exists. Two curves, both areas shaded, the crossing marked.

---

## 3. The Sample Is a Choice

**The idea.** Where and when you sample decides the result more often than what happens in the
laboratory. A sampling location is a decision about what you are willing to find out.

**The misconception.** That sampling is observation. It is design. This is also the root of "plant
compliance describes what comes out of the tap," because the compliance sample is not taken where
anyone drinks.

**Why now.** Upstream of every measurement brief we will ever write, and 40 CFR 141 sampling
requirements give hard regulatory anchors. Highest leverage item with no existing coverage anywhere
in the portfolio.

**The graphic.** The same system sampled at four points, producing four defensible and mutually
contradictory stories.

---

## 4. A Non Detect Is Not a Zero

**The idea.** A laboratory result is the output of a method, and the method decides what counts. A
non detect is a statement about a reporting limit, not about absence.

**The misconception.** That the number names the substance. This causes real damage in emerging
contaminant communication, where a reporting limit changes and a utility appears to have a new
problem it did not have yesterday.

**Why now.** Consequential, current, and the public-communication stakes are obvious. Federal
authority is strong: 40 CFR 136 approved methods and detection limit definitions.

**The graphic.** One sample, three methods, three different truthful answers.

---

## 5. Pressure Is the Barrier

**The idea.** In a distribution system the pressure itself is a treatment barrier. Lose it and the
pipe can take in whatever is around it, and the plant will never see it happen.

**The misconception.** That treatment happens at the plant and distribution just delivers. This is a
class of contamination event the plant cannot detect and cannot prevent.

**Why now.** Highest consequence idea in drinking water on this list. Federal anchors exist in the
Total Coliform and Revised Total Coliform Rules and in cross-connection control guidance.

**The graphic.** A pipe under pressure and the same pipe under a transient, with the surrounding
ground as a participant rather than scenery.

---

## 6. Only Wasting Changes What You Own

**The idea.** In activated sludge, returning solids moves inventory around and only wasting changes
how much you have. The lever most operators reach for is not the lever that changes the answer.

**The misconception.** That the return rate controls the solids inventory. Licensed operators hold
this, which makes it exactly the kind of correction a Concept Brief exists for.

**Why now.** Unlocks most of the wastewater family, and the mechanism is stable and well documented
in EPA operator guidance.

**The graphic.** A loop where the return arrow visibly cannot change the total, and the waste arrow
visibly can.

---

## 7. Compliant Is Not Safe

**The idea.** A standard is a negotiated line drawn with the science, the treatment technology, and
the cost of the day. Meeting it is a legal state, not a health guarantee.

**The misconception.** That compliance means the water is safe and non compliance means it is
dangerous. Both halves are wrong, and both damage public trust when an exceedance is explained badly.

**Why now.** Needed, and genuinely hard to write without becoming either alarmist or dismissive. The
federal frame is well documented: maximum contaminant level goals against maximum contaminant levels
in the Safe Drinking Water Act is exactly this distinction, written into law.

**Build it seventh, not first.** It only works once the measurement briefs above have established
what a number is. Placed earlier it reads as cynicism.

---

## 8. Deferred Maintenance Is Borrowing

**The idea.** Choosing not to maintain is not a saving. It is a loan taken at an interest rate nobody
quotes, repaid later as rehabilitation, capacity, or damage.

**The misconception.** That maintenance is a cost line and deferral is a saving. Held by exactly the
people who decide budgets.

**Why now.** No technical prerequisite at all, which means it proves the format works for a board and
finance audience the portfolio currently cannot reach. That audience approves the money for
everything else on this list.

**The graphic.** One asset, two spending paths, and the crossing point where deferral becomes the
more expensive choice.

---

## 9. Rain in the Wrong Pipe

**The idea.** Stormwater that reaches a sanitary sewer is conveyed, pumped, and treated at the
utility's expense, and it consumes capacity sized for something else.

**The misconception.** That it is only rainwater, so it is harmless.

**Why now, with a caveat.** Partly written already in brief 003. **Hold until the citation question
resolves.** The direct against delayed distinction currently rests on an archived EPA regional
document, and 40 CFR 35.2005 definitions are the likely replacement. Do not build this until that is
settled, because the entire brief hangs on it.

**The graphic.** Two systems, one storm, and a cost meter that only runs on one of them.

---

## 10. Coagulation and Flocculation, rebuilt

**The idea, reframed.** These are not two steps in a sequence. They are two different physics
operating on opposite timescales, and treating them as one process is why the fix is so often applied
to the wrong one.

**The misconception.** That flocculation problems are coagulation problems, or that more chemical
fixes a mixing problem.

**Why now.** Owner has directed the rebuild. Substantial evidence exists in the current package and
can be carried forward, but the design, diagrams, and white paper are being rebuilt rather than
patched.

**The graphic.** Two clocks running at different speeds on the same water.

## What I would sequence

**Now:** 1, 2, 3. Each is either already evidenced or has hard regulatory anchors, and 1 and 3 are
prerequisites for most of what follows.

**Next:** 10, then 6 and 4.

**Then:** 5, 8, 7.

**Blocked:** 9, until the I&I citation resolves to live federal authority.

## What I am deliberately not proposing

Anything answerable by a definition, anything that is a tour of a domain, and anything whose evidence
I cannot currently see a live federal path to. The archived-source problem in brief 003 is the reason
that last filter now exists. It is cheaper to not start a brief than to build one and discover at
publication that its central claim rests on a document the agency no longer publishes.
