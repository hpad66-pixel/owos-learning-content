## Executive result

The latest generated HTML was directionally stronger than the original supplied prototype because
it had already removed or withheld most universal numbers, facility-ready settings, equations, and
operating instructions. It was nevertheless not publication ready.

Of the 32 pending material claims sent to Perplexity:

- 14 are broadly aligned with the evidence direction and can be retained after exact citation and
  review;
- 13 require narrower wording, a stronger original source, reclassification, or an explicit
  limitation; and
- 5 should be removed or materially recast unless better evidence is found.

Formal release verification remains 0 percent because independent verification, freshness review,
and qualified technical approval have not been completed.

## What was most off in the HTML

1. **Evidence-state semantics.** The source manifest displayed source records as `verified` while
   the page simultaneously said independent source and qualified technical review were incomplete.
   “Source retrieved” and “claim use verified” must become separate fields and labels.
2. **Citation placement.** Sources appeared in a bottom manifest, but the learner could not always
   see which exact sentence, number, table row, visual, or interaction state a source supported.
3. **Claim-to-source granularity.** One block could contain several claims with different authority
   tiers and limitations while showing only a shared badge.
4. **Research-versus-authority separation.** The page correctly labeled Park and Aktas as
   research-only, but the next version must place the experimental conditions beside every numeric
   result.
5. **Unsupported propositions remained in the governed register.** This is appropriate for audit
   history, but the learner-facing page needs explicit disposition so a rejected or held claim
   cannot be mistaken for hidden support.

## Required next-version citation behavior

Each learner-facing material statement will carry a stable claim marker. Activating it will expose:

- source title and issuing authority;
- authority tier;
- exact page, section, table, figure, or paragraph;
- jurisdiction and applicability;
- verified wording and limitations;
- verification and next-review dates;
- independent verifier and qualified reviewer state; and
- correction history.

The bottom source manifest remains as the full evidence index. It will no longer be the only place a
learner can discover the support for a statement.
