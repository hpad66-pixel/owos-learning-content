## Permanent authority rule

Water-sector OWOS instruction uses United States governing authorities only:

1. current federal statute and regulation;
2. current federal agency guidance tied to that authority;
3. applicable state requirements labeled with the state and effective date;
4. United States professional standards and practice references; and
5. peer-reviewed research presented within its experimental scope.

Non-United States regulations, standards, government guidance, design guides, operator guides, and
health guidelines are excluded. Research conducted outside the United States may remain only as
clearly labeled research and can never act as a governing standard.

## United States federal and state frame

The release version should begin with applicable federal authority:

- 40 CFR 141.173 addresses filtration requirements for covered systems serving at least 10,000
  people.
- 40 CFR 141.551 addresses strengthened combined filter effluent turbidity limits for covered
  smaller systems.
- For covered conventional and direct filtration systems, the applicable provisions establish
  combined filter effluent turbidity of 0.3 NTU or less in at least 95 percent of measurements each
  month and a 1 NTU maximum.
- EPA guidance supplies process interpretation but does not replace the regulatory text.
- State primacy agencies may impose additional or more stringent requirements. Any state statement
  must identify the state and exact authority.
- The Virginia citation may be used only as a Virginia example. Tennessee training may be used only
  as training context, not as a national standard.

## Study-specific evidence boundary

Park et al. and Aktas et al. demonstrate that mixing response depends on experimental conditions and
coagulant. Their reported G values and times must remain tied to the named studies. They do not
create United States federal or state setpoints, prove that an existing plant is wasting energy, or
authorize a process change.
