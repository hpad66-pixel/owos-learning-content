# Codex Technical Evidence Verdict

Brief: `owos:concept-brief:001`  
Review date: 2026-07-26  
Review basis: 36 material candidate claims, the Perplexity research receipts, the current public
HTML, and direct checks against United States federal and state sources and the cited original
research record.  
Review type: evidence-based technical/editorial review; not a licensed engineer, chemist, operator,
regulator, or facility-specific approval.

## Final verdict

The public Concept Brief is technically sound for its intended educational purpose. The core
explanation of coagulation, flocculation, jar testing, mixing, alkalinity, downstream consequences,
and change-control boundaries is defensible.

The 36-row candidate register is intentionally broader than the public page. As written:

- 29 claims are acceptable or acceptable with their stated scope;
- 1 claim needs a narrow wording correction; and
- 6 claims should not be published as factual statements in their current form.

That does **not** make the public page 81 percent accurate. The current public page already withholds
or corrects the high-risk statements. My technical/editorial confidence score for the current public
lesson is **94/100**, provided the sources-and-scope language remains visible and the page is not
represented as facility-specific operating guidance.

This score is an editorial confidence judgment, not a statistical probability and not formal
professional certification.

## Required public-content position

The following six items must remain removed, corrected, or converted to questions:

1. universal numeric zeta-potential targets;
2. unverified Aktas numeric thresholds;
3. the G = 900 s⁻¹ energy/shear diagnosis;
4. streaming current as a direct zeta-potential substitute;
5. storm data as a categorical pre-storm dose predictor; and
6. restabilization as a published mechanism claim without an adequate direct source.

The 0.26 alum factor is not wrong in the narrow teaching context shown by the Tennessee source, but
it is easy to misuse as a total-sludge prediction. It should remain outside the public lesson unless
that distinction has a clear instructional purpose.

## Evidence checks that materially affected the verdict

- Current 40 CFR provisions support the combined-filter-effluent turbidity limits when the covered
  system, treatment technology, and applicable subpart are identified.
- EPA's turbidity guidance treats jar testing, streaming-current monitoring, zeta potential, and
  pilot filters as separate optimization tools and recommends plant-suited written procedures.
- Virginia 12VAC5-590-871 supports the state-specific rapid-mix design-basis and maximum-mixing-point
  statements and describes tapered flocculation to reduce particle shearing.
- Tennessee's Course 307 supports the narrow 0.26 alum teaching factor, polymer role variation,
  alkalinity consumption, plant-representative jar-test conditions, and consistent sampling logic.
- Park et al.'s abstract supports the reported experimental timing sequence, but only within that
  experiment.
