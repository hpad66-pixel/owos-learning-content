COAGULATION
VS FLOCCULATION

Two different jobs. Two different chemistries. Two different mixing energies. Confusing them is the most common reason a clarifier underperforms and a filter runs short.

01
Why This Exists
Chemistry changes the particle before physics can remove it

Raw water carries particles too small and too electrically stable to ever settle. Clay, silt, algae, organic color, bacteria, and precipitated metals all sit in the same trap: they repel each other, so they stay suspended. Gravity alone cannot solve this.

REGULATORY

Under the Surface Water Treatment Rule, conventional plants demonstrate turbidity and log-removal credit. That credit is earned in coagulation, not at the filter. REGULATORY

OPERATIONAL

Every dollar of overdose becomes sludge you dewater and haul. Alum produces roughly 0.26 mg/L sludge per mg/L fed. Underdose becomes short filter runs and lost production. STANDARD

PUBLIC TRUST

Turbidity is the visible proxy for pathogen protection. A clarifier that carries over is the failure the public can see and a consent order will name. JUDGMENT

03
Plain English
Key terms explained

No jargon used to explain jargon. Every term gets a meaning and a plant-floor example.

COLLOID

A particle small enough that it never settles — roughly 0.001 to 1 micron. Not dissolved, not settleable. It lives in the gap between the two. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTClay from a rain event. Raw water reads 40 NTU and the sed basin still reads 38 NTU after four hours without chemistry.

ZETA POTENTIAL

A measure of how strongly particles repel each other. Raw water typically sits near −15 to −30 mV. Coagulation drives that toward zero. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTA streaming current monitor is the practical real-time proxy. It tells you charge status between jar tests.

COAGULANT

An inorganic metal salt — aluminum or iron based — that hydrolyzes in water, releases positively charged species, and cancels the particle's negative charge. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTAlum, ferric chloride, ferric sulfate, PACl (polyaluminum chloride), ACH (aluminum chlorohydrate).

FLOCCULANT / POLYMER

A long-chain organic molecule that bridges already-destabilized particles into larger, stronger floc. It does not replace the coagulant. It amplifies it. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTAnionic polymer as a settling aid ahead of the sed basin; cationic polymer for sludge conditioning ahead of the belt press.

G VALUE

Velocity gradient in reciprocal seconds — the engineering number for mixing intensity. G = √(P / µV). High G disperses chemical; low G grows floc; high G applied late shears floc apart. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTGt (the Camp number) is the flocculation design parameter, typically 2×10⁴ to 10⁵. Large G with short time gives small dense floc; small G with long time gives large light floc.

TAPERED FLOCCULATION

Decreasing G across successive stages. Build small dense floc first, then aggregate gently into large dense floc. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTA commonly cited three-stage taper is 50, 20, 10 s⁻¹. Mechanical flocculators should have variable-speed drives with roughly 4:1 turndown.

ALKALINITY

The water's buffering capacity. Coagulants consume it. Too little and pH crashes, the coagulant stops hydrolyzing correctly, and the dose stops working. STANDARD

AT THE PLANTAlum consumes 0.45–0.5 mg/L alkalinity as CaCO₃ per mg/L fed. ACH consumes roughly 0.18 mg/L, which is why it is chosen where pH control is tight.

JAR TEST

Bench-scale replication of the full-scale train. The only defensible way to set a dose, and the record a regulator will ask for. JUDGMENT

AT THE PLANTRun it on every meaningful raw-water shift — storm event, seasonal turnover, source switch — not on a fixed calendar.
04
System Fit
Where it sits in the treatment train

Not a standalone process. It is the front end that decides how every downstream unit performs.

WASTEWATER

Same physics, different target. Chemically enhanced primary treatment uses ferric or alum to strip TSS and phosphorus ahead of biology. Polymer conditions sludge before dewatering.

STORMWATER

Ballasted and chemically assisted clarification for wet-weather flows and construction turbidity, where detention time is short and flows are flashy.

REUSE / INDUSTRIAL

Pretreatment ahead of membranes. Fouling control is largely a coagulation problem solved upstream of the RO train, not at the membrane.

Where The Evidence Splits
The rapid-mix energy debate

This is the section a dictionary cannot give you. The design convention and the peer-reviewed literature do not agree, and knowing that is worth more than knowing either one alone.

POSITIONWHAT IT SAYSTIER & SOURCE
Design conventionRapid mix at G of 600–1000 s⁻¹ for 30–60 seconds. Flocculation at 20–80 s⁻¹ for 20–60 minutes. This is what plants are built to and what most operator study guides teach.STANDARD
PNWS-AWWA 2024, Carollo
Research challengeStudies argue intense mixing in coagulation is largely unnecessary and consumes disproportionate energy. One study found maximum macrofloc growth at G of 200 s⁻¹ over 150 seconds, with breakup beginning after 180 seconds.CONTESTED
Environ. Sci. Technol. · PubMed 16752769
Coagulant-specificThe breakpoint depends on the coagulant. For PACl flocs, above roughly 390 s⁻¹ breakup dominates over formation; for polysilica iron the threshold is around 546 s⁻¹. A single plant-wide G target ignores this.CONTESTED
Aktas et al., floc growth study
Regulatory postureState code generally does not fix a number. Virginia requires the owner's engineer to submit the basis for the selected velocity gradient, considering chemicals added and water temperature, and requires coagulant application at the point of maximum mixing intensity.REGULATORY
12VAC5-590-871

What to do with this: if your rapid mix runs at 900 s⁻¹ on PACl, you may be spending energy to shear floc you just made. That is a jar test and an energy audit, not a rebuild.

Common Errors In Circulation
Corrections

These five errors appear repeatedly in widely shared industry graphics. Each is corrected here with the reason it matters.

ITEMCOMMONLY POSTEDCORRECT
Alum reactionAl₂(SO₄)₃ + 6H₂O → 2Al(OH)₃Al₂(SO₄)₃·14H₂O + 6HCO₃⁻ → 2Al(OH)₃↓ + 3SO₄²⁻ + 6CO₂ + 14H₂O. The bicarbonate term is what gets consumed. Omitting it hides why alkalinity drops and pH falls. STANDARD
Ferric reactionFeCl₃ + 3H₂O → Fe(OH)₃ + 3HClDirectionally right, but in practice FeCl₃ + 3HCO₃⁻ → Fe(OH)₃↓ + 3Cl⁻ + 3CO₂. Same alkalinity consumption story. STANDARD
"Result: cloudy water"Listed as the outcome of coagulationCoagulation produces pin floc / microfloc — destabilized particles too small to settle yet. Not a failure state. It is the intended intermediate. STANDARD
PAC listed twiceOnce as pre-hydrolyzed Al, once as pH/hardness adjustmentPACl = polyaluminum chloride, a pre-hydrolyzed coagulant. PAC = powdered activated carbon, an adsorbent for taste, odor, and organics. Different chemicals entirely. Lime handles pH and hardness. STANDARD
Polymer chargeCationic / anionic listed under both columnsCationic: sludge conditioning, dewatering, some primary coagulation. Anionic: settling aid and filter aid on already-coagulated water. Non-ionic: bridging where charge is already handled. PRACTICE

08
Operating Judgment
Do this, not that
NOT THIS

Add polymer at the rapid mix along with the coagulant, because it's one dosing point and it's easier.

→ The polymer chain is sheared into fragments before it can bridge anything. You pay for polymer and get nothing.
DO THIS

Coagulant at rapid mix. Polymer at the first or second flocculation stage, where G is low enough for the chain to survive and work.

→ Larger, denser floc, better settling, lower solids loading on the filters.
NOT THIS

Hold a fixed coagulant dose year-round because that's what has always been run.

→ Overdose in clear-water season builds sludge cost and can restabilize particles. Underdose during a storm pushes turbidity through the filters.
DO THIS

Jar test on every meaningful raw-water shift. Trend dose against turbidity, TOC, temperature, pH, and alkalinity.

→ A defensible dose record, lower chemical spend, and documentation a regulator will accept.
NOT THIS

Chase a turbidity problem by raising the coagulant dose alone.

→ In low-alkalinity water, pH falls out of the coagulant's effective range and more chemical makes it worse, not better.
DO THIS

Check pH and alkalinity first. Confirm you are inside the coagulant's operating window. Consider supplementing alkalinity with lime or soda ash.

→ Iron salts operate over a wider pH range than alum and form a heavier floc, at the cost of being corrosive and staining.

Role-Based Takeaways
What this means for you

UTILITY DIRECTOR

Chemical spend and sludge hauling are two lines on the same budget, and both are set by coagulation practice.

→ MONDAYAsk for 12 months of dose vs. raw turbidity vs. sludge tonnage on one chart.

PLANT OPERATOR

You are the sensor the plant depends on. Floc appearance and clarity between floc tell you more than any single meter.

→ MONDAYRun a five-jar dose series on today's raw water and log it.

COMPLIANCE / PE

Log-removal credit lives here. Your record of dose rationale is the evidence in an inspection.

→ MONDAYConfirm jar test records are retained and traceable to dose changes.

IT / SCADA

Dose, raw turbidity, settled turbidity, pH, alkalinity, and streaming current should be one queryable dataset, not six screens.

→ MONDAYVerify all six tags are historized at the same interval.

GIS / SOURCE WATER

Raw water quality is a watershed output. Storm response and land use predict your dose before the storm arrives.

→ MONDAYOverlay rainfall gauges with historical raw turbidity spikes.

FINANCE / RATES

Chemical optimization is one of the few operating savings that needs no capital and is defensible in a rate case.

→ MONDAYModel a 10% dose reduction against chemical plus residuals cost.
