# Coagulation vs Flocculation Design Brief

## Intake and evidence

- Source types: existing HTML prototype and product-direction conversation.
- Source visibility: internal working material.
- Evidence cutoff: 2026-07-25.
- Known limitations: the supplied HTML has now been fully inventoried and primary sources have been
  traced, but independent verification, qualified technical review, regulatory effective-date
  review, and editorial approval remain incomplete.
- Claims requiring qualified review: all operating ranges, chemical reactions, alkalinity
  consumption values, sludge production values, charge and particle-size examples, turbidity
  examples, design conventions, regulatory statements, and chemical comparisons.

## Learner job

- Primary question: Are coagulation and flocculation the same process, and how can I tell which part
  is failing?
- Misconception or risk: treating visible pin floc as a failed result, treating dose and mixing
  controls as universal targets, or changing chemistry before checking the process sequence.
- Intended audience: operators, designers, engineers, consultants, utility leaders, regulators, and
  relevant suppliers.
- Decision or action affected: what to observe, what to test, and when to seek site-specific
  engineering or operational review.
- What this brief will not attempt: site-specific dosing, design, regulatory advice, or a substitute
  for an approved facility procedure.

## Design fingerprint

- Opening pattern: show the mechanism before giving formal definitions.
- Narrative archetype: controlled process failure lab.
- Central mental model: change the condition, create contact, then separate.
- Signature mechanism: a four-stage qualitative vessel that can show an insufficient chemical
  condition, possible restabilization, limited contact, or illustrative floc breakage without
  numerical controls.
- Dominant visual: animated particle jar.
- Interaction signature: change a condition, observe a consequence, then diagnose the failure.
- Role treatment: connect the same mechanism to different role decisions.
- Closing action: record one facility-specific question before changing dose or mixing.
- Surface rhythm: Graphite and charcoal surfaces carry the complete experience. Water blue identifies
  the central concept, navigation, and active states. Amber is reserved for caution and consequence,
  while green is reserved for favorable or stable process meaning.
- Patterns deliberately avoided: a fixed nine-section page, decorative cards, a generic quiz block,
  and unlabeled numerical thresholds.
- Differences from adjacent briefs: future sedimentation and filtration briefs need different
  mechanisms, visual grammar, and learner actions.

## Teaching sequence

The brief begins with the process mechanism because the learner must see that coagulation and
flocculation perform different jobs. It then establishes the United States federal treatment and
combined-filter-effluent frame before introducing technical terms. A process-fit section shows how an upstream condition can
reach clarification and filtration. A correction clinic preserves rejected prototype assertions
and separates study-specific evidence from plant setpoints. The brief closes with a diagnostic,
role decisions, and the boundary between a public learning brief and a private facility SOP.

## Visual and interaction selection

| Teaching idea | Natural shape | Selected visual or interaction | Learner conclusion | Mobile treatment | Reduced-motion treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coagulation condition and particle contact | Cause and effect over time | Four-stage qualitative vessel | Coagulation changes particle stability or creates precipitates, while flocculation changes contact and aggregate growth | Stage controls form a two-by-two grid above a full-width canvas | A static canvas frame and the same text carry the complete state |
| Where failures travel | Process and dependency | Treatment-train consequence map | A clarification error becomes a solids-loading and filter problem downstream | The overview scales to the content width and stacked sector cards preserve readable meaning | Dashed paths remain visible without animated flow |
| Corrections and contested claims | Evidence comparison | Claim correction table | A correction needs the original statement, replacement, source, and review state | Table becomes labeled records | No motion required |

The supplied v4.2 page is the experience reference, not merely a palette reference. The working
implementation preserves its full-width moving-particle canvas, four-stage tabs, stability-to-contact
graphic, horizontal treatment-train graphic, gold pull-quote pacing, numbered blue bands, and
scroll-reveal rhythm. Numeric dose and mixing sliders are replaced by qualitative states because the
prototype values are not universal operating thresholds.

## Graph and Community

- Dictionary terms: coagulation, flocculation, zeta potential, velocity gradient, alkalinity, pin
  floc, jar testing, sedimentation, and filtration.
- Graph questions: why are filter runs short, what should be checked before increasing coagulant
  dose, and what evidence supports a mixing target.
- Course modules: exact modules remain unresolved.
- Competencies: clarification diagnosis and evidence-based process review are proposed, not
  published.
- Adjacent concepts: sedimentation, filtration, residuals, chemical optimization, and jar testing.
- Community purpose: facility-specific questions, field observations, corrections, and source
  suggestions.
- Correction path: discussion becomes a tracked proposal, then source review, technical review,
  revision, and owner approval.

## Commercial boundary

- Relevant organization categories: coagulant suppliers, instrumentation providers, engineering
  firms, jar-test equipment providers, and residuals specialists.
- Contributor opportunities: cases, original sources, practitioner review, and clearly labeled
  technical explanation.
- Sponsorship state: disabled for the pilot.
- SOP product boundary: public templates may explain the required fields and governance workflow;
  facility-ready procedures remain private tenant artifacts requiring named facility approval.
- Conflicts: no organizations or reviewers are assigned.
- Editorial firewall: sponsors receive no claim, tier, source, correction, or approval rights.
