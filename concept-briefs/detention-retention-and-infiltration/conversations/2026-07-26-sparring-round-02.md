## Terminology correction

The intended stormwater term is wet detention. The prior phrase “direct wet detention” was a
dictation error.

## Central story

The owner accepted one storm traced through several stormwater practices as the likely central
story and asked what else should be added.

The recommended addition is a before, during, and after-storm timeline plus two visible pathways:

1. the intended stormwater route through storage, infiltration, outlets, outfalls, and receiving
   water; and
2. the unintended route into a separate sanitary sewer through infiltration and inflow.

## Wastewater connection

The paper must not compare stormwater detention and retention with wastewater equalization, holding,
clarification, or biological process tanks.

The correct wastewater connection is infiltration and inflow, commonly called I&I. The paper will
show how rainfall or groundwater can enter a separate sanitary sewer through defective pipes,
leaking joints, openings, or direct connections. It will explain the distinction between
infiltration and inflow very clearly.

The paper must also distinguish this condition from a combined sewer system. The owner's core point
is that, in a separate sanitary sewer, stormwater or groundwater entering through I&I may be
conveyed, pumped, and treated even though it was not intended to enter the wastewater system.

## Capacity and financial consequence

The paper will connect I&I to occupied hydraulic capacity, wet-weather wastewater flow, pumping and
treatment energy, operating expense, capital expense, rehabilitation or expansion decisions, and
compounded lifecycle cost.

Financial professionals are not being taught to design stormwater assets. They are being taught the
process and terminology so they can understand the causes, consequences, risks, budgets, and
investment decisions.

## Geographic example

Florida may be used as a familiar visual setting for man-made lakes, wet ponds, rainfall, and the
stormwater-to-wastewater connection, while the public Concept Brief remains national and avoids
jurisdiction-specific requirements.
