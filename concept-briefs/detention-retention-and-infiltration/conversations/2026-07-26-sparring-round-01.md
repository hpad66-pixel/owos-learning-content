## Citation and editorial standard

The owner clarified that the governing standard is APAS or OWS, not the APA academic citation
standard. Canonical use of APAS and OWOS naming will be confirmed in the next round.

## Audience

The paper is for a broad, cross-sector utility audience. It includes operators, engineers,
inspectors, leaders, elected officials, financial professionals, and anyone who needs to understand
and discuss the terms correctly.

## Teaching purpose

The paper must explain detention, retention, and infiltration in the world of stormwater and show
how similar terminology may carry different meanings in wastewater or another utility process.

The learner must see where these concepts appear, what the assets look like, how the water moves,
and why the distinctions matter.

## Required examples and explanations

The owner requested plain-language explanations and visuals for:

- wet and dry detention;
- permanent pools;
- man-made lakes in Florida;
- bioretention cells;
- infiltration trenches;
- permeable pavement;
- outfalls;
- flood management;
- pollutant and contamination load reduction;
- receiving-water effects;
- maintenance consequences;
- rainfall, hydrographs, storage routing, modeling, and water balance; and
- the connections to future Concept Briefs.

## Intended learner capability

Learners should be able to interpret the concepts, recognize common configurations, ask design and
maintenance questions, and participate in informed conversations.

They are not being trained to perform design, sizing, operations, compliance, or
jurisdiction-specific determinations.

## Visual expectation

The owner wants real diagrams, pictures, comparisons, flowcharts, and an understandable animation
concept that traces one storm through several practices and produces both aha moments and wow
moments.

Every technical term should be taught in the narrative and later supported by a tooltip.
